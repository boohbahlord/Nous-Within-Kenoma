1 How to Start

  • 1.A Every player starts with one Ace from their own deck already on the field, on any tile of their spawn lines they choose. Every Ace spawns with one Jack from that player's deck inside it, wherever it is placed. Which Ace or Jack doesn't matter.
  • 1.B First when starting, draw 2d4 cards for both sides.
  • 1.C Second, both players roll dice; whoever goes first by highest roll will set up their cards on the field first, then the second player places their cards on the field.
  • 1.D Third, the one with the highest roll takes the first game-starting turn, with the prep phase as the first part of this turn. The player will be able to move cards, place cards, or even sacrifice cards, and during this prep phase, the starting player's attack will not happen yet.
  • 1.E The other player can now be attacked; this is the attack phase, and the attacker cannot move their cards, place their cards, or sacrifice during this phase. After, this is the end of the first player's turn.
  • 1.F The second player at the start of their turn has an optional draw card phase: roll a d4 as many times as wanted, and the dice result tells how many cards to draw. When doing this optional phase, you cannot place cards, move cards, sacrifice cards, or attack.
  • 1.G What follows is the prep phase; the player can move cards, place cards, or sacrifice cards, but cannot attack during this phase.
  • 1.H Last is the final phase of the turn, and no actions from the prep phase can be taken during it: the attack phase. Each card can only attack once.

The players should use this pattern each turn:

Prep Phase (optional drawing first) → Attack Phase

  • 1.I As many times as desired, roll four-sided dice to optionally draw cards.
  • 1.J Move cards. Place cards. Sacrifice cards.
  • 1.K Attack enemy cards on the field; each card can only attack once.

2 Major Rules

There are two ways to beat a player, and the last side standing wins:

  • 2.A Domination: every card the player has on the field is destroyed (in Headquarters, every base). Zero cards on the field and they are out.
  • 2.B Economic victory: the player sinks below the lowest mana allowed, −8 (−12 under the Oath of Jupiter). Mana can go below zero, through debts, tolls and an empty deck, down to that floor; one more mana below it (−9) and the Bank takes everything, and they are out.

Running out of cards in the deck, the hand and the soul pile doesn't end a player's game by itself: the deck is not a player's life. An empty deck only means there is nothing left to draw (your own draw roll takes just what is left, never mana).

Certain rules can be changed as desired, such as the cards present in the Ethereal Realm, or the number of cards in the players' hands.

Beside the field lie the Graveyard and the Ethereal Realm.

  • 3.A.1 The Ethereal Realm is a realm of form that can be a source of summoning, conjuring, or materialization. Spend or offer mana to summon souls into the material realm.
  • 3.A.2 The grave is where dead souls sleep in their corpse, waiting to be set free. This is where sacrificed souls go after being spent of their mana, or where dead cards lie trapped and bound to their flesh. The grave is shared.

5 Card Roles and Stacking

5.A 2–10: Soldiers

  • 5.A.1 2–6 are Grunt soldiers; 7–10 are Infantry soldiers.
  • 5.A.2 Grunts stack to four (24 ÷ 6) and Infantry to three (24 ÷ 8), or any mix whose dice add up to 24 or less.

5.B Jacks

  • 5.B.1 A Jack can only stack with two Grunts, one Infantry, or one other Jack (12 + 12 = 24).
  • 5.B.2 Jacks can move two spaces, or use both for a diagonal.
  • 5.B.3 Jacks cannot defend.

5.C Queens

  • 5.C.1 A Queen has no Dice Max, so she adds nothing to the stacking sum: she can join four Grunts, three Infantry, or a King with the one soldier he can hold.
  • 5.C.2 Anyone stacked with the Queen gets modifiers for both offense and defense.
  • 5.C.3 The Queen can use her modifier for both offense and defense.
  • 5.C.4 The special abilities of Queens do not stack with other Queens, and they do not work in base or spawn mode.

5.D Kings

  • 5.D.1 Cards of the King's suit can be placed inside a King when defending.
  • 5.D.2 A King has a DM of 20 and an MSV of 28, so he holds one Grunt or one Infantry at most (20 + 8 = 28).
  • 5.D.3 A Joker can stack with the King, with his one soldier or without.
  • 5.D.4 Cannot attack when defending while alone.
  • 5.D.5 Cannot be sacrificed on the field when stacked in offensive stance.
  • 5.D.6 A Queen can stack with a King, granting the Queen's bonuses. A Joker can also be stacked here, but only with a King present.
  • 5.D.7 When successfully defeated in defensive placement mode, all units die.
  • 5.D.8 Jokers can only spawn on a King of the same color.

5.E Joker (Necromancer)

  • 5.E.1 Any enemy killed next to the Joker goes to its sacrifice pile (Soul Capture): an upside-down stack inside the Joker, its personal sacrifice pile.
  • 5.E.2 Has no Dice Max, so it can stack with four Grunts, three Infantry, one King, a King and Queen, or a King with his one soldier.
  • 5.E.3 When the Joker sacrifices himself, the player gains access to its sacrifice pile.
  • 5.E.4 The Joker's personal sacrifice pile holds up to six, though players can change this for balance.
  • 5.E.5 A captured soul stays inside the Joker until the Joker dies, when the Joker and its souls go to the grave, or until it uses Sacrificial Suicide.
  • 5.E.6 If the Joker is defeated by enemies, its cards go to the grave pile.
  • 5.E.7 If a Joker kills itself during a Blood Moon, it sends itself and every card it held to the Outer Darkness.
  • 5.E.8 Can only spawn on a card of the same color (red or black).

5.F Ace

  • 5.F.1 Each player opens the game with one Ace in their spawn lines, holding one Jack, with a Miner already in it (that first Ace only; an Ace holds as many Miners and Repairmen as you like).
  • 5.F.2 Cannot move or attack, and is always in defense.
  • 5.F.3 Can only be placed in open tiles in the backlines.
  • 5.F.4 Anything inside an Ace cannot use modifiers for defense.
  • 5.F.5 An Ace holds one stack inside it: up to six cards that could stand together on the field (Scouts, Healers, Repairmen and Miners ride along). Every base works the same way.
  • 5.F.6 If an Ace is destroyed, all cards inside are destroyed.
  • 5.F.7 Cannot be sacrificed while on the field.
  • 5.F.8 Each Ace on the field grants +1d6 for pulling cards from the grave.

5.G Optional Ace rule

Card placement inside Aces can be locked to only the Ace's color (red, black) or suit (diamonds, spades, hearts, clubs).

Each card type has an MSV that limits its stacking based on its DM. Calculate how many cards can stack by dividing MSV by DM. For Grunts:

MSV ÷ DM = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 (allowing four Grunts)

Mixed stacks: different cards can stack together if their combined value doesn't exceed the highest MSV. The card in a stack with the highest MSV sets the MSV for the formula.

Stack Limit (SL): the most cards of a type that can be stacked, found by dividing the MSV by the DM for that type, or for the combined value of a mixed stack.

Σ DM ≤ MSV

where Σ DM is the sum of the dice max values of every card in the stack, and MSV is the Maximum Stack Value of the highest card in the mixed stack.

7 Hand Rules

  • 7.A Only six cards are allowed in a player's hand.
  • 7.B Cards drawn over the hand limit go to the grave pile. Once a draw roll overdraws, no more draw rolls that turn.
  • 7.C Overdrawing: every card that is drawn and goes to the grave because the hand is full takes one die off that round's Grave Blessing maximum, so a maximum of six dice drops to five. The Grave Blessing shows the count as −{n}d6 max; past six it turns bold and red (7, 8, and so on) and the rest carries over: overdraw 7 cards and you have no Grave Blessing this round and −1d6 next round; past 12 it carries into the round after that, and so on. It only happens when there were cards in your deck to lose.
  • 7.D Cards can only be placed in their respective card spawn, such as Aces or Kings.
  • 7.E Cards in hand, or certain cards on the field, can be sacrificed during the sacrificial part of the prep phase.

8 Sacrificial Rules

Soul currency: each player has a pile of face-down sacrificed cards used as currency for mana, or soul points. This pile of publicly revealed cards owned by the player can be spent during the sacrificial part of the prep phase. The pile holds six mana at most.

9 Spells

9.A Necromancy

  • 9.A.1 Grave Blessing (free): roll 1d6; each natural 6 raises one card from the grave. Add +1d6 per Ace on the field, or more with Ritual Dice; 6 dice at most per round.
  • 9.A.2 Ritual Dice (1 mana): roll an additional 2d6 for your Grave Blessing.
  • 9.A.3 Sacrificial Suicide: by default (the Rules option, on), the souls the Joker captured fall into the Outer Darkness, their worth comes to the player as mana, and the Joker goes back into the player's deck. With the option off, the Joker sacrifices its soul to the grave and all the souls it captured move into the player's soul pile for spending; a Joker can hold six cards and six mana of souls in all, and any surplus goes to the grave.
  • 9.A.4 Soul Capture: the Joker captures the souls of enemies that die or are killed next to it, up to six mana of souls. They are not available to its player until Sacrificial Suicide. If the enemy kills the Joker, its souls are released into the graveyard, unless an enemy Joker is there to capture them.
  • 9.A.5 Resurrection (1d2 + 1 mana): the grave is shuffled, then choose one card from it and bring it back into your hand.
  • 9.A.6 Flesh Severance: because body and soul are two in this game, the Joker can spend mana from the soul pile to disembody the soul of a unit killed next to it, exiling its form to the Ethereal Realm. This is the player's choice, and it costs half the price to summon the units severed that turn, rounded up: severing a 2-mana and a 1-mana unit costs 2.

9.B Enchantments

  • 9.B.1 Hypostasis (3 mana for one card, +1 for every more card in the stack): grants a stack temporary invulnerability for two rounds. During it the stack can't move forward toward the enemy's side, only to the side or backward if the stack's movement allows, and it can't attack. Afterward the effect disappears and the stack returns to its normal rules. It can't be cast if you have only one stack or card on the board.
  • 9.B.2 Divine Odds (1d2 + 1 mana): advantage on all modifiers of one of your stacks for one combat. Mark the cards with a coin or chess piece. Advantage means rolling the dice twice and taking the higher total.

9.C Curses

  • 9.C.1 Forced Hand (1d2 mana): the other player draws 1d2 cards; any over their hand limit go straight to the grave.
  • 9.C.2 Hexed Odds (1d2 + 1 mana): disadvantage on all modifiers of one enemy stack for one combat. Disadvantage means rolling twice and taking the lower total.
  • 9.C.3 Three of Swords (1d3 mana): roll 1d2. A 1 destroys one random card from the enemy's hand and one from your own; a 2 destroys two from each. If you have nothing in your hand, you lose that many cards from your deck instead; the enemy loses nothing from the deck if their hand is empty.
  • 9.C.4 Double Edged Sword: roll 3d6. A 1 means the caster loses a card from their deck; a 6 means the target loses a card from their deck. Only works against the deck.

9.D Sorcery

  • 9.D.1 Clairvoyance (2 + 1d2 mana): pull 2d6 cards from your deck and look at them; choose one to put in your hand or place on the board, then reshuffle. The number pulled is limited by the size of your deck.
  • 9.D.2 Naked Hand (1d2 mana): reveals the other player's hand, and tells both players the cards have been seen. It costs 6 at the New Moon, nothing at the Full Moon, and 1 under a Blood Moon.
  • 9.D.3 Teleport (1d2 − 1 mana, rolled each round, so half the time free): a card leaves the field now and comes back into your hand at the start of your next turn. You must have room in your hand, no enemy next to it, and it must be in your own lines.

Rolled prices: a price written with dice is rolled behind the scenes at the start of each round, once for everyone, and that price holds for the whole round; the spell list shows it and the log lists the new prices. The moon's prices replace the roll only while their phase lasts: Clairvoyance costs 6 at the New Moon and 2 at the Full Moon; Naked Hand costs 6 at the New Moon, nothing at the Full Moon and 1 under a Blood Moon.

10 The Moon

Every round sits under one phase of the moon, shown at the top of the table. A round is one turn for each player, every turn with all its phases (draw, prep, attack); when every player has finished their turn, the moon moves to its next phase, in this order, and then it repeats:

New Moon → Waxing Crescent → First Quarter → Waxing Gibbous → Full Moon → Waning Gibbous → Last (Third) Quarter → Waning Crescent

10.A New Moon

  • 10.A.1 Players get to choose 1d2 cards from the grave for free.
  • 10.A.2 Clairvoyance and Naked Hand cost six mana, and their spell options turn dark grey.

10.B Full Moon

  • 10.B.1 Each player is asked for a random stack of two cards; with none, a random card from the field; with nothing on the field to take, three cards from their shuffled deck. Given, they go to the soul pile. Refused, it costs 2d2 + 2 mana, which can take you below zero.
  • 10.B.2 All decks are shuffled.
  • 10.B.3 No one can draw cards or cast necromancy spells.
  • 10.B.4 Naked Hand is free and Clairvoyance costs only two mana; both turn a bright grey, the color of the Full Moon.

10.C Blood Moon

  • 10.C.1 Every Full Moon may turn into a Blood Moon. The moon turns red, and so does every necromancy spell, and the table turns the crimson of the Disturbed Satan chapter and starts to glitch.
  • 10.C.2 New Moon effects take place: players pick cards from the grave. The Full Moon's drawbacks (the toll, the shuffle, no drawing, no necromancy) are void, but its cheaper prices stay.
  • 10.C.3 Every card killed during the Blood Moon is sent to the Outer Darkness and can never be returned. A Joker that kills itself during it sends all the cards it held to the Outer Darkness.

10.D The Outer Darkness

Cards in the Outer Darkness can be viewed at any time, in a box the red of the Disturbed Satan chapter. Each card carries the symbol of the player it belonged to at the time of its death.

11 How the digital table reads the rules

Some rules leave room for the players to decide. Play Game uses these readings:

  • 11.A In Standard, two to four players share one device; the screen goes black between turns until the next player confirms, and the board turns so the player at the device always sits at the bottom. With one person at the device, that person always sits at the bottom. Hands show face down to the other side unless Naked Hand reveals them.
  • 11.B The field is five tiles wide by default, or six or seven. It is five lines tall with the midline (the default) and four without it. Survival always keeps the midline. Three and four players use a 7 by 7 field (corners cut by default) where each player's back line is their whole edge; two against two on the same side uses 6 by 6, two lines and three columns for each player.
  • 11.C When play is together, the computers take their phase first and you play yours after; the shared phase ends when you press Ready. Spells that pick one player (Forced Hand, Double Edged Sword, Three of Swords, Naked Hand) ask which opponent when there is more than one, and Naked Hand shows that one hand only, to the caster and the caster's team.
  • 11.D Cut corners remove the four corner tiles from play. Nothing can stand on a removed tile or the river, and a Jack cannot leap over them; the only way across the River map is its two-tile bridge.
  • 11.E The opening roll is a d6 each, rerolled on a tie. Each player's Ace and Jack are taken out of the deck before the opening 2d4 hand is drawn, and the Ace is placed during setup, before any other opening cards. The first turn of the game has no draw phase.
  • 11.F Scores use the Dice Max of each card: 2–6 roll d6, 7–10 roll d8, Jacks d12, Kings and Aces d20. Natural values are the card's number, Jack 10, King 11, Ace 11, Queen 0.
  • 11.G Stacking follows the MSV and DM formula, with one Queen and one Joker per stack at most. Every base (Ace, Tree, Wall, Lighthouse) holds one stack inside it: up to six cards that could stand together on the field, with Scouts, Healers, Repairmen and Miners riding along. Where an older line of the rules gave a different number (soldiers stacking to three, three soldiers with a King, a d12 for the King), the MSV, DM and SL formula decides.
  • 11.H A stack's score is its best card. Dice add on attack. On defense, cards use their natural value only, unless a Queen is in the stack; an Ace stack defends with the Ace's 11 + d20. Jacks don't count on defense.
  • 11.I If the attacker's total is higher, the defender takes one damage; otherwise the attacker takes one. Each damage removes the stack's lowest card, or a heart from a card that has hearts. Every Ace has two hearts: the first hit takes a heart and the cards inside are safe; when the last heart goes, the Ace falls with everything inside it. A Repairman can give an Ace a third heart, once. King and Ace rolls of 20 and 1 change the damage as written above. The dice box shows the attack total on the left and the defense total on the right, like 12>1.
  • 11.J The landing roll: in every fight both sides also roll 1d20. The attacker's 1 is a Crit Miss: the attack misses. The attacker's 20, with two or more cards in the stack that can each attack, is a Crit Hit: the defender takes the blow whatever the scores, unless it rolls a 20 too. A defender's 1 costs it nothing. Three or more Puppets in a stack crit on 19 or 20; Puppets never miss and shrug off Crit Hits. Assassins shrug off Crit Hits and dodge on 17 to 20 of their own roll (a Miss). Every player sees the Crit Hit, Crit Miss or Miss on screen.
  • 11.K Dice Plus (Enchantment, 1d3 mana): on one of your stacks, a sword (its next attack) or a shield (its next defense) adds a d12 on this table: 1: +1 · 2: +1d2 · 3: +1d3 · 4: +1d4 · 5–6: +1d6 · 7–9: +1d8 · 10: +1d10 · 11: +1d12 · 12: two more d12 on the table, and every 12 rolls again. Once on a stack; as many stacks as you like. The stack glows orange (sword) or purple (shield).
  • 11.L Spells on a stack: their names show in faint blackletter at the bottom of the stack, and how long they last (and their odds) at its top right.
  • 11.M Cards are placed from the hand into your own spawn lines: on an empty tile, or onto your own stack if the stack stays legal. Your spawn lines are your backline and frontline; on a field 5 wide and 5 tall or smaller they are your backline alone. Teammates on the same side may spawn on each other's lines. Aces go only on empty backline tiles; Jokers spawn onto your stack holding a card of the same color.
  • 11.N Each stack moves once per round, one tile straight. A group made only of Jacks moves two tiles straight or one diagonal. You can split a stack by choosing which cards move.
  • 11.O Stacks attack an enemy stack next to them (not diagonal, except Jack-only stacks). Aces and a King standing alone don't attack.
  • 11.P Paying mana spends your smallest souls first; any mana overpaid is lost, and spent souls go to the grave. Sacrifices happen before any mana is spent, and the Grave Blessing must come before spending too (Ritual Dice excepted).
  • 11.Q A Joker's captured souls hold six mana at most. Double Edged Sword has no listed cost, so it costs 1 mana here.
  • 11.R The Ether is shared. It holds every special the players do not start with, the unused half of the deck in a one-deck Survival game, and the whole third deck in a three-deck game. A conjure takes a random soul of that kind from it, and Flesh Severance sends exiled souls back into it. A conjure whose kind is not in the Ether cannot be cast. Starting mana takes soldier souls from it; when it has none, new soldier souls are made.
  • 11.S Overdrawing counts every card burned by a full hand, including draws forced by Forced Hand, but not the opening hand. At the end of each round six of the count are used up and the rest carries on.
  • 11.T Resurrection brings the card into your hand. Three of Swords destroys cards into the grave (or the Outer Darkness under a Blood Moon). Naked Hand lasts until the caster's turn ends.
  • 11.U Hypostasis lasts two rounds and ends at the start of your second turn after casting. A stack under it can't be targeted by attacks or by the Full Moon's toll.
  • 11.V The moon moves one phase each round, at the start of the first player's turn. Its New Moon gift and Full Moon toll reach both players at the start of the round, the first player first; the moon's prices, draw ban and Blood Moon last the whole round, and the spell prices apply to whoever casts that turn.
  • 11.W The Full Moon never takes a card inside an Ace or under Hypostasis. What is given goes to the soul pile up to your maximum; the rest goes to the grave.
  • 11.X Not yet in the digital table: the Queen's protection from being surrounded, and placing same-suit cards inside a defending King. A King can't be sacrificed from a stack that holds other cards.

12.A Game types

12.A.1 Standard

Local play: against computers, together on this device, or both. Two to four players on one device (up to eight in King of the Hill); any seat can be a computer. Each picks an Emanation (see Emanations below), a color, and a platonic solid as a symbol. The symbol marks the player's cards everywhere in the player's color, and the player's deck bar is drawn in that color, one block for every card in the deck, with a brighter overlay at its left end when the deck grows past its starting size. The mana bar is a deep indigo, one block for each mana.

12.A.2 Computer seats

Each computer also plays one of seven styles, Random by default: Aggressive (offensive and reckless), Defensive (careful, keeps its cards alive, tall), Encirclement (wide, in small clusters that surround), Stack Tall (tall stacks that keep together as one army), Destroy Bases (goes for the Aces), Economy (Miners, land and mana first) and Spellcaster (spells and Oaths first).

One to three coded opponents, not a language model, at three difficulties each, 1v1, free-for-all, or two against two with a computer as your teammate. Each round it chooses between offense and defense from the field and the decks: defense bunkers (Kings with Queens, Aces held back, Hypostasis over a threatened stack); offense surrounds enemy stacks and casts curses. Easy plays an easier version of this, Medium is balanced, and Hard goes all out on your field and your deck, plays the Joker for souls, stacks Aces for the Grave Blessing and keeps its own deck high.

12.A.3 King of the Hill

A layout for Standard, for 2 to 8 players, on its own 25 by 25 night field: forests, mountains (tiles that aren't there) winding around the middle in a ring with 2 by 2 gaps, scattered peaks, and in the middle a 5 by 5 Hill, white and glowing, never in the fog. Spawns are 2 deep and 3 wide on the edges and in the corners, with potions along them; by default players spawn far enough from their enemies but not as far as possible, or, with Next to enemies, closer to enemies than to teammates. Team mode (on by default) makes two sides, Side A and Side B, that need not be even; a Hill tile shows its side's color, outlined in the holder's own color. At the end of every round (after the attack phase) each side holding at least one Hill tile scores 1; first to 30 or 50 points wins, or 100 in a Marathon. Double points doubles every point; Contest means nobody scores while more than one side holds the Hill. Tug of war (team mode): one shared bar from −25 to +25 that the holding side pulls its way, and that stops while both hold the Hill; pull it to the end to win. In a Marathon, holding the Hill can raise mercenaries: on each round, one chance in four, a band of soldiers under a crown in your side's color, which stays yours; everyone is told. The Carriage (this mode only; standard, 13 in its corner, Carriage Conjure for 1 mana, as many as you like, not counted against the Ether): two hearts, no attack or defense; it carries any stack but one with an Archer up to 3 tiles in any direction; blows hit it first, and of the ranged cards only Archers can shoot at it; when it breaks, every card it carried rolls 1d4 and dies on a 1. The Ether starts with three decks' worth of every kind, gains one more every 10 rounds, and never holds more than four decks' worth of a kind. The Bank stands beside the Hill: nothing can enter its building, the Banker beside it can't be attacked, and Debt Collectors on Carriages guard it; here the Bank's Collectors ride Carriages and cover 3 tiles a round. Every other way to win still counts. Sight is a circle of 2 tiles (a Scout's a circle of 4, and a Scout moves up to 3 tiles in any direction), and a meteor can fall on any turn (one in twenty). The field uses the large-map camera. Field: the Hill (default), or the Cross (the Hill is the 2 by 2 very middle of its diamond; one player to an arm, then two to an arm in 3 by 2 boxes; in team mode each side holds two opposite arms), or the Small Cross (the Hill is its center tile; up to 4 players, one to an arm). The hole option is off on a Cross played for the Hill.

12.A.4 Online

Play with friends on their own devices. One player chooses Online → Open a room and shares the five-letter room code; the others choose Online → Join and type it in. The game runs in the host's browser: the others see it from their own seat and play by clicking as usual. The host sets up the game as usual, and each seat can be the host, a player in the room, or a computer. Online, play is always turn by turn and there is no phase timer. Everyone sees the same animations and hears the same sounds, from their own seat. If a player leaves, a computer takes over their seat until they come back: the game reconnects on its own for a while, and joining the same room again from the same browser gives the seat back. The host's device can hold more than one player ("Here, on this device"), who pass it as in a local game. Anyone who joins without a seat, or after the start, watches. There is a chat for everyone in the room (bottom left). No account and nothing to install: the connection is made through the free PeerJS service and then runs directly between the browsers (a few strict school or work networks block that). To try it alone, type LOCAL as the room code before opening a room, and join it with LOCAL from a second tab of the same browser.

12.A.5 Survival

A tower defense for one to four players, always played with the midline. By default it is played on the large map, 6 wide and 10 long: the Horde holds the top two lines, and each survivor spawns in a box 3 wide and 2 deep at the bottom (two boxes side by side, then a second row of boxes on top of them; one survivor holds the whole back line). The standard sizes are offered only while every survivor gets the same five or more spawn tiles behind the midline: one survivor 5 to 7 wide, two 6 or 8 wide, three 9 wide (four always play on the large map), splitting the bottom two lines between them; the Horde spawns once for each survivor still standing, and the survivors take their turns one after another on the device. You open with an Ace and its Jack; the Horde takes the first turn. The first three rounds are a grace period. Then the Horde spawns a wave every 1d2 + 2 rounds from a fresh deck on its two lines, each wave a step stronger than the last (bigger every wave, with royals from the sixth wave and Jokers from the eleventh), stacking where it can, then moves on you and attacks the stacks that threaten it most. It has no spells, hand, deck or grave: its dead vanish, unless your Joker captures them. Spells that only touch a hand or a deck (Forced Hand, Double Edged Sword, Three of Swords, Naked Hand) are off. The moon still turns and Blood Moons still rise. When a Horde Joker shoots itself, the cards it captured from you go to the Outer Darkness and you lose 1 mana. From the eleventh wave, when you lean on something it has no answer to, a wave can bring two counter cards with it, never wildcards: Battering Rams against your Walls, Archers against packed bases, now and then a Healer. No more than six counter cards stand on the field at once. Every round opens with its number in the middle of the screen. You lose when your last card on the field falls, and the round count, in Roman numerals, is your score. In Survival the players are named P1, P2 and so on, Field Automation plays at Hard by default, and the Ether is Infinite by default.

12.A.6 More players

  • 12.A.6.a Free-for-all (3 or 4 players): 7 by 7 by default, or any square from 5 to 9, with the corners cut by default. Each player spawns and raises Aces only on their own edge. The last player standing wins.
  • 12.A.6.b The game is Free For All (1v1 with two players), Teams, or King of the Hill. With Teams you choose where teammates spawn.
  • 12.A.6.c Factions (Teams, then Factions; 3 to 8 players): three or four teams, Red, Blue, Green and Yellow, of one or two players each. A faction's players always spawn side by side on its own side (an arm of the Cross, or an edge of the 10 by 10 map; one player alone holds the whole end). Every card carries its faction's color in its top-left and bottom-right corners. The last faction standing wins.
  • 12.A.6.d Teams (two against two, 4 players; or two teams of up to four on the Cross, two to an arm, on opposite arms or arms side by side): Side by side (6 by 6 by default, 6 to 8 wide and 4 to 6 tall; each team holds one end and each player half its columns, like 1v1; on the Cross, both on one arm), Opposite (teammates on opposite edges or arms), or On neighboring edges (two edges or arms that meet at a corner). Each player chooses Side A or Side B in their setup; each side needs two players. Teammates can't attack each other. A team wins when both enemies have fallen. Every card of a side carries its side's color (Red or Blue) in its top-left and bottom-right corners, and each player's box names the side.
  • 12.A.6.e Decks: each team in 2v2 splits one deck by default (two suits and one Joker to each teammate); with a shared team deck teammates draw from one deck and spend from one soul pile but keep separate hands.
  • 12.A.6.f When a player falls: their deck and hand go to the grave and the grave is shuffled (default), or they go to the Outer Darkness. A shared team deck stays with the teammate still standing.
  • 12.A.6.g Turn order: drawing is the first part of the prep phase; press Done drawing to go on to the rest of it. Classic (each player's whole turn in order), draw and prep together with attacks in turns (the default with one person at the table), or every phase together. Played together, everyone draws together ("Waiting for players to draw" shows until all are done), and the rest of prep starts when everyone is done drawing. More than one person on one device always plays classic.
  • 12.A.6.h Phase timer (off by default): 30, 60 or 90 seconds a phase; under 60 and 90 the draw phase gets 30. When it runs out the game moves to the next phase, and during setup it places your cards the way a Medium computer would.
  • 12.A.6.i Computer speed: slow, normal (default) or fast.

12.C Setup options

  • 12.C.1 Field size: 1v1 is 5 (default), 6 or 7 tiles wide, with the midline on (default) or off and the corners cut or kept. Free-for-all and the edge 2v2 layouts are a square from 5 to 9 (7 by default); 2v2 on the same side and co-op Survival pick a width and a height. On a phone, 5 wide is the largest size that stays easy to read.
  • 12.C.2 Midline: on by default; can be removed outside Survival.
  • 12.C.3 Battle map: no map (plain tiles) by default, Random (one of the four regular maps), or chosen: Keep (a stone keep on your backline, a graveyard on the midline, the enemy's barracks), Sahel (red laterite and dry grass of the far north at night, with mud-brick walls and thatched huts), Forest, Dungeon (no castle or barracks), or the special maps: River and no map. Every map is at night. The art is drawn under the tiles and shows through the gaps between them. The maps are drawn in pixel art, like the dungeon background; the Menu's Map art switches back to the old drawings.
  • 12.C.4 Spawn lines (not King of the Hill): a field 6 by 6 or smaller has one spawn line a side, your backline; larger 1v1 fields have two. With three or more players on a field 7 by 7 or larger, each spawn line is the five tiles in the middle of its edge. Teammates who share a side (2v2) hold both of its rows, half the width each. You spawn only in your own spawn lines (and on your Bank land and Flags): in Free For All and Factions a teammate or an ally may let you spawn in theirs, asked in Diplomacy (a computer teammate always agrees, a computer ally only at Great trust), and anyone can keep teammates' and allies' cards out of their spawn lines; in the other modes nobody shares. Any Ace (the opening one too) goes on any empty tile you may spawn on.
  • 12.C.5 Large maps (the Map tab's Size): 10 long and 6 wide (1v1, or 2v2 with each team on one short end), 10 by 10 (any layout), the Cross (a real cross: four arms 6 wide and 6 long around a diamond middle, a square about 14 on a side turned on its point; one player at the end of each arm, and in 2v2 teammates on opposite arms, on arms side by side, or both on one arm, each in a box 3 wide and 2 deep; the Small Cross has arms 3 by 3 around a diamond about 6 on a side, one player to an arm; the middle can have a hole in its very center and, on the standard size, four hollows, 2 by 2 each, out on its diagonals, one tile from the hole and one from the diamond's edge), the Two-armed Cross (only the top and bottom arms; up to 4 players, two to an arm in boxes 3 wide and 2 deep; 2 on the small size), and Cross Map 2 (the 10 by 10 plus with arms 4 wide; everyone spawns at the end of an arm). A large map opens on your own spawn. Spawn lines are two deep, away from the corners. A large map scrolls inside a frame: the mouse wheel zooms around the pointer, dragging an empty spot or WASD moves it (Shift: faster; two at once go diagonally), E zooms in and Q out, always around the middle of the screen; on a touch screen it scrolls. The Menu's Large Map Controls can turn the wheel off, reverse the wheel's or the keys' zoom, and set the speed; every key can be changed in the settings' Controls tab (Key bindings), where the Alternative Controls move the camera with the arrow keys and the field's selector with WASD.
  • 12.C.6 River: a wide brown river like the Ogun near Lagos runs along the midline. It can only be crossed on a bridge two tiles wide, on the left or the right. The River map is always 7 wide and 5 tall and can't cut its corners. It isn't offered in Survival.
  • 12.C.7 Hole in the middle (off by default, never on the River): an empty pit of one or two tiles each way in the center, doubled (a cross) or tripled (a square) where there's room outside everyone's lines.
  • 12.C.8 Headquarters (Standard): each side opens with two Aces as bases, the first holding its Jack, and troops can only be spawned inside your own Aces. New Aces may still be raised in your spawn lines and become bases too. The only ways to win are destroying every enemy base or an economic victory; losing every other card on the field doesn't end the game.
  • 12.C.9 Decks per player: half a deck (one red and one black suit and one Joker; in 1v1 the two players split one deck, dealt at random or chosen; the default in 2v2), one deck (default: all four suits and both Jokers), two decks or three. With two or more, set Specials and Aces to Half, so everyone starts with half the specials and bases.
  • 12.C.10 Ether decks per player: the Ether's Scouts, Assassins, Archers, Healers, Repairmen, Walls, Rams, Miners and Cannons come in as many decks' worth as the players hold, or its own number: half a deck, one (the default: the Ether holds a deck of its own for every player), two or three per player; or Infinite (the default in Survival): whatever is taken comes back at once, so the Ether never runs out. The Ether (Rules, on by default) can be turned off for any game: nothing can then be conjured or bought from it (the Ether shop, the conjurings that bring a soul from the Ether, mystery buys), and it holds nothing
  • 12.C.11 No Emanations (on by default in Survival): every player plays by the plain rules, with no Emanation's advantages or drawbacks.
  • 12.C.12 An empty deck costs mana: every card your deck can't give to a toll, a price or a draw something else makes you take costs 1 mana instead, as far below zero as it goes; past the floor you are out (economic victory). Your own draw roll is the exception: it takes only what the deck has, and with an empty deck there is nothing to roll for. When a Heavenly Body asks for cards from your hand and your hand is empty, it takes them from your deck, then mana (an enemy's spell on your hand stops at the hand). Every window that asks for deck cards says what a short deck will cost.
  • 12.C.13 Specials and Aces (Jacks, Queens, Kings, Jokers and Aces): none besides the opening Ace and its Jack; half (the default): the Jacks, Queens and Kings of half your suits, picked at random (with a full deck, one red and one black suit, say Hearts and Spades), and one more Ace and one Joker (one of each per deck you hold), nothing else; or all. Every special a player does not start with waits in the Ether, ready to be conjured. Besides the specials, a deck starts with only its standard cards (its soldiers and its two Scouts): no Cannon, no Carriage, nothing else from the Ether.
  • 12.C.14 At least one Joker (on by default; with None or All): every player's deck holds at least one Joker, taken from the Ether when the specials setting would leave none. Half always holds one.
  • 12.C.15 A Miner in every deck (on by default): one Miner, taken from the Ether, is shuffled somewhere into every player's deck.
  • 12.C.16 Starting mana: each player starts with souls in their soul pile, taken from the Ether as soldier souls (or newly made when the Ether has none). Default 3d2; it can be 1d6, a fixed number, or none. With half a deck each player rolls their own by default; with one deck or more one roll sets the same starting mana for everyone by default. Either can be switched.
  • 12.C.17 Moon combat bonus (on by default): at the Full Moon every attack adds 1d2 to its score; at the New Moon every defense adds 1d2.
  • 12.C.18 Heavenly Body shop (on by default): any of the eight Heavenly Bodies can be bought for the sky. See The Heavenly Body shop under The sky.
  • 12.C.19 Striking Emanations (not in Survival): the small check in an Emanation tile strikes it from the game for every seat: nobody can pick it and Random never rolls it. At least as many Emanations as seats stay (no two players share one), and Random needs two to choose from.
  • 12.C.20 Striking Heavenly Bodies: the check on a Heavenly Body under Starting Heavenly Body strikes it from Astrology: it can't be bought in the Heavenly Body shop and the Firmament can't raise it. The starting Heavenly Body can't be struck.
  • 12.C.21 Spells in the attack phase (off by default): enchantments, curses, sorcery and illusions can also be cast during the attack phase. Necromancy, conjuring and sacrifices stay in the prep phase.
  • 12.C.22 Optional Ace rule: the cards inside an Ace must match its color.
  • 12.C.23 Blood Moon mode: the game begins on a Blood Moon. Each player's deck is shuffled before two thirds of it go to the graveyard, and each player starts with one Joker in hand.
  • 12.C.24 Diplomacy (on by default in Factions; an option in Free For All): a flag under the Bank opens Diplomacy between the sides (each player in Free For All, each faction in Factions). Everyone starts at war. Peace is a pact of non-aggression: neither side may attack the other for 1 to 4 rounds, as agreed, then it is war again unless renewed; Mercury's Total War lifts it while it lasts, and breaking it makes the computers' trust in you fall all the way (−100). An Alliance shares vision, lets friendly spells reach each other, and makes surrounding an enemy work as it does for teammates; there is no shared win, and when only two sides are left every alliance ends by itself (peace is still allowed). Breaking an alliance makes trust fall hard (−70 with them, −40 with everyone else), and for 8 rounds the Banker disapproves: Bank loans grow half as fast again, cards from the Ether cost 1d3 − 2 more (from −1 to +1) and other spells 1d4 − 3 more (from −2 to +1), rolled each round, so the dice can also make them cheaper (never below 0); under Total War none of that applies and trust falls less (−30 and −10). Trust is the computers': from −100 to +100, in colors from dark red (hostile) through red, orange, yellow (neutral), light green and green to a rich green (great); it starts at −20, rises with peace and more with an alliance, falls with every attack, and decides what a computer accepts. A proposal to a person waits up to two rounds for Accept, Decline or Counter (another number of rounds of peace). Envoys: a Joker from your hand or the field can go as your diplomat to another side, two at most; souls it held go to the grave as it leaves the field, and recalled it goes back into your deck as it was. Each envoy is a point with that side: a computer side trusts you 4 more each round and counts it for 10 more when you propose or ask for a loan, and it adds a vote at the Council. Allies can give each other mana or a hand card, and agree trade deals: mana each round, each way, for 1 to 4 rounds (a side that can't pay ends it, and trust falls). Loans between players are asked and answered in Diplomacy while it is on; a computer lends by its trust in you. Terms of war (two sides at war, both agreeing): first to kill an Ace of the other (or the Tree, when a Jester has one on the field) within 8 rounds; holding the middle longest over 4 rounds (the middle tiles are outlined in red while it runs, and at each round's end whoever holds more of them scores); or the most value killed over 4 rounds (each card killed scores its soul's worth). The winner takes 2 + 1d6 mana in tribute, and Peace follows for 2 rounds; a tie brings the peace without tribute. The Council meets on the first round after every cycle (every 8 rounds) and votes on one resolution: the Ether's Tithe (every conjuring costs 1 more), War Tax (every attack costs the attacker 1 mana), the Bank's Dividend (1 mana at the start of each round), Cheap Credit (Bank loans don't grow) or the Silence (no Sorcery). Each player has a vote and one more for each envoy out; more votes for than against passes it for 8 rounds.
  • 12.C.25 How the sky changes (By bidding by default): after 2 + 1d2 − 1 completed cycles (the Moon's cycle ends at the Full Moon, every 8 rounds; every other Heavenly Body's cycle is as long, every 8 rounds), the Heavenly Body announces that it is leaving. The bidding comes at the end of the round of that cycle event (or eclipse), once the last attack phase is over: every player bids mana openly, to keep it for one more cycle or to see it go, or passes; everyone sees each bid as it comes, and the bidding goes round again while anyone raises. The highest bid wins, keeping wins a tie, and only the winner pays, to the Bank; a Heavenly Body that loses leaves at the start of the next round; under that Heavenly Body's Oath a bid counts half again on a 2 of 1d2. If nobody bids to keep it, a new Heavenly Body takes the sky, picked at random (never one that is eaten or struck). The host can choose instead: Never by itself, Every few cycles or Every few rounds (a number, or dice of up to 100 in all, rolled again for every new Heavenly Body; every few rounds can give each one a random start in its cycle). Oaths hold through every change.
  • 12.C.26 Cycle events and Eclipses (both on by default) can each be turned off: no Full Moon toll, no cycle of Venus, Mercury, Mars, Jupiter or Saturn, no Second Sun judgement or cycle; or no eclipses and no Blood Moon.
  • 12.C.27 Eclipse mode: the game opens under the eclipse of the chosen sky (the Blood Moon, for the moon), and every cycle event is an eclipse: every Full Moon is a Blood Moon, and every other Heavenly Body is eclipsed each time its cycle comes round. Eclipses stay on. Checking it shows Always in eclipse: the eclipse never passes. The Moon is a Blood Moon at every phase (its phases still turn); any other Heavenly Body stays eclipsed for as long as it hangs in the sky, and a new one is eclipsed from the next round.

12.E The Bank and the economy

  • 12.E.1 Spending: Every mana spent on a spell goes to the Bank. The Bank lends only what it holds.
  • 12.E.2 Loans: mana now, paid out of the Bank's own mana (the Bank can go 8 into the red, no further). Up to 12, 6 if you are below zero, 5 at minus your maximum. Due in half the amount in rounds, at least 2 (12 mana: 6 rounds). A loan grows by 1d2 every 3 rounds. It is never taken back by itself: when it comes due the Bank reminds you, and you pay it at the Bank with Repay; left open for 3 + (owed ÷ 3) rounds, it brings the Debt Collectors. Only credit (mana below zero, mana debt) pays itself off, as mana comes in. One loan at a time, one a round, and the next no sooner than 3 rounds after the last.
  • 12.E.3 Credit: With Spend on credit ticked, spells you can't afford go on your debt, with no other limit. Mana debt grows by 1d2 every 3 rounds, and every soul you gain pays it first.
  • 12.E.4 The floor: You can go down to −8 mana. Below −8 you lose at once.
  • 12.E.5 Upselling: A spell bought more than twice in each of two rounds in a row costs 1d2 − 1 more.
  • 12.E.6 A Bank in the red: The Ether goes on sale (1 less), and the Bank asks the richest player for a loan, which it owes back with 20% more every 1d2 rounds.
  • 12.E.7 Debt Collectors (tax collectors): Stay below zero for 3 rounds, or leave a Bank loan open for 3 + (owed ÷ 3) rounds, and you are in default. From then on, at the start of each round the Bank sends 1d2 Debt Collectors (half as many again after six rounds in default; six at most on the field) after whoever in default owes the most, and they strike every round until it is paid off. Only a player who owes the Bank can strike a Debt Collector, so teammates can't fight them off for you. They wear the Bank's top hat. Whatever they kill goes to the Ether, and its soul worth pays off the victim's debt. On a 4 of 1d4 a good card they kill (not a 2 to 10, never an Emanation card) turns up at the Auction instead, sold by the Bank for less than the Ether asks, to anyone who pays.
  • 12.E.8 The Bank's land: the Bank window's Land tab sells eight plots, 1 mana each, one plot every two rounds. A plot is a Miner slot no enemy can reach: put a Miner from your hand in it and it brings 1d2 − 1 mana a round, like a Miner in an Ace (1d2 under Jupiter). Jupiter in the sky adds 1 mana a plot each round, and 1d2 a plot during its eclipse. A plot with its Miner can be sold back to the Bank for 2 to 3 mana (1 + 1d2, rolled after the sale): it settles the Bank's half of that plot's takings, then pays your debt, and the rest goes to your soul pile; the Bank sells plot and Miner on for 3 mana. When a player is out of the game, the Bank takes their land. At every Full Moon (every eighth round when the Moon isn't in the sky) the Bank takes half of what your plots brought in since. What you can't pay becomes mana debt and Debt Collectors come for it; miss two pay days in a row and the Bank takes the land back and turns each Miner on it into two Slave cards, sold at the Auction for 1 mana each. Whenever the Bank sells something of a player who owes it, the money pays that player's debt.
  • 12.E.9 Loans between players: the Bank window's Between players tab. Ask another player for 1 to 8 mana; they refuse, or lend it and set when it is due, 2 to 12 rounds (a request waiting for you lights the Bank with a small "!"). You then see the terms and take the loan or turn it down. It grows by 1d2 every 3 rounds, to the lender, until it is paid. It is never taken by itself: the borrower pays it at the Bank with Repay, choosing which loan (the Bank's or a player's). When it is due they are reminded; still unpaid a round later, the Bank sends Debt Collectors after them, and whatever those kill goes to the lender until it is paid. The Auction lights up the same way when something new is for sale.
  • 12.E.10 The Credit and Loan Report (the Bank window's own tab): every Full Moon (every eighth round when the Moon isn't in the sky) each player gets a paper for the turns since the last: mana taken on loans and on credit and the two together, the interest on each and together, the taxes taken, the mana earned, and the standing. Standing turns bad by going into default, leaving a tax unpaid or robbing the Bank; in bad standing the Debt Collectors inquire.
  • 12.E.11 Bounties (the Bank window's own tab): each Report puts a bounty on every player in bad standing (debt, unpaid loans or taxes, a robbery), worth what they owe. Under the Oath of Mercury, or for anyone while Mercury is in the sky, you can take one, more than one at a time. Taking one puts 5 Debt Collectors, made from nothing, into your deck and 2 into your hand, even past the hand limit (the only time it can be broken). Placed, they strike only that player, a teammate too, without it being betrayal. More can be bought for 2 mana each (3 at most on the field for a bounty); those come down near the target. What they kill goes to the Ether and its price comes off the target's debt; a captured support card of the target's can be turned in for its price too. Collect it all within two Reports: the Bank pays you half of what was owed and forgives your own debts and loans. Fail and your Debt Collectors in hand, deck and field go over to the Bank, your other bounties are cancelled, and you can't take another until the next Report. A bounty ends with no reward if the target pays everything first. Debt Collectors can't be bought any other way.
  • 12.E.12 Taxes (taken only on Report day): 1d2 for a base in the middle of the field (ground that is nobody's home rows); every other Ace or Tree has a 1 in 3 chance (1 in 2 in bad standing) of a 1d2 tax. Taxes can't be paid on credit: with no mana they go unpaid, and the standing turns bad.
  • 12.E.13 Land in the middle: 2 mana at the Bank buys a spawn point on a free tile of the middle ground, on your side of the midline, for 8 rounds, the round you buy it in included (one at a time; none in King of the Hill), marked with a $ in your color. Any card but a Wall can come down there; a card that comes down without a base on the tile costs 1d2 − 1. A base there stays a spawn point after the land runs out, until it falls.
  • 12.E.14 Gifts and forgiveness: in the Bank window's Between players tab a lender can forgive a loan, and anyone can give mana or a card from the hand to another player. Anyone can trade a card from the hand at the Auction.
  • 12.E.15 Collapse: If the Bank and every player are 3 or more below zero, it collapses: everyone back to zero, every maximum falls to three, five cards from each deck and one Ace from the field go to the Ether, and prices wobble up for 4d2 rounds.
  • 12.E.16 Opening and closing: The Bank can open with (2 × players)d2 mana (off by default). One round in a thousand the Banker dies of a heart attack, and the Bank and the Ether close for good.
  • 12.E.17 Surplus mana: you can hold up to three times your maximum for a round (6/6 can become 18/6); at the start of the next round anything over the maximum goes to the grave.
  • 12.E.18 Gambling (Sorcery, 1 mana): 1d10; a natural 10 wins 7d2 mana from the Bank; a 1 costs one maximum mana, 7d2 of mana debt and a card from your deck. Increase Max Mana (Conjuring, 4): +1 maximum mana, eighteen at most. Mystery Generation (in the Auction, 3 to 8 mana, rolled when you pay): three brand-new cards, face down until you turn them, made from thin air into your deck. Rarity: Common 47.5%, Uncommon 23.75%, Rare 14.25%, Epic 4.75%, Legendary 4.75%, Mythic 5% (the wildcards Antony, Goatbull, the Leviathan, Samson, BullGoat and Satan, rarer the stronger they are; never the Collector, Slave, Slavemaster or Trafficker). A wildcard comes without its Emanation's spells, and only the Leviathan player's Leviathan can rise to the moon.
  • 12.E.19 Demons (on by default): every 4 + 1d12 rounds (never while a band still stands), and with every Blood Moon, a crimson band takes the middle of the field: a stack of 1d3 + 1 Grunts, a second stack (more Grunts, or 1d3 Infantry or 1d2 Jacks), sometimes a third, and an Ace base on a 6 of 1d6; more with three or four players (at least two Aces with four). They don't move and strike at anything beside them. Whoever kills the most of a band gets an Ether sale for three rounds (every conjuring costs 1d2 − 1 less, rolled each round, but never less than 1 mana); if the band had an Ace that died and two or more stacks, or three stacks and no Ace, the Banker also forgives that player's debts. If Demons hold the middle for 7d2 rounds, the Banker gets angry and every price goes up by 1 until they're gone.
  • 12.E.20 The meteor: one round in a hundred a meteor falls on a random tile: one damage there, one damage on a 4 of 1d4 on every tile around it (Walls take 1d2 + 1), and fire burns the area for two rounds (one damage on a natural 20 at the start of a stack's turn).

12.F Siege, support and mining

  • 12.F.1 Archers (standard, 1d2 mana): 1d20 + 1d10 at a stack exactly two tiles away, straight or diagonal; a miss costs nothing; no defense. Or Suppress: the target attacks with disadvantage next time (or waits 1d2 rounds), and the Archer rests 1d2 rounds. Alone, with a Queen, or inside an Ace, the Tree or a Wall. Eight per deck in the Ether.
  • 12.F.2 Cannons (6 mana, one per deck): 2d20 at anything up to three tiles away, once every 1d2 + 2 rounds; a miss costs nothing; two damage to a Wall; it can't take an Ace's last heart or harm the Tree. One step a round, no defense; alone or inside a base.
  • 12.F.3 Walls (base, 4 mana): five hearts, defense 10, never move; go up on any empty tile of your spawn lines; hold one stack like an Ace, but only Archers and Cannons fire from inside (one shot a round). Charges hit them twice as hard; Rams, Cannons and Lightning Strike do two. When a Wall falls, everything inside dies.
  • 12.F.4 Lighthouses (base, 3 mana, as many as you like): in your prep phase one goes up on any empty tile beside or diagonal to one of your stacks that isn't a base, anywhere on the field, and never moves. It lifts the fog of war in a circle seven tiles across (eleven in King of the Hill); under the Firmament's Fog it sees no farther than any card. It holds one stack (no other base), which attacks and defends from inside it and takes the blows first; then the tower's three hearts go. It has no defense of its own. A Repairman can mend it; a Healer can't.
  • 12.F.5 Support cards: never fight and die last in their stack. Standing alone, an enemy beside them can Capture one into its stack's capture slot (one per stack), or Destroy it. Set a captive to work beside the stack in your prep phase and it is yours. Healer (2 + 1d3): a shield that turns one blow (back in 1d4 rounds), or one heart healed in or beside its stack (1d2 + 2 rounds between heals; the shield is down 3 rounds after); never with a Queen. Repairman (2): one heart back to a Wall, Ace or Tree in or beside his stack, then 1d3 + 1 rounds of rest during which nothing fires from that base; he can strengthen the Tree up to five hearts, or upgrade an Ace once with his life. Battering Ram (3): worked by one or two Grunts, Infantry or Puppets, two damage to a Wall beside it; blows never kill it. Miner (2): in an Ace (as many Miners and Repairmen as you like; selecting an Ace leaves them behind unless you check them), 1d2 − 1 mana a round from the next round; on a crystal it digs; elsewhere 1 mana on a 6. Miners and Repairmen ride along with a stack without counting toward its value, but only with cards that may stand with others (not Samson or Antony, a Cannon, an Assassin, Satan, the BullGoat or a Slave); outside an Ace two Miners (or two Repairmen) never share a stack. In a hit stack they are the last cards to be hurt, and they can be captured only once no card is left to defend them.
  • 12.F.6 Crystals (on by default): a crystal cluster with 12 mana rises in the middle of the field every 1d6 + 4 rounds (Headquarters opens with one); a Miner on it digs 1 + 1d3 mana whenever it rolls a 2 on 1d2.
  • 12.F.7 Field Libation (Necromancy, free, not under the Full Moon): your stacks show an X; check cards and pour them out; each is worth its mana on a 2 of 1d2 and goes to the grave on a 1.
  • 12.F.8 Stacked odds: advantage and disadvantage cancel one for one. Two of the same kind roll with that advantage (or disadvantage) and add +2 (or −2) on top; each more adds 1.

12.G Fog and stealth

  • 12.G.1 Fog of war (on by default): each of your cards sees only the eight tiles around it (straight and diagonal), a Scout two tiles out in a diamond (two straight, one diagonal), and a Jack (or a stack holding one), Samson and Antony one tile further along each diagonal (not under the Firmament's Fog); foggy tiles are shaded. Archers, Cannons, Rifles and the Slugshot Cannon may fire into a fogged tile in reach: with nothing there the shot is always lost, and a hit is heard but not seen. A fogged tile looks empty, so a stack may try to move into it (or through it on a two-tile step): if a hidden enemy card stands there, the move stops with Card Blocks Path (Cards Block Path for a stack), the stack keeps its move, and that tile shows to you and your side for a round (through your next turn). It can be switched off before a game.
  • 12.G.2 Claimers (special, marked F, a flag): never attack or defend; one heart. Two per deck wait in the Ether (Claimer Conjure: 2 mana). In your prep phase a stack holding one can Claim Land: a Flag goes up on that tile, and the Claimer dies, his card going straight back to the Ether. A Flag is not a card: it has three hearts (shown while nobody stands on it), turns see-through under a stack like a crystal, and is a spawn point for bases: you can put a base down on your own empty Flag anywhere on the field. Every base but a Wall is a spawn point too: you can place cards into your own Ace or Tree wherever it stands. An enemy stack beside a Flag (or on it) can strike it in its attack phase for one heart, no roll, as its attack; a Repairman beside your Flag can mend a heart, then rests 1d3 + 1 rounds.
  • 12.G.3 Scouts (support): never attack or defend; see two tiles out in a diamond (two straight, one diagonal), and uncover everything there: Assassins, ambushers, face-down cards and enemy Scouts. Enemies see a Scout only from right beside it or with their own Scout close by. Every deck holds two; your opening Ace brings one from the Ether, and a later Ace brings one while you have no Scout on the field (Lucifer & Ouroboros get none). Scout Conjure: 1 mana.
  • 12.G.4 Assassins: two hearts (a pair stacked together has three), attack 10 + 1d12, defense 8; one straight, two diagonal, or two forward or back. Hidden like a Scout. Only another Assassin stacks with one. Assassin Conjure: 2d2 + 1 mana.
  • 12.G.5 Face-down cards: tick "Place face down" before putting a card down. You see it with a faint stripe; enemies see only its back. It attacks with double disadvantage and defends with advantage, and flips face up once it attacks or is attacked. A Scout within two tiles sees what it is.
  • 12.G.6 Ambush: an Assassin or a face-down stack can be set in ambush (the card turns sideways). It vanishes from enemy sight unless they stand right beside it, and it strikes with advantage, out of turn, at the first enemy that steps beside it. Moving it ends the ambush.
  • 12.G.7 Quiet Eyes (Illusion, 2 mana): on each enemy's next turn, and only that turn, they see only the tiles their own cards stand on; every other tile is fog. Scouts don't see through it. Bird's Eye (Sorcery, 1d2 mana): the fog lifts for you and your team for the rest of the round; it doesn't help against a Delusion.
  • 12.G.8 Stray Mana (Illusion, 1 mana; then not for 7d2 rounds, or 6d2 or 5d2 under the Oath of Neptune on a 1d2): choose an enemy. For the rest of the round, each mana they earn or spend rolls 1d20: on a 1 it strays to you (what they earn comes to you, what they spend is paid to you); on a 20 you share it (you gain what they gain, and pay what they pay).

12.H On the screen

  • 12.H.1 Spells: the right column holds the Bank, then the spells in their own scroll box, then the selected stack. Within each school the spells run from cheapest to dearest. Rest the mouse on a spell (or long-press it on a touch screen) for its price, when it can be cast, what it needs, what it does and its limits.
  • 12.H.2 The Ether: the Ether entry at the top of Conjuring opens a panel on the right with every card you can conjure, by type (Standard, Special, Support, Base), cheapest first, with how many are left. Increase Max Mana sits right under it.
  • 12.H.3 Oaths and Heavenly Bodies: Astrology starts with the Oaths panel, and for the Firmament a Heavenly Bodies panel with its Heavenly Bodies. Spells that need a certain sky (Peace Time, Total War, Buy Peace, The Sun Cries) show only while that sky is up.
  • 12.H.4 Grave Blessing on its own: the auto switch on the left of Grave Blessing casts it for you at the start of every prep phase.
  • 12.H.5 Configure Automation (the ▦ button by the turn buttons, or the ⚙︎ on Ritual Dice and Lazy Hand): four tabs, each with its own on/off switch at the top. Field: the computer moves and attacks with the cards on your field, as set (it can be switched off for everyone before a game; the other three always stay). Ritual Dice: buys Ritual Dice before Grave Blessing, by a playstyle or your own Custom settings, which are remembered. Lazy Hand: places and sacrifices hand cards. Grave: picks the cards raised from the grave (Grave Blessing, the New Moon and the rest): which kinds, with a Yes-and list for single kinds, in what order, the strongest first; when nothing matches it can ask, or raise nothing. Every ranking of kinds starts grouped (Special, Base, Standard, Support, Wildcard) and can be split into single kinds, like the King.
  • 12.H.6 Display: the Display button at the bottom of the screen opens the theme, the card icon size, the interface size, the text size (small words grow the most), the hand position, the tile names (columns A, B, C from the left and rows 1, 2, 3 from the bottom, like a chessboard) and the page width (full width by default on a computer). The static switch sits beside it. The Menu holds the same settings, plus where the selected-stack box and the turn buttons sit.
  • 12.H.7 Card info: rest the mouse on the top-right corner of any card or stack for its stats, or tap the corner on a touch screen: type (Standard, Special, Support, Wildcard or Base), attack and defense averages with their min–max, hearts, soul worth, effects, the cards in it and anything it captured or imprisoned. Drag that corner from one stack on the field onto another to compare them: higher in green, lower in red, equal in yellow. The selected-stack box shows the same stats and can compare with another stack or any kind of card.
  • 12.H.8 Status: hover or tap a player's symbol for their limits, effects and recent spells; hover or tap the sky for what it means for you.
  • 12.H.9 Windows: every window has an X in its top-right corner, and Escape closes it.
  • 12.H.10 Log: every action is listed with the time to the second on your computer, colored in the color of the player who took it, with the tiles it happened on.
  • 12.H.11 Dice box history: each dice box notes the player's last spell, the tile it was cast on and the time. Click a dice box to open the history of every player's rolls and spells, with tiles and the time on your computer; click anywhere else or press Escape to close it.
  • 12.H.12 Menu: at the top of the page; it pauses the game and holds every display setting.
  • 12.H.13 Phase timer off: while a timer is running, the quote box has a button that switches it off for the rest of the game.
