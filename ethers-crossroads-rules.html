Ether's Crossroads

All Rules

See the bottom of this page to understand game types and rule options.

How to Start

  • Every player starts with one Ace from their own deck already on the field, on any tile of their backline they choose. Every Ace spawns with one Jack from that player's deck inside it, wherever it is placed. Which Ace or Jack doesn't matter.
  • First when starting, draw 2d4 cards for both sides.
  • Second, both players roll dice; whoever goes first by highest roll will set up their cards on the field first, then the second player places their cards on the field.
  • Third, the one with the highest roll takes the first game-starting turn, with the prep phase as the first part of this turn. The player will be able to move cards, place cards, or even sacrifice cards, and during this prep phase, the starting player's attack will not happen yet.
  • The other player can now be attacked; this is the attack phase, and the attacker cannot move their cards, place their cards, or sacrifice during this phase. After, this is the end of the first player's turn.
  • The second player at the start of their turn has an optional draw card phase: roll a d4 as many times as wanted, and the dice result tells how many cards to draw. When doing this optional phase, you cannot place cards, move cards, sacrifice cards, or attack.
  • What follows is the prep phase; the player can move cards, place cards, or sacrifice cards, but cannot attack during this phase.
  • Last is the final phase of the turn, and no actions from the prep phase can be taken during it: the attack phase. Each card can only attack once.

The players should use this pattern each turn:

Optional Draw Card Phase → Prep Phase → Attack Phase

  • As many times as desired, roll four-sided dice to optionally draw cards.
  • Move cards. Place cards. Sacrifice cards.
  • Attack enemy cards on the field; each card can only attack once.

Major Rules

The goal is to either destroy all card placements in the field or have the other player run out of cards in their deck, hand, and soul pile. Running those cards out is called an economic domination or economic victory, and it is only valid when the other player completes their turn.

Certain rules can be changed as desired, such as the cards present in the Ethereal Realm, or the number of cards in the players' hands.

Beside the field lie the Graveyard and the Ethereal Realm.

  • The Ethereal Realm is a realm of form that can be a source of summoning, conjuring, or materialization. Spend or offer mana to summon souls into the material realm.
  • The grave is where dead souls sleep in their corpse, waiting to be set free. This is where sacrificed souls go after being spent of their mana, or where dead cards lie trapped and bound to their flesh. The grave is shared.

Card Roles and Stacking

2–10: Soldiers

  • 2–6 are Grunt soldiers; 7–10 are Infantry soldiers.
  • Grunts stack to four (24 ÷ 6) and Infantry to three (24 ÷ 8), or any mix whose dice add up to 24 or less.

Jacks

  • A Jack can only stack with two Grunts, one Infantry, or one other Jack (12 + 12 = 24).
  • Jacks can move two spaces, or use both for a diagonal.
  • Jacks cannot defend.

Queens

  • A Queen has no Dice Max, so she adds nothing to the stacking sum: she can join four Grunts, three Infantry, or a King with the one soldier he can hold.
  • Anyone stacked with the Queen gets modifiers for both offense and defense.
  • The Queen can use her modifier for both offense and defense.
  • The special abilities of Queens do not stack with other Queens, and they do not work in base or spawn mode.

Kings

  • Cards of the King's suit can be placed inside a King when defending.
  • A King has a DM of 20 and an MSV of 28, so he holds one Grunt or one Infantry at most (20 + 8 = 28).
  • A Joker can stack with the King, with his one soldier or without.
  • Cannot attack when defending while alone.
  • Cannot be sacrificed on the field when stacked in offensive stance.
  • A Queen can stack with a King, granting the Queen's bonuses. A Joker can also be stacked here, but only with a King present.
  • When successfully defeated in defensive placement mode, all units die.
  • Jokers can only spawn on a King of the same color.

Joker (Necromancer)

  • Any enemy killed next to the Joker goes to its sacrifice pile (Soul Capture): an upside-down stack inside the Joker, its personal sacrifice pile.
  • Has no Dice Max, so it can stack with four Grunts, three Infantry, one King, a King and Queen, or a King with his one soldier.
  • When the Joker sacrifices himself, the player gains access to its sacrifice pile.
  • The Joker's personal sacrifice pile holds up to six, though players can change this for balance.
  • A captured soul stays inside the Joker until the Joker dies, when the Joker and its souls go to the grave, or until it uses Sacrificial Suicide.
  • If the Joker is defeated by enemies, its cards go to the grave pile.
  • If a Joker kills itself during a Blood Moon, it sends itself and every card it held to the Outer Darkness.
  • Can only spawn on a card of the same color (red or black).

Ace

  • Each player opens the game with one Ace on their backline, holding one Jack.
  • Cannot move or attack, and is always in defense.
  • Can only be placed in open tiles in the backlines.
  • Anything inside an Ace cannot use modifiers for defense.
  • Aces can hold up to six cards inside.
  • If an Ace is destroyed, all cards inside are destroyed.
  • Cannot be sacrificed while on the field.
  • Each Ace on the field grants +1d6 for pulling cards from the grave.

Optional Ace rule

Card placement inside Aces can be locked to only the Ace's color (red, black) or suit (diamonds, spades, hearts, clubs).

Each card type has an MSV that limits its stacking based on its DM. Calculate how many cards can stack by dividing MSV by DM. For Grunts:

MSV ÷ DM = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 (allowing four Grunts)

Mixed stacks: different cards can stack together if their combined value doesn't exceed the highest MSV. The card in a stack with the highest MSV sets the MSV for the formula.

Stack Limit (SL): the most cards of a type that can be stacked, found by dividing the MSV by the DM for that type, or for the combined value of a mixed stack.

Σ DM ≤ MSV

where Σ DM is the sum of the dice max values of every card in the stack, and MSV is the Maximum Stack Value of the highest card in the mixed stack.

Hand Rules

  • Only six cards are allowed in a player's hand.
  • Cards drawn over the hand limit go to the grave pile.
  • Overdrawing: every card that is drawn and goes to the grave because the hand is full takes one die off that round's Grave Blessing maximum, so a maximum of six dice drops to five. The Grave Blessing shows the count as −{n}d6 max; past six it turns bold and red (7, 8, and so on) and the rest carries over: overdraw 7 cards and you have no Grave Blessing this round and −1d6 next round; past 12 it carries into the round after that, and so on. It only happens when there were cards in your deck to lose.
  • Cards can only be placed in their respective card spawn, such as Aces or Kings.
  • Cards in hand, or certain cards on the field, can be sacrificed during the sacrificial part of the prep phase.

Sacrificial Rules

Soul currency: each player has a pile of face-down sacrificed cards used as currency for mana, or soul points. This pile of publicly revealed cards owned by the player can be spent during the sacrificial part of the prep phase. The pile holds six mana at most.

Spells

Necromancy

  • Grave Blessing (free): roll 1d6; each natural 6 raises one card from the grave. Add +1d6 per Ace on the field, or more with Ritual Dice; 6 dice at most per turn.
  • Ritual Dice (1 mana): roll an additional 2d6 for your Grave Blessing.
  • Sacrificial Suicide: the Joker sacrifices its soul, and all the souls it captured move into the player's soul pile for spending. A Joker can hold six cards and six mana of souls in all; any surplus goes to the grave.
  • Soul Capture: the Joker captures the souls of enemies that die or are killed next to it, up to six mana of souls. They are not available to its player until Sacrificial Suicide. If the enemy kills the Joker, its souls are released into the graveyard, unless an enemy Joker is there to capture them.
  • Resurrection (1d2 + 1 mana): choose one card from the grave and bring it back into your hand.
  • Flesh Severence: because body and soul are two in this game, the Joker can spend mana from the soul pile to disembody the soul of a unit killed next to it, exiling its form to the Ethereal Realm. This is the player's choice, and it costs half the price to summon the units severed that turn, rounded up: severing a 2-mana and a 1-mana unit costs 2.

Enchantments

  • Hypostasis (3 mana for one card, +1 for every more card in the stack): grants a stack temporary invulnerability for two turns. During it the stack can't move forward toward the enemy's side, only to the side or backward if the stack's movement allows, and it can't attack. Afterward the effect disappears and the stack returns to its normal rules. It can't be cast if you have only one stack or card on the board.
  • Divine Odds (1d2 + 1 mana): advantage on all modifiers of one of your stacks for one combat. Mark the cards with a coin or chess piece. Advantage means rolling the dice twice and taking the higher total.

Curses

  • Forced Hand (1d2 mana): the other player draws 1d2 cards; any over their hand limit go straight to the grave.
  • Hexed Odds (1d2 + 1 mana): disadvantage on all modifiers of one enemy stack for one combat. Disadvantage means rolling twice and taking the lower total.
  • Three of Swords (1d3 mana): roll 1d2. A 1 destroys one random card from the enemy's hand and one from your own; a 2 destroys two from each. If you have nothing in your hand, you lose that many cards from your deck instead; the enemy loses nothing from the deck if their hand is empty.
  • Double Edged Sword: roll 3d6. A 1 means the caster loses a card from their deck; a 6 means the target loses a card from their deck. Only works against the deck.

Sorcery

  • Clairvoyance (2 + 1d2 mana): pull 2d6 cards from your deck and look at them; choose one to put in your hand or place on the board, then reshuffle. The number pulled is limited by the size of your deck.
  • Naked Hand (1d2 mana): reveals the other player's hand, and tells both players the cards have been seen. It costs 6 at the New Moon, nothing at the Full Moon, and 1 under a Blood Moon.
  • Teleport (2 mana): return a card that isn't in placement mode to your hand. You must have fewer than six cards in hand, no enemy next to it, and it must be in your two lines.

Rolled prices: a price written with dice is rolled behind the scenes at the start of each round, once for everyone, and that price holds for the whole round; the spell list shows it and the log lists the new prices. The moon's prices replace the roll only while their phase lasts: Clairvoyance costs 6 at the New Moon and 2 at the Full Moon; Naked Hand costs 6 at the New Moon, nothing at the Full Moon and 1 under a Blood Moon.

The Moon

Every round sits under one phase of the moon, shown at the top of the table. A round is one turn for each player, every turn with all its phases (draw, prep, attack); when every player has finished their turn, the moon moves to its next phase, in this order, and then it repeats:

New Moon → Waxing Crescent → First Quarter → Waxing Gibbous → Full Moon → Waning Gibbous → Last (Third) Quarter → Waning Crescent

New Moon

  • Players get to choose 1d2 cards from the grave for free.
  • Clairvoyance and Naked Hand cost six mana, and their spell options turn dark grey.

Full Moon

  • Players have to sacrifice their most powerful card or stack, by its point rating. If you don't accept, you are drained of all your mana and five random cards from your deck go to the grave.
  • All decks are shuffled.
  • No one can draw cards or cast necromancy spells.
  • Naked Hand is free and Clairvoyance costs only two mana; both turn a bright grey, the color of the Full Moon.

Blood Moon

  • Every Full Moon may turn into a Blood Moon. The moon turns red, and so does every necromancy spell, and the table turns the crimson of the Disturbed Satan chapter and starts to glitch.
  • New Moon effects take place: players pick cards from the grave. The Full Moon's drawbacks (the toll, the shuffle, no drawing, no necromancy) are void, but its cheaper prices stay.
  • Every card killed during the Blood Moon is sent to the Outer Darkness and can never be returned. A Joker that kills itself during it sends all the cards it held to the Outer Darkness.

The Outer Darkness

Cards in the Outer Darkness can be viewed at any time, in a box the red of the Disturbed Satan chapter. Each card carries the symbol of the player it belonged to at the time of its death.

How the digital table reads the rules

Some rules leave room for the players to decide. Play Game uses these readings:

  • In Local, two to four players share one device; the screen goes black between turns until the next player confirms, and the board turns so the player at the device always sits at the bottom. With one person at the device, that person always sits at the bottom. Hands show face down to the other side unless Naked Hand reveals them.
  • The field is five tiles wide by default, or six or seven. It is five lines tall with the midline (the default) and four without it. Survival always keeps the midline. Three and four players use a 7 by 7 field (corners cut by default) where each player's back line is their whole edge; two against two on the same side uses 6 by 6, two lines and three columns for each player.
  • When play is together, the computers take their phase first and you play yours after; the shared phase ends when you press Ready. Spells that pick one player (Forced Hand, Double Edged Sword, Three of Swords, Naked Hand) ask which opponent when there is more than one, and Naked Hand shows that one hand only, to the caster and the caster's team.
  • Cut corners remove the four corner tiles from play. Nothing can stand on a removed tile or the river, and a Jack cannot leap over them; the only way across the River map is its two-tile bridge.
  • The opening roll is a d6 each, rerolled on a tie. Each player's Ace and Jack are taken out of the deck before the opening 2d4 hand is drawn, and the Ace is placed during setup, before any other opening cards. The first turn of the game has no draw phase.
  • Scores use the Dice Max of each card: 2–6 roll d6, 7–10 roll d8, Jacks d12, Kings and Aces d20. Natural values are the card's number, Jack 10, King 11, Ace 11, Queen 0.
  • Stacking follows the MSV and DM formula, with one Queen and one Joker per stack at most. An Ace holds up to six cards inside it. Where an older line of the rules gave a different number (soldiers stacking to three, three soldiers with a King, a d12 for the King), the MSV, DM and SL formula decides.
  • A stack's score is its best card. Dice add on attack. On defense, cards use their natural value only, unless a Queen is in the stack; an Ace stack defends with the Ace's 11 + d20. Jacks don't count on defense.
  • If the attacker's total is higher, the defender takes one damage; otherwise the attacker takes one. Each damage removes the stack's lowest card; an Ace that takes damage falls with everything inside it. King and Ace rolls of 20 and 1 change the damage as written above. The dice box shows the attack total on the left and the defense total on the right, like 12>1.
  • Cards are placed from the hand into your own two lines: on an empty tile, or onto your own stack if the stack stays legal. Aces go only on empty backline tiles; Jokers spawn onto your stack holding a card of the same color.
  • Each stack moves once per turn, one tile straight. A group made only of Jacks moves two tiles straight or one diagonal. You can split a stack by choosing which cards move.
  • Stacks attack an enemy stack next to them (not diagonal, except Jack-only stacks). Aces and a King standing alone don't attack.
  • Paying mana spends your smallest souls first; any mana overpaid is lost, and spent souls go to the grave. Sacrifices happen before any mana is spent, and the Grave Blessing must come before spending too (Ritual Dice excepted).
  • A Joker's captured souls hold six mana at most. Double Edged Sword has no listed cost, so it costs 1 mana here.
  • The Ether is shared. It holds every special the players do not start with, the unused half of the deck in a one-deck Survival game, and the whole third deck in a three-deck game. A conjure takes a random soul of that kind from it, and Flesh Severance sends exiled souls back into it. A conjure whose kind is not in the Ether cannot be cast. Starting mana takes soldier souls from it; when it has none, new soldier souls are made.
  • Overdrawing counts every card burned by a full hand, including draws forced by Forced Hand, but not the opening hand. At the end of each of your turns six of the count are used up and the rest carries on.
  • Resurrection brings the card into your hand. Three of Swords destroys cards into the grave (or the Outer Darkness under a Blood Moon). Naked Hand lasts until the caster's turn ends.
  • Hypostasis lasts through the enemy's next two turns and ends at the start of your second turn after casting. A stack under it can't be targeted by attacks or by the Full Moon's toll.
  • The moon moves one phase each round, at the start of the first player's turn. Its New Moon gift and Full Moon toll reach both players at the start of the round, the first player first; the moon's prices, draw ban and Blood Moon last the whole round, and the spell prices apply to whoever casts that turn.
  • The Full Moon's most powerful stack is the one whose best card has the highest point rating (its value plus the top of its die), with more cards breaking ties. A sacrificed stack's souls go to the soul pile up to six mana; the rest go to the grave.
  • Not yet in the digital table: the Queen's protection from being surrounded, and placing same-suit cards inside a defending King. A King can't be sacrificed from a stack that holds other cards.

Game types and rule options

Local

Two to four players on one device; any seat can be a computer. Each picks a Substance (see Substances below), a color, and a platonic solid as a symbol. The symbol marks the player's cards everywhere in the player's color, and the player's deck bar is drawn in that color, one block for every card in the deck, with a brighter overlay at its left end when the deck grows past its starting size. The mana bar is a deep indigo, one block for each mana.

Versus Computer

One to three coded opponents, not a language model, at three difficulties each, one on one, free-for-all, or two against two with a computer as your teammate. Each turn it chooses between offense and defense from the field and the decks: defense bunkers (Kings with Queens, Aces held back, Hypostasis over a threatened stack); offense surrounds enemy stacks and casts curses. Easy plays an easier version of this, Medium is balanced, and Hard goes all out on your field and your deck, plays the Joker for souls, stacks Aces for the Grave Blessing and keeps its own deck high.

Survival

A tower defense for one to four players, always played with the midline. Two survivors play on a 6 by 5 field, three on 9 by 6 and four on 8 by 6 by default (the width and height can be changed), splitting the bottom two lines between them; the Horde spawns once for each survivor still standing, and the survivors take their turns one after another on the device. You open with an Ace and its Jack; the Horde takes the first turn. Each round the Horde spawns a group from a fresh deck on its two lines, stacking where it can, then moves on you and attacks the stacks that threaten it most. It has no spells, hand, deck or grave: its dead vanish, unless your Joker captures them. Spells that only touch a hand or a deck (Forced Hand, Double Edged Sword, Three of Swords, Naked Hand) are off. The moon still turns and Blood Moons still rise. When a Horde Joker shoots itself, the cards it captured from you go to the Outer Darkness and you lose 1 mana. You lose when your last card on the field falls, and the round count, in Roman numerals, is your score.

Substances

A Substance is what you play as. Each one changes your deck, your spells or your limits. Random picks one for you when the game starts.

  • Beginner: eight starting cards (six drawn, plus a second Ace and a Joker), eight mana at most and an eight-card hand, and three Grunts already standing as one stack on the center of your frontline (or just left or right of it). Please Don't Hurt Me (Enchantment, free, once a game): none of your stacks can be attacked until your next turn begins; your cards turn pink and everyone is told.
  • Angel Machine: Antony's Substance, always Bone white with the cracked mask (no one else can pick Bone). Three of Swords costs 1 mana. You earn a bolt for every card you lose while defending against an enemy's attack, and for every card Antony kills. With three bolts, Lightning Strike (Sorcery, free) immobilizes an enemy stack for 1d2 + 1d2 − 1 of its turns: a blue bolt with the count sits on it. The deck holds Samson, the mech suit: two hearts, defense 12; one step straight or one or two diagonal; the Slugshot Cannon (3d20, at an enemy two tiles away in a straight line or one diagonal, and from two away a miss costs him nothing), the Arm Blade (12 + 3d8, up close) or the Mech Slam (16 + 1d4, up close). When Samson falls, everyone is told that the angel Antony was sharing his form, lodged in his heart, and has raised his corpse with himself: Antony takes his tile, with two hearts, 12 + 6d4 on attack, 12 on defense, and Samson's movement. Samson wanders through Limbo and Antony, when he falls, goes missing: both show up as tabs (Limbo, Unknown) inside the Outer Darkness, and neither returns. Neither stacks with anything but an Ace.
  • Satan: always his very dark crimson with the claw. The Satan card: three hearts, 6d12 on attack and on defense with no modifiers (no moon bonus, no odds, no Hate), never stacks, and falls into the Outer Darkness, where it statics red. Hate (Curse, 3 mana): an enemy stack's strongest card turns red; while it lives, its stack has disadvantage on every attack and defense, and everyone attacking it has advantage.
  • Binah: three Slavemasters in the deck (8 + 1d8, stacking like an Infantry). Enemy soldiers killed in a Slavemaster's fights become your thralls, ten at most. Slaves (Necromancy, free) puts a thrall down on an empty tile of your ground as a Slave: a rifle of 10 + 1d12 at an enemy two tiles away in a straight line or one diagonal (from two away a miss costs it nothing), one straight step a turn, no defense, and it stands alone. When a Slave dies it goes to the grave as the soldier it was. Binah's Jokers add 3 to their attacks.
  • Jester Fool: a seven-card hand and seven mana at most. Every soldier in the deck is a Puppet (a 6 Grunt). The Tree of Neither Life Or Death starts in your hand: put it on an empty tile of your ground. It has three hearts and cannot defend; it holds up to six cards like an Ace, but never Kings or tens; it gives 2 mana at the start of each of your turns and grows a free Puppet inside it every two rounds; it falls into the Outer Darkness with every card in it. BullGoat (Conjuring, 3 mana) brings a BullGoat into your hand: two hearts, defense 10, 10 + 1d20 up close, or a charge of 10 + 2d20 at an enemy two or three tiles away in a straight, empty line that carries it up to its target, even in the attack phase. Deck of Cards (Conjuring, 6 mana, once a game, while the Tree stands) makes your deck full again as it was at the start; the cards left in it go to the grave and your hand stays.
  • Tiphareth of Kether: the Northern tribe of the Euclids, with a symbol of your choice. Three Traffickers in the deck (8 + 1d8, stacking like an Infantry) chain any enemy card that dies on the eight tiles around them, three prisoners each, wildcards included. Life for Sell (Sorcery, free) puts a prisoner up at the Auction, which opens beside the Outer Darkness: any player can offer mana or cards from the hand, and the sale goes through only if you approve it; the buyer takes the card into the hand. The Blindfold (Enchantment, 2 mana, while you are under a Delusion): when the Delusion ends you keep it three more turns, blind but with nine mana and the free dice, and without anything Lucifer paid to stifle it. Immune to the Leviathan's toll. Jokers add 1 to their attacks.
  • Lucifer & Ouroboros: always the ink and scroll; the color is yours. Delusion of Ouroboros (Sorcery, 1d2 mana, rolled each round): for 2d2 of your turns you and your teammates can't see enemy cards beyond your own cards' reach (the tiles around them, and two tiles away in a straight line), and meanwhile your mana can rise to nine and you get 3d2 free mana at the start of each turn. Delusion of Lucifer (Curse, 6 mana, free in a round you cast Ouroboros on yourself): the same Delusion, with the same blindness and the same gifts, on an enemy; then you can stifle it, paying 2 mana or 2 random cards from your deck for each maximum mana or free die you take from them while it lasts. Deconjured (Curse, 4 mana): no one can conjure during the next Full Moon. Pleasure and Joy (Curse, 3 mana): until your next turn your cards glow in your color, and those that are killed go back into your deck instead of the grave.
  • The Leviathan: color and symbol are yours. The Leviathan card: three hearts, 6d4 + 1d2 + 6 on attack and defense, no stacking and no Queen bonus; what it kills falls into the Outer Darkness, and so does it when it dies. Rise to the Moon (Sorcery, free, only at the New Moon while it is on the field) lifts it onto the moon, with the serpent's words in a box and then “Spit the empty full into”: from the next New Moon until it is dead, every enemy pays 2d4 random deck cards, two random hand cards and one mana into the Outer Darkness at each New Moon (Tiphareth of Kether is immune). On the moon your mana cap rises by one for every card in the Outer Darkness, up to twelve. Any enemy unit can attack it there, only at the New Moon (select the stack, then click the moon or the attack button), and it defends with advantage. Antony's lightning needs no roll against it and takes one heart; if lightning takes the last one, the box tells of the twelve strikes. Exile: the Outer Darkness (Necromancy, free): a Joker of yours sends the souls it captured into the Outer Darkness.
  • Yaldabaoth: always the symbol of Saturn; the color is yours. Night's Judgement (Sorcery, free, at the New Moon or the Full Moon): he eats the moon; it leaves the sky and its phases stop. A Leviathan on it goes back into its player's deck. Take 3 mana, or count the moon as three cards toward the hunger. God Hunger (Necromancy, 1d2 mana, twice a turn): eat 1d2 cards from the grave, your own deck, the selected stack on the field (not cards with hearts, Aces or the Tree), or an enemy's deck; for an enemy's deck they roll 1d4 against yours, and if you don't beat it you lose 2 mana to them. Each time, take the eaten as mana, or feed the hunger (the eaten fall into the Outer Darkness). At seven, Regurgitate Saturn (free): Saturn takes the moon's place, and every enemy, at the start of each turn, pays 1 (a random hand card, the top deck card or 1 mana) into your soul pile, or refuses and a Debt Collector rises on your ground. A Debt Collector copies the leading card of whatever it fights, without its advantage, and each one still standing at a new round raises the price by 1 for everyone. Binah never pays.

Substance cards with hearts lose one heart each time they take damage and fall at none. They can't be sacrificed, and they never lie in the grave. One-time spells say so when they are cast. Big moments come up as a box; smaller ones as a short notice across the top of the screen in the color of the player they belong to.

More players

  • Free-for-all (3 or 4 players): 7 by 7 by default, or any square from 5 to 9, with the corners cut by default. Each player spawns and raises Aces only on their own edge. The last player standing wins.
  • Two against two (4 players): side by side (teammates on neighboring edges of a square field), across (teammates on opposite edges), or same side (6 by 6 by default, 6 to 8 wide and 4 to 6 tall; each team holds one end and each player half its columns, like one on one). Each player chooses Side A or Side B in their setup; each side needs two players. Teammates can't attack each other. A team wins when both enemies have fallen.
  • Decks: free-for-all players take a whole deck each (default) or half a deck (one red and one black suit). A team splits one deck, two suits and one Joker to each teammate; with a shared team deck teammates draw from one deck and spend from one soul pile but keep separate hands.
  • When a player falls: their deck and hand go to the grave and the grave is shuffled (default), or they go to the Outer Darkness. A shared team deck stays with the teammate still standing.
  • Turn order: classic (default for Local), draw and prep together with attacks in turns (default against the computer and in single-player Survival), or every phase together. A phase played together ends when every player is Ready. More than one person on one device always plays classic.
  • Phase timer (off by default): 30, 60 or 90 seconds a phase; under 60 and 90 the draw phase gets 30. When it runs out the game moves to the next phase, and during setup it places your cards the way a Medium computer would.
  • Computer speed: slow, normal (default) or fast.

Rule options

  • Blood Moon mode: the game begins on a Blood Moon. Each player's deck is shuffled before two thirds of it go to the graveyard, and each player starts with one Joker in hand.
  • Battle map: no map (plain tiles) by default, Random (one of the four regular maps), or chosen: Keep (a stone keep on your backline, a graveyard on the midline, the enemy's barracks), Sahel (red laterite and dry grass of the far north at night, with mud-brick walls and thatched huts), Forest, Dungeon (no castle or barracks), or the special maps: River and no map. Every map is at night. The art is drawn under the tiles and shows through the gaps between them.
  • River: a wide brown river like the Ogun near Lagos runs along the midline. It can only be crossed on a bridge two tiles wide, on the left or the right. The River map is always 7 wide and 5 tall and can't cut its corners. It isn't offered in Survival.
  • Field size: one on one is 5 (default), 6 or 7 tiles wide, with the midline on (default) or off and the corners cut or kept. Free-for-all and the edge 2v2 layouts are a square from 5 to 9 (7 by default); 2v2 on the same side and co-op Survival pick a width and a height. On a phone, 5 wide is the largest size that stays easy to read.
  • Display: the hand can sit under your deck or beside the field; the selected-stack box above or below the spells; the turn buttons in a bar right under the field (default) or at the top. At the bottom of the screen you can change the card icon size, the interface size, the text size (words only), the hand position, the tile names (off by default; columns A, B, C from the left and rows 1, 2, 3 from the bottom, like a chessboard), the page width on a computer (standard or full width) and the theme.
  • Log: every action is listed with the time to the second on your computer, colored in the color of the player who took it, with the tiles it happened on.
  • Phase timer off: while a timer is running, the quote box has a button that switches it off for the rest of the game.
  • Dice box history: each dice box notes the player's last spell, the tile it was cast on and the time. Click a dice box to open the history of every player's rolls and spells, with tiles and the time on your computer; click anywhere else or press Escape to close it.
  • Headquarters (Local and Versus Computer): each side opens with two Aces as bases, the first holding its Jack, and troops can only be spawned inside your own Aces. New Aces may still be raised on the backline and become bases too. The only ways to win are destroying every enemy base or an economic victory; losing every other card on the field doesn't end the game.
  • Midline: on by default; can be removed outside Survival.
  • Decks: one deck between the two players (each takes one red and one black suit, dealt at random or chosen), two decks (default; a full deck each), or three decks (a full deck each and a whole third deck waiting in the Ether).
  • Specials (Jacks, Queens, Kings, Aces and Jokers): none besides the opening Ace and its Jack (default), half (the specials of half your suits), or all. Every special a player does not start with waits in the Ether, ready to be conjured.
  • At least one Joker (on by default): every player's deck holds at least one Joker, taken from the Ether when the specials setting would leave none.
  • Starting mana: each player starts with souls in their soul pile, taken from the Ether as soldier souls (or newly made when the Ether has none). Default 3d2; it can be 1d6, a fixed number, or none. With one deck each player rolls their own by default; with two or three decks one roll sets the same starting mana for everyone by default. Either can be switched.
  • Moon combat bonus (on by default): at the Full Moon every attack adds 1d2 to its score; at the New Moon every defense adds 1d2.
  • Spells in the attack phase (off by default): enchantments, curses and sorcery can also be cast during the attack phase. Necromancy, conjuring and sacrifices stay in the prep phase.
  • Optional Ace rule: the cards inside an Ace must match its color.
