Chapter 46

Hide & Seek

Held by her arms while lying out on the cold floor, tasting her tears, Christopher speaks in a quivering cry, “I don’t want this to end, like it was just a dream.”

The ground growls a little, like the shaking of the foundations of the world. Christopher looks up, where the sight of an angelic murderer watches deep into his iris, piercing deeper than the soul, as if looking for a secret behind it. The first time Christopher ever hears it, Antony speaks like a man-made strength, a gravel sound father instigating what is within, spoken in calm certainty like a primordial hunt: “I know you are in there…”

Christopher jumps like an electrified corpse jolt, blood coming out of his eyes now, flailing, torturing loudly while in crawl, through and over the blank canvas stone of floor. He screams like blood curdle, like family murdered in the dark.

Antony roars back in fatherly grit and grate, as Alice screams in a full voice pitch pierce highly. From the same form, they speak to him together: “Christopher!” He rolls over and through the floor like eight legs broken in stumbling claw crawls, gagging words with terror horns from his mouth, climbing to escape the soul death. Alice says to him again, “Christopher!”

Christopher pauses as the blood stops rolling for a moment, and he sees her. Now his tears are normal, washing the blood away, and his eyes water profusely with his pale and still frown. He speaks like a petrified child riddled in shellshock: “What if I can’t get out?” He pouts like a boy, his face staring off a little distant… Eventually, he looks missing, and she can see it.

Alice tries to reassure him with a cradle voice for the blood sobbing man, “I have you, you are with me now. I found you. Hey, it is going to be okay.” When she says it all to Christopher, she waits for anything, anything at all, even a slight, but there is only a muted gaze. She says again, though a little too quiet from the coldness that stole him, “You are with me…” She doesn’t believe the words, but only goes through the motion of comforting without convince.

Christopher begins a slow pull below the cheeks, the look not his own. As the mouth hikes up a little with the muscles, up and up with a squirm, they end in the expression of amusement, before mouthing the words silently, “Born again…” His smile stretches small and blank like replica.

Alice asks him, and quietly in what feels like a half-minute-long moment, in a head spoken voice over the top of breathless lungs, “Where did you go?” He keeps staring, as the sounds of the Angel machine ratchet and crank with mechanical works, clicks and pumps accelerating. She looks for Christopher with a word spoken still like a tired pond, “Hey.” Nothing said from the cadaver smile, as some kind of evidence show of absence.

The lights flicker like a flower wilting into rotting deflowerment, and sparks fly with sputters and ticks in cobalt colored streaks over the carcass pupils, where the trace of shine is consumed by their pitch—dim. Bloody tears come out faintly, and the machine baby cries at the sight of the machine father, before a pounding machination is heard in weight drops not seen in the dark.

Alice looks around with neck cramped pulls of the head jerks, wired with the intoxication of a vagus jump start, nervous in drag search race. Nothing. Electric embers flashing blue. She tries to slam the brakes at the eyes, and the gears send it into a rapid crankshaft shake, firing them dead center with difficulty at the one point. Where the eyes struggle to sit still like restless legs, she sees it: Christopher is gone. The lights come back on all at once, as if to make the certainty more clear, and he lies spilled still in red like himself.

Alice is convinced he is dead, but there it is again, as earlier, those flails slamming up and down like a defibrillator shocking Christopher in human toss. He flicks with alarm gash, and he says it, the words told like someone’s convulsence: “I spit folly like pride! I eat the swallow of thirteen eternities long! Repeating itself! Repeating itself! I repeat!”

Alice yells in voice made splice and splinter, throwing it out: “‘Alice shrieks,’ wait, why did I say that? ‘Alice asks herself.’” She turns like spins, pale in gut gruesome. The word she spoke was many in one, like a cursed abracadabra.

Christopher says the squirming words through his burning throat, smelling like sand traced vile from his mouth no longer smiling: “Someone is watching us…” He heaves a little, like a sick dog, heaving harder still, like a tip on the tongue choked backwards, before heaving his hardest with stomach choke until hurling the blasphemy out of him. A tail long slips out his lips in tentacles that start feeling around about like newfound blindness. He suffocates on the word, before it slides out wet like a catheter ripped out by yanking force. The serpent crawls up to Alice, as she shrinks into her head while a pool of shadow covers her.

Alice lies on the hospital bed, seeing the serpent now by the left of her shoulder, a déjà vu flashing the soul red-hot like a brand of curse. It slithers closer and closer, sliding in its winding path till its split tongue meets the ear with a cold and wet voice like devil: “You are the hole of seed, the seed drops into you, and like God, it is fatherless hate.”

No heart shake, no word, nothing. She stares at the ceiling, pissing away like a tube’s relief, and drifting away like Columbus.

The page turns away from Alice. In the other room, Lucifer is still writing. Lucifer, still inside the room with his book, sees the image of the angel beyond the iris, and he crams his nose into it, getting covered in sweat to fight him. He darts his write in rapids, but the current is dry, without ink, as if powerless now. Lucifer drops with his gut, his head and drench wet falling into the book. He says with mouthing, without word, “No…” He lifts his head again, letting the hand hurdle wildly with jolts of letters, but no word, not a single word. His eyes rip open, prying wetly, and his eyebrows are soaked rags. The blank expression of Lucifer is as notes without language, and he aches in the bones at the now of it. “I need one word, just one.” He raises the eyes over up, past the obstruction of his own face, and his eye meets the angel again at his third view. He sees it: the brother has left the home. Like a lamb to the slaughter, he cries his blood offering, saying, “I am the same as the snake, so it won’t be long.”

The deep rasp calls from above his second face, “Lucifer!” The death rattle sounds with a tail, and the thoughts are stuffed in standoffish speed with the pricking hairs on end.

Lucifer drops the writing, the same as he did forever ago, over and over, smelling like a déjà vu seizure coming on like yellow. He knows the moment for the amount of times more numbered than age. He drops dead like a puppet. His strings look cut like his tether to Christopher, where the word which found him was sewn in his undone stitch, foretold like dice and cards. He lies like ruin, where his blood runs out the white of goreshot eyes, ripping out red words over the pages he fell into with pause. His wrists and ankles become corpsely swollen like sweet scents lived, while his dead beating heart pumps the secretion of iron fluid. Its metallic trickle authors about those smells huffed in his dead mother’s home. He was born into an end, aborted by lineage, and then adopted by an unwilling foster cocoon who he was born out of, after his death. A manger paper coffin holds his first and last writing before rebirth, where his promise waits until the boy is made twice as he.

The pages leave Lucifer where he fell. Back in the stone chamber, it is not done. The snake chases away from itself, swallowing their way into themselves, summoning themselves in from the folly puked out from the hardened heart that never made promise to the angel machine. It comes out of him before taking her in, Alice. In the fall of the serpent’s throat, she is on the way to the belly of another beast after regurgitation. Alice tumbles and rolls across her back, skipping like a rock across water, her river casket underground. She is carried away by the stream, into the scornful tragedy of slapstick weight. Antony hangs alone on the wall, his soul in his mechanistic husk prison, its cell inside of the exile of the Tartarus complex.
