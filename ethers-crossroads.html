Getting Started

Ether's Crossroads is a card game for two players. Each player brings a deck of playing cards with both Jokers, and the table needs a handful of dice: d2, d4, d6, d8, d12 and d20. Cards are soldiers, knights and royalty placed on a field of lines; dice decide every fight; the dead become souls that can be spent as mana on spells, or raised again from the shared grave; and the moon turns one phase every round.

  1. Raise your Ace. Each player takes one Ace and one Jack from their own deck (or from the Ether, when the specials are set to wait there). The Ace goes on any tile of your spawn lines you choose, with the Jack inside it.
  2. Draw. Each player draws 2d4 cards from their own shuffled deck. A hand holds six cards at most.
  3. Roll for the opening. Both players roll. The highest roll sets up first, placing any opening cards on their own two lines, then the other player sets up. The highest roller also takes the first turn.
  4. Take turns. Every turn runs Prep → Attack. The prep phase starts with drawing: roll a d4 as many times as you like and draw that many cards. Then place, move and sacrifice cards and cast spells. In the attack phase, each of your stacks may attack once. The very first turn of the game has no draw phase.
  5. Fight with dice. A stack fights with the score of its best card: the card's value plus its die. Stacking cards does not add their scores together, but every card in a stack is another hit it can take.
  6. Gather souls. Sacrificed cards become your soul pile, worth up to six mana (a card from your hand is worth its Ether price − 1d2 + 1, rolled as you sacrifice it, but a card that costs 1 is always worth 1; a card on the field is worth its soul), which pays for spells such as Divine Odds, Clairvoyance or a King Conjure. Once a turn, the free Grave Blessing lets you roll for the dead.
  7. Watch the moon. The New Moon gives back cards from the grave; the Full Moon takes a stack of two or a card, or 2d2 + 2 mana. Rarely, the Full Moon turns to a Blood Moon.
  8. Win. Destroy every card the other player has on the field, or leave them with no deck, no hand and no soul pile when their turn ends: an economic victory.

The full rules, including every card's role, the stacking formula, all the spells, the moon and its events, are under All Rules. Play Game runs the game in the browser.

Game Modes

Standard

Local play: against computers, together on this device, or both. Up to four people share one device, and every other seat is a computer: you against one to three computers, friends together against computers, or friends against each other, up to eight seats in King of the Hill. Each player picks an Emanation (or the host turns Emanations off), a color and a symbol, which marks every card they own. With more than one person, the board turns to face whoever is playing, and the screen goes dark between turns until the next player confirms they hold the device. Everyone sees every die.

The computers

The computers are written as plain code rather than a language model. Each turn the computer weighs offense against defense from the field and the decks. Easy plays loosely and makes mistakes. Medium is balanced and a real fight. Hard plays the Joker for souls, stacks Aces for the Grave Blessing, keeps its own deck full and eats away at yours.

Survival

Tower defense for one to four players. You raise your Ace and its Jack, and the Horde comes: after three rounds of grace, a new wave every 1d2 + 2 rounds from a fresh deck, each one a step stronger, with Jacks, Queens and Kings from the sixth wave and Jokers from the eleventh; from then on it brings counter cards (Battering Rams for your Walls) when you lean on something it has no answer to. The Horde has no spells, hand or grave. The moon still turns. You lose when your last card on the field falls, and the round you reach is your score.

Emanations

An Emanation is what you play as. Each one changes your deck, your spells or your limits. Every Emanation in full.

More players, maps and options

With three or four players the field is a 7 by 7 square by default: free-for-all, or two against two with teammates side by side, across, or sharing one end. The battle maps are the Keep, the Sahel, the Forest, the Dungeon and the River, all at night. Before a game you can set the field size, the decks, the specials, the starting mana, the sky, the Bank, Demons, crystals, fog of war and more. Every option.
