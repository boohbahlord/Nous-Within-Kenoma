Getting Started

Ether's Crossroads is a card game for two players. Each player brings a deck of playing cards with both Jokers, and the table needs a handful of dice: d2, d4, d6, d8, d12 and d20. Cards are soldiers, knights and royalty placed on a field of lines; dice decide every fight; the dead become souls that can be spent as mana on spells, or raised again from the shared grave; and the moon turns one phase with every turn.

  1. Raise your Ace. Each player takes one Ace and one Jack from their own deck. The Ace goes on any tile of your backline you choose, with the Jack inside it.
  2. Draw. Each player draws 2d4 cards from their own shuffled deck. A hand holds six cards at most.
  3. Roll for the opening. Both players roll. The highest roll sets up first, placing any opening cards on their own two lines, then the other player sets up. The highest roller also takes the first turn.
  4. Take turns. Every turn runs Optional Draw → Prep → Attack. In the draw phase, roll a d4 as many times as you like and draw that many cards. In the prep phase, place, move and sacrifice cards and cast spells. In the attack phase, each of your stacks may attack once. The very first turn of the game has no draw phase.
  5. Fight with dice. A stack fights with the score of its best card: the card's value plus its die. Stacking cards does not add their scores together, but every card in a stack is another hit it can take.
  6. Gather souls. Sacrificed cards become your soul pile, worth up to six mana, which pays for spells such as Divine Odds, Clairvoyance or a King Conjure. Once a turn, the free Grave Blessing lets you roll for the dead.
  7. Watch the moon. The New Moon gives back cards from the grave; the Full Moon takes your strongest stack or a heavy toll. Rarely, a Blood Moon, Leviathan or the Shadow Woman appear.
  8. Win. Destroy every card the other player has on the field, or leave them with no deck, no hand and no soul pile when their turn ends: an economic victory.

The full rules, including every card's role, the stacking formula, all the spells, the moon and its events, are under All Rules. Play Game runs the game in the browser.

Game Modes

Local 1v1

Two players share one device. Each player picks a tribe of the Euclids, Binah or Tiphareth, a color, and one of the five platonic solids as a symbol; the symbol marks every card that player owns, in their color, on the field, in the hand and even in the Outer Darkness. The board turns to face whoever is playing. When a turn ends the screen goes black and stays black until the next player confirms they are holding the device, so no one sees the other's hand while it changes hands. Both players see every die the other rolls.

Versus Computer

You play against an opponent written in plain code, not a language model. Every turn it weighs offense against defense from the state of the field and the decks. On defense it bunkers down: Kings with Queens, Aces held back, Hypostasis over a threatened stack. On offense it pushes forward, surrounds your stacks with several of its own and casts curses like Forced Hand and Three of Swords. Easy plays loosely, misses chances and makes mistakes. Medium balances everything and is a real fight. Hard goes all out: it plays the Joker for souls, stacks Aces to roll more Grave Blessing dice, keeps its own deck full and eats away at your field and your deck.

Survival

A single-player tower defense. You raise your Ace and its Jack, and the Horde comes. Every round the Horde spawns a new group from a fresh deck on its side of the field, stacking its cards where it can, then marches at you and attacks the stacks that threaten it most. It has no spells, no hand and no grave. Its numbers grow every round and its cards grow stronger every five: soldiers first, then Jacks, Queens and Kings from round six, and Jokers from round eleven, which capture your souls and, when they shoot themselves, cast those cards into the Outer Darkness and take a mana from you. The moon still turns, and Blood Moons still rise, but Leviathan and the Shadow Woman stay only three turns. The round is counted in Roman numerals at the top of the screen. You lose when your last card on the field falls.

Rule options

Insanity, not for beginners, makes the moon's events far more likely at every Full Moon (Blood Moon 10%, Leviathan 5%, Shadow Woman 5%) and starts each player with a Joker from the Ether in hand. Blood Moon mode opens the game under a Blood Moon: each deck is shuffled, two thirds of it go to the grave, and each player starts with a Joker in hand. The midline can be switched off outside Survival, and the optional Ace rule locks the cards inside an Ace to its color. The rarity of every event is listed at the bottom of All Rules.
