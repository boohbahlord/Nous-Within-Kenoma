# Ether's Crossroads: a night's worth of ideas

Written 2026-10-20 while you slept. Nothing here is built. It's a well to draw from: some ideas are small, some would
take a season, and a few are deliberately strange. Everything tries to grow out of what the game and the book already
are: the Substances, the Moon, the Bank, the Ether, the Outer Darkness and Limbo, and the beings and places in the codex.

Each idea has a short name so you can point at it ("do S3 and M7"). Where I got carried away, I let myself.

## 1. New Substances from the book (S)

The Substances are the heart of the game: each one bends the rules in its own direction. The book has many beings that
aren't playable yet, and some of them already have exactly the shape a Substance needs.

### S1. Sophia, Wisdom

Sophia is Yaldabaoth's mother, the form of Wisdom, the one who made the mistake the whole Kenoma rests on. As a
Substance she should be about **knowing, and the price of knowing**.

- **Color and symbol:** a pale gold with a seven-pointed star, or an eye inside a teardrop.
- **Passive: Foresight.** At the start of each of her turns she sees the top card of every player's deck. Not the hand,
  just the next card each player will draw. It changes how she reads the board: she knows the enemy's next draw is a
  King, so she holds back.
- **Wildcard: The Aeon.** A card with no attack and no defense of its own. While it stands on the field, every friendly
  stack within two tiles rolls one extra die on attack. It can't be captured; when it dies, it doesn't go to the grave or
  the Outer Darkness but "returns to the Fullness": it is gone from the game, and Sophia's player gains 1 maximum mana
  permanently. Losing it hurts and helps at once, like the myth.
- **Spell: Error.** Sophia's signature curse, the mistake that made the world. Choose an enemy spell they cast last turn:
  the next time they cast it, it resolves as though cast by you. Costly (4 or 5 mana), once per game.
- **Spell: The Lower Wisdom.** For one round, every player plays with their hand face up. Everyone, including Sophia.
- **Weakness:** Sophia can't use necromancy at all (Grave Blessing raises nothing for her). Wisdom doesn't raise the
  dead; it remembers them. Instead, each card in the grave from her own deck gives her a small bonus to Foresight (see
  two cards deep instead of one once five of hers lie there).
- **Interaction with Yaldabaoth:** if Sophia and Yaldabaoth are both in a game, Yaldabaoth's Night's Judgement can't eat
  the moon on a turn after Sophia cast a spell. And if Yaldabaoth's hunger reaches seven, Sophia's player may pay 3 mana
  to "weep", cancelling Saturn's arrival once. The mother can stop the son, but only once.

### S2. The Clowns, and the Fool's Circus

The Jester Fool already exists, but the Circus Tent under the rabbit hole, the Clowns' Room and the clowns who take a
paralyzed man are their own nightmare. The Circus as a Substance would be **a performance that the other players are
forced to watch and take part in**.

- **Color:** striped red and white, the symbol a pierced balloon or a tent.
- **Passive: The Show Must Go On.** Every time the Circus wins a fight, the loser's owner must pay 1 mana "for the
  ticket" or discard a card from hand. Crowds pay to see.
- **Wildcard: The Ringmaster.** A unit with a whip. Once per turn it can force an enemy stack within two tiles to move
  one tile in a direction of the Ringmaster's choosing (the ring forms around it). It can't push a stack into a mountain
  or onto another stack; pinned or base stacks can't be moved.
- **Wildcard: The Stretcher.** Carried by two Clowns (it needs two soldiers in its stack to move). When an enemy card
  dies next to it, instead of going to the grave the card is "taken to the Clowns' Room": the Circus player holds it
  face down. The Circus can play it later as their own, but it plays at −2 because it's paralyzed. The "paralyzed man"
  from the book becomes a capture mechanic.
- **Spell: Applause.** Every player claps or doesn't: each player secretly chooses to pay 1 mana or not. If all pay, the
  Circus gets nothing; if any don't, the Circus gains 2 mana for each who didn't. A tiny bluffing game inside the game.
- **Spell: The Act.** Choose two enemy stacks; they swap places. If they belong to different players and either is now
  beside an enemy, the two players fight each other immediately (no one chose it; the crowd wanted it).
- **Weakness:** the Circus has no Aces of its own beyond the opening one. Its "base" is the Tent: a 2x2 structure
  (taking four tiles) that it pitches once per game. While the Tent stands, Circus cards inside it can't be attacked,
  but the Tent itself can be, and it has many hearts.

### S3. The Wired Angels

Angels of metal, wires and drives: the cold machinery of the demiurge. Where Antony's Angel Machine is about a heart
inside a machine, the Wired Angels should be **a network**.

- **Color:** cold steel blue, with a circuit or a wired halo for the symbol.
- **Passive: The Network.** Every Wired card that stands in a straight line (row or column) with another Wired card,
  with nothing between them, is "linked". A linked card's defense is shared: when one is attacked, the defender rolls
  as though the linked stack's best card were also there. Draw the links as thin glowing wires on the field.
- **Wildcard: The Relay.** Has no attack. Extends links through itself diagonally, and lets the Wired player cast any
  targeted spell as though they stood at the Relay's tile (range from the Relay).
- **Wildcard: The Drive.** A "hard drive" angel: when a Wired card dies, the Drive saves it. Up to three dead cards are
  stored "on the Drive" instead of the grave, and the Wired player can restore one to their hand for 2 mana. If the
  Drive dies, everything on it is lost to the Outer Darkness.
- **Spell: Overclock.** One linked group attacks twice this turn; afterward each card in it takes a heart of damage
  (or, if it has no hearts, rolls 1d4 and dies on a 1). Burnout.
- **Weakness:** Rain (the Firmament's spell, and any rain) shorts the network. While it rains, there are no links.

### S4. The Woman with Crab Claws, the Weaver on the Moon

"A woman of shadow who stands on the moon and weaves." She's already half a mechanic: the moon is a place in the game,
and the Leviathan rises to it. She should be **a Substance of threads and fate**.

- **Color:** shadow violet with silver threads; a spindle or a crab claw for the symbol.
- **Passive: The Loom.** At every New Moon she weaves one thread: she chooses any two tiles on the field and ties them.
  Whatever stands on one tied tile feels what happens to the other: damage dealt to one is also dealt to the other,
  and a buff on one is a buff on both. Threads last until the next New Moon. She can tie an enemy stack to her own
  wall, or an enemy King to an enemy Grunt, so that killing the Grunt hurts the King.
- **Wildcard: The Claw.** Cuts threads, including the Fates' own. Once per game the Claw can cut "a thread of fate":
  cancel one roll of the dice that has just been made (any player's) and have it rolled again.
- **Interaction with the Leviathan:** if the Leviathan rises to the moon in a game with the Weaver, the two fight for
  the moon. The Weaver's player rolls 1d20 against the Leviathan's; whoever wins keeps the moon. If the Weaver holds it,
  the Leviathan's New Moon tax is paid to her instead.
- **Spell: Unravel.** Remove all threads, including other effects that "tie" things (Hypostasis shields, Quiet Eyes, the
  Pin of the Goatbull): one big reset.
- **Weakness:** she has no soldiers in her deck at all: only Specials and her own cards. She has to conjure or weave her
  army from the Ether, and her Ether conjuring costs 1 less.

### S5. Kōaḥ, the Soothsaying Voice of Dice

Kōaḥ is "the soothsaying voice of dice and numbers." The game is drenched in dice: d4 draws, d20 crits, 6d12 Satans,
Ritual Dice. A Substance that **manipulates dice themselves** would be fun and very readable, because every player
already sees dice rolled.

- **Color:** ivory and black like a die; the symbol a d20 seen from above.
- **Passive: Prophecy.** At the start of each turn Kōaḥ names a number from 1 to 6. Every time any die of that size (a
  d6) shows that number this round, Kōaḥ gains 1 mana. The more d6s fly, the better.
- **Wildcard: The Numberer.** A unit whose attack is "the sum of the last three dice rolled on the field by anyone."
  Its strength depends on what just happened. It shows the three dice on its card.
- **Spell: Loaded.** Choose one die size (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20). For one round, every die of that size rolled by
  anyone rolls twice and Kōaḥ chooses which result stands. Very strong; costs a lot (5 to 6) and can't be cast two
  rounds running.
- **Spell: Even and Odd.** Choose "even" or "odd". Until your next turn, every die that comes up the other way adds 1 to
  the next roll Kōaḥ makes. A slow build.
- **Weakness:** Kōaḥ never benefits from Divine Odds or Dice Plus (it's already the dice) and its Ace has no hearts (it
  falls on any lost fight). It is fragile but loud.

### S6. The Three Titans, Watchers of the First Moat

"Covered in eyes." The Titans should be **a Substance of watching**: vision, fog, and punishing those who move in sight.

- **Color:** dark bronze with many small eyes for the symbol.
- **Passive: Eyes Everywhere.** The Titans' player is never affected by fog of war beyond two tiles from their cards;
  instead of the usual sight, their cards see in a cross shape all the way to the edges of the field (the whole row and
  column). Fog is their enemy's problem.
- **Wildcards: The Three.** Exactly three Titan cards, huge (four hearts, big dice), very slow (they move only every
  other turn). Each is named: the Titan of Memory, the Titan of Fear, the Titan of Return. The Memory titan makes every
  card that dies beside it go to the grave instead of the Outer Darkness (even under a Blood Moon); Fear makes enemy
  stacks beside it attack at disadvantage; Return lets the Titans' player draw an extra card whenever an enemy stack
  moves out of its sight.
- **Passive: The Watch.** Any enemy stack that moves within a Titan's sight during the enemy's turn takes a "seen"
  marker. A seen stack can't ambush and can't be placed face down, until the next round.
- **Weakness:** the Titans have no spells of movement, and their deck is small (fewer, stronger cards). They are bad at
  King of the Hill (slow) and good at holding a line.

### S7. Curse, the Angel Who Refuses Redemption

"The one angel who refuses Redemption." Curse is the anti-angel. As a Substance: **refusal**.

- **Color:** ash grey with a cracked halo.
- **Passive: I Refuse.** Once per round, Curse's player can refuse one spell aimed at their cards: it simply doesn't
  happen, and the caster gets their mana back. The refusal is shown with a big "Refused" stamp over the spell name.
- **Wildcard: The Unredeemed.** A card that can't be healed, can't be shielded, can't be raised from the grave and
  can't be resurrected, even by its own player. In return it gets stronger every time it's offered something and
  refuses: each refusal adds 1 to its attack permanently.
- **Spell: Contagion of Refusal.** For one round, every player's Grave Blessing raises nothing. The whole table loses
  necromancy.
- **Weakness:** Curse can never take loans from the Bank. Its player is "beyond the Bank's redemption" as well.

### S8. Alice, the Painter

Alice paints the faces of her tormentors in the new chapter 44, "Art Talks Back; I Can't". A Substance built on
**painting the field itself**, and on the horror of what she paints coming alive.

- **Color:** acrylic, a different shade each game (a palette). The symbol is a brush.
- **Passive: The Canvas.** Each turn, Alice can paint one empty tile in one of three colors (crimson, blue or yellow).
  Painted tiles change the rules for whoever stands on them: crimson gives +2 attack and −2 defense; blue gives
  +2 defense and no movement; yellow means "the Yellow Room": a stack on it forgets (its odds, buffs and curses are
  cleared). Paint lasts until rain or until Alice paints over it.
- **Wildcards: The Dolls.** In chapter 44 the painted faces become dolls, mocking and towering. A Doll is created when
  Alice paints a face on one of her own cards (a spell, 2 mana): the card becomes a Doll, which can't attack but
  mocks: every enemy stack beside a Doll loses 1 from every roll. Dolls are the art talking back.
- **The cost:** every Doll on the field drains 1 mana from Alice at the start of each of her turns (they mock her too).
  If she can't pay, a Doll turns on her and attacks her nearest card. Art that talks back.
- **Spell: I Can't.** Removes every Doll from the field at once; each one becomes a "drained" card in her grave. She
  loses all her mana. It's her escape hatch, and it costs everything.

### S9. Christopher and the Maiden

"A man in the stone chamber, caught in the maiden." The stone chamber is a cruel place, and Christopher is caught in a
device. A Substance of **endurance, pain turned into strength, and imprisonment**.

- **Passive: Within the Maiden.** Christopher's Ace starts with an Iron Maiden around it: it takes no damage the first
  three times it is hit, but each hit adds a "spike". At three spikes the Maiden opens, and every enemy stack beside the
  Ace takes 1d4 damage.
- **Wildcard: The Raised Stone.** A base card that can't hold soldiers. It can hold one enemy card captured in a fight
  (the stone chamber): while the captive is held, its owner pays 1 mana a turn or the captive dies.
- **Spell: Love, Through the Wall.** If Alice is also in the game, Christopher and Alice can share mana: once per round,
  either can give the other up to 3 mana. The two lovers of the book find each other on the field.

### S10. Red and Blue, the Rose and the Gentian

"A crimson rose of a man" and "a trumpet gentian of a woman." A two-player-in-one Substance: **a pair**.

- **Mechanic:** the player has two Aces at the start, a Red Ace and a Blue Ace, and two decks shuffled together but
  marked red and blue. Red cards are stronger near the Blue Ace and vice versa. They grow toward each other.
- **Passive: Bloom.** When a Red card and a Blue card stand on adjacent tiles, they "bloom": both gain a heart, once.
- **Weakness:** if one Ace dies, the other wilts: it loses all its hearts but one.

### S11. The Hermit (who dies believing he is God)

A Substance for a single, isolated player who is **stronger the more alone they are**.

- **Passive: Solitude.** For each enemy player that has no stack within four tiles of the Hermit's Ace, he gains 1 mana a
  turn.
- **Wildcard: The Hermit himself.** Strong, but he must stand alone (no stacking). If he's ever surrounded by enemies on
  three sides, he "believes he is God": he doubles all his dice for one fight, then falls.
- **Weakness:** the Hermit can't make alliances in team games (he refuses to be on a side); he's only playable in
  free-for-all.

### S12. The Rabbit Mask (Thomas, Ana and the Rabbit-Masked Father)

The House of the Rabbit Mask, the stone tiles, the wasps' nests, the thorned window. A Substance of **masks and hidden
identities**.

- **Passive: Masks.** All of this Substance's cards are placed face down, always. They turn face up only when they fight.
  Its whole army is a mystery to the other players until contact.
- **Wildcard: The Wasp Nest.** A tile hazard: placed on an empty tile, it stings any stack that ends its move beside it
  (1 damage to the weakest card). It can be destroyed by any attack.
- **Spell: Wear the Mask.** Swap the faces of two of your own cards on the field: the King is now where the Grunt was,
  and no one else knows.

### S13. Damon and the Pills

Chapter 42 is brutal. A Substance about **addiction and withdrawal**, as a resource mechanic.

- **Resource: Pills.** Instead of (or alongside) mana, Damon has pills. A pill gives a big burst: +3 to every die for one
  fight, or two extra moves. Each pill taken raises "need" by 1. At the start of each turn, if Damon's need is higher than
  the pills he takes that turn, his cards shake: they roll at disadvantage. Taking a pill every turn keeps him strong
  and his need climbing. Stopping is the hardest thing in the game.
- This would be a difficult, uncomfortable Substance to play, and I think that's the point. It should probably be
  handled with care: no cartoonish "drug power-up", and the flavor text should come straight from the chapter.

### S14. The Golden Child (the Good Puppet with a pink television)

A Substance of **broadcasts**: the pink television shows things.

- **Passive: Broadcast.** Once per round, the Golden Child "broadcasts" one thing to everyone: a card from their hand is
  shown to all players. Whatever is broadcast costs 1 less to play for the Golden Child this turn (the audience wants it).
- **Wildcard: The Pink Television.** A base that shows any other player's view: while it stands, the Golden Child sees
  everything one chosen enemy sees (shared fog).

### S15. The Antichrist Doll

"A baby angel doll that weeps blood." Small, eerie. Could be a **single Wildcard** rather than a whole Substance: a doll
any player might get from a rare event. While it's in your hand, at the start of each of your turns it weeps: 1 card from
your deck goes to the grave. While it's on the field, enemy cards beside it can't be healed. It can't be sacrificed. To
be rid of it, you must give it to another player (spell: Gift the Doll).

## 2. New wildcards and units (W)

Not every idea needs a whole Substance. Some units could join the Ether for everyone.

- **W1. The Lantern Bearer.** A support card that carries light: fog around it is pushed back one tile further, and
  enemy face-down stacks within two tiles are shown. A soft counter to fog and ambush, for anyone. Cheap (2 mana).
- **W2. The Gravedigger.** A support card that stands on the field next to the grave... no: a card that, when on the
  field, lets its player pick the top card of the grave instead of drawing (once per turn). A slow, deliberate necro
  support.
- **W3. The Standard Bearer.** A flag on a pole. Friendly stacks within one tile attack with +1. If it's captured, the
  captor gets the bonus instead. Thematically close to the Claimer's flags.
- **W4. The Bell Ringer.** When it rings (once per game), every player's next turn is skipped... no, too strong. When it
  rings, every stack on the field that has moved this turn must stop. Interrupts rushes.
- **W5. The Ferryman.** Crosses the Scalding River (see M1): the only unit that can carry a stack across water tiles.
  On the River map it would change everything.
- **W6. The Mirror.** A support card: when an enemy spell targets a stack with a Mirror, roll 1d2; on a 2 the spell is
  reflected back to the caster's nearest stack. Cheap insurance against curses.
- **W7. The Debt Collector's cousin, the Notary.** A Bank card a player can hire: while it's on your field, your loans
  accrue no interest, but the Notary takes 1 mana every pay day for its fee.
- **W8. The Spider.** A Grunt-sized card that leaves webs: each tile it leaves becomes webbed (a stack entering a web
  stops there). Fits the Weaver (S4) or works alone.
- **W9. The Twin.** Two cards that are one: Twins are always placed together and share hearts. Killing one kills both,
  but each attacks separately. A strange double-edged soldier.
- **W10. The Penitent.** A card that grows stronger by taking damage: each heart lost gives +2 attack. It has many
  hearts and no defense. Born to be hit.
- **W11. Veterans (an upgrade, not a card).** Every soldier that survives three fights becomes a Veteran: a small chevron
  appears on its card and it rolls one extra die. Lose a Veteran and you lose something you built.

## 3. New spells, by school (P)

The schools now are Necromancy, Enchantment, Curse, Sorcery, Conjuring, Astrology, Illusion and the Bank. Ideas for
each:

### Necromancy
- **P1. Danse Macabre.** Raise up to three cards from the grave for one turn only: they fight, then crumble back. Good
  for a last push.
- **P2. Bone Wall.** Turn three cards from the grave into a Wall on an empty tile of your spawn lines (the dead as
  masonry).
- **P3. Memento.** Look at the whole grave and choose one card; it goes to the top of your deck.
- **P4. Speak with the Dead.** Choose a card in the grave that once belonged to an enemy: see that enemy's hand.

### Enchantment
- **P5. Second Wind.** A stack that has already attacked this turn may attack again, at disadvantage.
- **P6. Bulwark.** A base gains a temporary heart until its next turn.
- **P7. Sanctuary.** A tile becomes holy for one round: no fights can start on or into it.
- **P8. Marching Song.** Every one of your stacks moves one extra tile this turn, in the same direction.

### Curse
- **P9. Rot.** An enemy stack loses one card at random at the start of each of its owner's turns, for three turns, unless
  they pay 1 mana each time.
- **P10. Leaden Feet.** An enemy stack can't move for two turns (like the Goatbull's pin, as a spell).
- **P11. Whispers.** The target player's next spell costs 2 more.
- **P12. The Evil Eye.** A target enemy stack attacks with disadvantage until it wins a fight.

### Sorcery
- **P13. Swap.** Two of your own stacks trade places.
- **P14. Pocket Dimension.** A stack vanishes from the field and reappears on your next turn on any of your spawn tiles.
- **P15. Transmute.** Turn a Grunt into an Infantry of the same suit (or Infantry into a Jack) at the cost of 2 cards
  from your deck going to the grave.
- **P16. Echo.** Cast the last spell you cast again, for half its price, rounded up.

### Conjuring
- **P17. Ether Lottery.** Pay 2 mana: a random card from the Ether comes into your hand (any kind). Gambling with souls.
- **P18. Call of the Ether.** Look at the next five cards the Ether offers; pick one at full price.

### Astrology
- **P19. Read the Stars.** See the next three moon phases ahead and how long each lasts.
- **P20. Eclipse Call.** Call an eclipse of a planet already in the sky, early.

### Illusion
- **P21. Phantom Army.** Place three face-down "phantom" stacks on empty tiles: they look like real stacks to enemies and
  vanish when attacked (the attacker wastes their attack).
- **P22. False Ace.** A phantom Ace: enemies see a second base where there is none.
- **P23. Hall of Mirrors.** For one round, every enemy sees your stacks in shuffled positions (their view is wrong; the
  game stays right).

### The Bank
- **P24. Insurance.** Pay 2 mana: the next card of yours that dies this round is refunded at its soul worth.
- **P25. Short Selling.** Bet against another player: if they lose a base before your next turn, you get 6 mana;
  otherwise you pay 3.
- **P26. Futures.** Pay now for a card from the Ether delivered three turns from now, at half price.

## 4. The sky: new Heavenly Bodies, Oaths and moon events (K)

The sky is one of the most distinctive things about the game: the Moon's phases, the Blood Moon, eclipses, the Second
Sun, Saturn, Venus, Jupiter, Mars, Mercury. The book's cosmos has more.

- **K1. Neptune, beyond the old spheres.** "The planets, with Neptune beyond." Neptune is far and strange. **In the sky:**
  tides. Every 1d4 rounds the tide rises: all water tiles (River map, Leviathan's Ocean) spread one tile, and stacks
  caught in water lose a heart unless they hold a Ferryman (W5). **Oath of Neptune:** your cards in water tiles don't lose
  hearts and move one extra tile through water. The Leviathan's player can draw Neptune into the sky from the deep.
- **K2. The First Sun, Lost.** "The first Sun, lost." An event, not a body: once a game, rarely, the First Sun returns for
  a single round. Fog is lifted everywhere, every card's hidden face is shown, and every Illusion and face-down stack is
  revealed. The truth shines for one round, then is lost again.
- **K3. The Dome of Stars: Constellations.** The True Angels hang by the spine from the stars. Each round, a constellation
  rises (the Lion, the Serpent, the Scale, the Crow, the Wheel...) and gives a small rule for the round: the Lion makes
  Kings stronger, the Serpent makes Jokers stronger, the Scale makes every fight tie on a tie (no ties broken), the Crow
  lets the dead speak (anyone may look at the grave), the Wheel reverses turn order for one round. Light, flavorful,
  and it makes every round a little different. Could be an option ("Constellations: off / on").
- **K4. The Weaver on the Moon (as an event).** If the Weaver Substance (S4) isn't in the game, she can still appear as a
  rare moon event: at a Full Moon, "a woman of shadow stands on the moon and weaves". Two random tiles are tied for the
  round.
- **K5. Oath of the Fullness.** The hardest Oath. Swear to the Fullness: you can't cast any spell that costs mana for the
  rest of the game (only free spells), and in return your cards never go to the Outer Darkness and every soldier of yours
  gets +1. An ascetic's path.
- **K6. Oath of Limbo.** Your dead go to Limbo instead of the grave. From Limbo, at every New Moon, one returns to your
  hand at random. You never get to choose who comes back.
- **K7. Oath of the Tar (the Second Moat).** Every enemy that attacks you is "tarred": it can't move next turn. But your
  own stacks move one tile less.
- **K8. The Moon Breaks.** If the Moon is eaten by Night's Judgement and the Leviathan was on it, a rare event: the Moon
  breaks into shards that fall as meteors over three rounds.
- **K9. Meteor Showers with Gifts.** Some meteors that fall leave a crystal behind (for the Miners), so the sky's violence
  feeds the economy.
- **K10. Moon Phases as a visible clock.** A small ring around the Moon showing how many turns remain in this phase, and
  the next phase's icon. The Moon is already beautiful; making its schedule readable at a glance would help planning.

## 5. Maps and places (M)

The pixel-art maps and King of the Hill showed how much a map changes the game. The codex has forty places; here are
the ones that would make the best fields.

- **M1. The Three Moats.** A large ring map. The center is "creation" (where the Hill would be); around it, three rings:
  the Scalding River (the first moat, water that burns: 1 damage to cross), the Tar (the second moat, twice as wide:
  stacks move one tile per turn in it), the Outermost Darkness (the third moat, where cards that die are lost to the
  Outer Darkness, always). Spawns are outside the third moat; the goal is to reach the center. A pilgrimage map.
- **M2. The Fool's Realm.** Green fields under a black sky with the Tree of Neither Life Nor Death in the middle. The
  Tree is neutral: whoever holds the tile beside it at the end of a round draws a card. Little hills of grass hide
  stacks (forest-like tiles that block sight).
- **M3. The Circus Tent.** A round arena, small (like a 7x7 circle), with the ring in the middle: fights in the ring are
  watched (the winner's owner gains 1 mana from "the crowd"). Trapdoors: a few tiles drop to the Clowns' Room (a stack
  that steps on one is moved to a random edge tile). Perfect for 1v1 duels.
- **M4. Tartarus, the Prison in the Ocean.** An island surrounded by water, with cells: walled tiles with one opening.
  A stack inside a cell can't be attacked except from the opening. A siege map.
- **M5. The Black-and-White Tiles.** "A world of squares, a bridge-door and words." A chessboard field: black tiles and
  white tiles. Cards on white tiles defend better, on black attack better. Diagonals matter: Jacks and Assassins shine.
  The bridge-door: two tiles at opposite edges connected as a portal.
- **M6. The Land of Two Suns.** Mountains, a crystal shore, two setting suns. Crystals grow on the shore twice as often.
  Over the game the suns set: from round 20 on, the field darkens (fog thickens, sight shrinks by one), and at round 30
  it's night: Assassins and face-down stacks double their effects. A map with a built-in clock.
- **M7. The God Machine.** "A fractal of every eternity." A map that changes: every five rounds, the field rotates by 90
  degrees around its center (everyone's "forward" changes) or one quarter of it mirrors another. Disorienting by design;
  an experimental mode.
- **M8. The Antique Store.** A maze of shelves (blocked tiles), narrow aisles, rain outside the windows. Every few rounds
  "a trinket" appears on a random empty tile: a small relic (Y1) that the first stack to reach it picks up.
- **M9. Kether's Hall.** A long throne hall: one player (or the Tiphareth player, if any) starts on the throne at one
  end; everyone else at the other end, down the crimson hallway. Asymmetric: the throne holder has fewer cards but a
  fortified position. A "king defends" scenario.
- **M10. The Monolith Towers.** Towers risen from no ground: tall tiles that give sight over the whole map to whoever
  holds them. Holding a tower reveals the fog around it for everyone on your side.
- **M11. The Graveyard Crossroad.** "A graveyard of fog, and the tree of both life and death." Crossroads (the game's own
  name!) as a map: four roads meeting in the middle of a foggy graveyard. Cards that die on the graveyard tiles can be
  raised for free once (the earth is restless). A natural home for necromancy.
- **M12. The Yellow Room and the Brown Carpet.** "A yellow light on brown carpet that keeps returning." A small map where
  one tile is the Yellow Room: whatever stands on it at the end of a round is sent back to where it stood three rounds
  ago. The room keeps returning; so do you.
- **M13. Map editor.** A simple tile painter in the setup: choose the size, paint mountains, water, forests, crystals,
  spawn zones; save the map and share it as a short code. Players would make things you'd never think of.

## 6. Modes (G)

- **G1. Story mode: The Road Through the Kenoma.** A campaign of short scenarios, one per chapter or per chapter group,
  each a small field with a story setup and a goal. "Dead Home": survive five rounds in a house of fog. "Seven Lies": the
  enemy's cards lie about their numbers. "Shoreline Lectures and Sermons from the Snake": a duel against the snake, who
  speaks between turns with lines from chapter 4. "Tank Meat", "Hexahedone", "Pills", "Art Talks Back"... Each scenario
  opens with a quote from its chapter and closes with one. Winning unlocks the next, and maybe card art or a Substance.
  This is the biggest idea here, and I think it would be the most beloved: it makes the book and the game one thing.
- **G2. Puzzle mode.** Fixed boards with a goal ("Win this fight in one turn", "Take the Ace with these three cards").
  No dice luck: puzzles would use the expected value, or fixed dice shown in advance. Good for teaching, and good on a
  phone in a waiting room.
- **G3. The Daily Field.** One seeded game a day, the same for everyone: the same map, the same Substances, the same
  draws. Compare your result with your own past days (and, if online, with others').
- **G4. Draft mode.** Before the game, players draft their deck from a shared pool: each in turn picks a card from a row
  of five. Substances still apply, but the deck is built at the table.
- **G5. Escort the Carriage.** King of the Hill's Carriage, turned into a mode: each side has a Carriage carrying their
  King across the map to the other side. Get there first, or kill the enemy's.
- **G6. Capture the Flag, with Claimers.** Each side has a flag on their back line; Claimers can pick up a flag and carry
  it. Bring the enemy's flag home.
- **G7. The Tar Rises (battle royale).** Free-for-all where the Tar creeps in from the edges every few rounds: tiles
  turn to tar, stacks in it lose a card each turn. The field shrinks until someone stands alone.
- **G8. Gauntlet against the Horde, with rests.** Survival mode in waves, with a "camp" between waves: buy upgrades with
  what you earned, heal, choose a blessing. A small roguelike in the Horde mode.
- **G9. Hot Seat Story: The Curse Passes.** A party mode: a Curse token (Curse, S7, or the Antichrist Doll) passes from
  player to player when they lose a fight. Whoever holds it at the end of round 20 loses.
- **G10. Two Moons.** A variant: two moons with their own phases, so a Full Moon and a New Moon can come at the same time.
  Chaos for experienced players.
- **G11. Pilgrimage (cooperative).** All players on one side, against the field itself: cross the Three Moats (M1) to
  reach the Fullness before the Kenoma swallows you. Events from the sky and the Demons get worse each round.

## 7. Systems that tie things together (Y)

- **Y1. Relics.** Small objects found on the field (from meteors, from the Antique Store map, from killing Demon Aces).
  A stack carries one relic. Examples: *The Pink Television* (see one enemy's hand), *The Rabbit Mask* (your stack looks
  face down to enemies), *Samson's Old Heart* (one revive), *A Painted Face* (enemies beside you roll −1), *The Horn*
  (the horn from chapter 44: once, force an enemy stack to skip its turn as it "listens to the sad song"). Relics would
  make the field feel like a world with things in it.
- **Y2. Scars and stories.** Cards remember. A King that killed three cards gets a small notch; a Jack that survived
  five fights gets a name. At the end of the game, a short "story of the game" lists the heroes: "Your Seven of Hearts
  killed a Satan and lived."
- **Y3. Reputation with the Bank.** The Bank already has a report and good standing. Push it further: a reputation level
  that changes the Banker's portrait and lines, opens better loan terms at high reputation, and at the lowest makes the
  Debt Collectors visit even when you're not in debt ("We're keeping an eye on you").
- **Y4. Diplomacy, a little more formal.** Peace Time, Buy Peace, Betrayal and War already exist. A small "treaty" window
  where two players agree on terms (no attacks for N rounds, share sight, pay mana each turn) that the game enforces,
  with a visible "treaty" line between their colors. Breaking a treaty is a betrayal with consequences everyone sees.
- **Y5. Hidden objectives.** At the start, each player secretly draws an objective card: "Kill three Kings", "Hold the
  midline for four rounds", "Never lose an Ace", "Swear three different Oaths". Achieving it gives a reward (a free
  conjuring, mana, a card). Revealed at the end.
- **Y6. A corruption track.** The more Outer Darkness you feed (cards you send there), the more "corrupted" your Substance
  becomes: at certain thresholds your cards get darker and stronger, and your necromancy weaker. Leaning into the dark
  should change you.
- **Y7. Memories (the Scalding River).** "The river of dying memories." When a card dies, it may leave a memory on its
  tile: a faint glyph. Another card stepping on it can "drink" the memory to learn something (see the hand of the dead
  card's owner, or regain a heart). Memories fade after a few rounds.
- **Y8. Achievements and a trophy room.** "Win with the Hermit alone", "Make the Leviathan eat a Blood Moon", "Hold the
  Hill with a single Puppet", "Survive the Second Sun's judgement five times". A page on the site listing them, ticked
  off on this device.
- **Y9. Card collection and art.** Unlockable card art drawn in the game's pixel style: different faces for Kings,
  Queens and Jacks; an alternate Satan; a golden Golden Child. Earned through play, not bought.
- **Y10. A real end-of-game screen.** After a game: a chart of each player's mana over time, cards on the field over
  time, the score in King of the Hill, the biggest fight, the most dangerous card, the Oaths sworn and broken. Games
  have stories; the end screen should tell them.

## 8. The computers and the automation (A)

The archetypes (v69) are a start. More:

- **A1. Archetype presets for your own automation.** The same seven playstyles as one-click presets in Configure
  Automation ("Play my field like a Bulwark"). I offered this already; it would make Field Automation friendlier.
- **A2. Computer voices.** Each computer says something now and then in the log, in the voice of its Substance and
  archetype. The Leviathan speaks like the sea; Satan threatens; the Jester jokes in riddles; a Warlord boasts after a
  kill; a Bulwark mutters when attacked. Short lines, not spam: once every few rounds, and always when something big
  happens. Lines could be drawn from the book.
- **A3. Computers that remember you.** Across games on this device, the computers keep a little memory: if you always
  rush, they learn to wall up; if you always hoard, they push early. A "rival" that adapts.
- **A4. A Nightmare difficulty.** Above Hard: it plans two turns ahead, reads the Moon's schedule, saves Jokers for the
  right moment, and coordinates with other Nightmare computers. For people who want to lose.
- **A5. Computer teammates you can talk to.** In 2v2 with a computer partner, a small "orders" menu: "Defend", "Follow
  me", "Go for the Hill", "Save your mana". The teammate follows them.
- **A6. Explain the computer's move.** A toggle that makes the log say why the computer did something: "Attacks because
  it wins 72% of the time", "Holds back: the enemy has more on the field". It would teach new players how the game thinks.
- **A7. Automation for the Bank.** A tab in Configure Automation for the Bank: auto-repay loans when you have spare mana,
  never let debt pass a line, auto-sell surplus cards. The Bank is powerful and fiddly; automating it would help.
- **A8. Automation "rules" in plain language.** A small rule builder: "When an enemy stack is next to my Ace, attack it
  with everything", "When I'm below 3 mana, sacrifice the lowest card". For people who like to program their army.

## 9. The interface and the feel of play (U)

- **U1. Fight forecast.** Before you confirm an attack, show the odds (the game already computes them for the AI): "You
  win about 64% of the time; expected losses: 1 card." Optional, default on for Beginner.
- **U2. Undo a move in the prep phase.** Until you attack or cast something, let a move be undone. Misclicks on a phone
  are cruel.
- **U3. Path preview.** Hovering a tile while moving a Carriage or a Scout shows the path and what it passes.
- **U4. Turn summary.** When it's your turn again, a short summary of what happened since your last turn: who attacked
  whom, what died, what spells were cast. "While you were away" in three lines, with a "Show me" button that replays it.
- **U5. Replays.** Save a game's log as a replay and watch it again, with a timeline slider. Share a replay as a link.
- **U6. Keyboard shortcuts on PC.** Space for Next phase, D for Draw, numbers for hand cards, arrow keys to move the
  selection, Esc to cancel. A small "?" sheet listing them.
- **U7. Colorblind patterns.** Each player's cards get a faint pattern too (stripes, dots, crosses), so colors aren't the
  only way to tell sides apart. It fits the dark, textured look.
- **U8. The Codex in the game.** A card's info could link to its being in the Codex ("The Leviathan: read about it"),
  and the Codex could show the card. The two halves of the site, joined.
- **U9. A bigger, prettier death.** When a big card dies (Satan, the Leviathan, a King, an Ace), a short special death
  animation in its own style: Satan cracks into embers, the Leviathan sinks into black water, an Ace crumbles to stone.
- **U10. A tutorial that is a story.** The first time someone plays, a short guided game with Samson as the guide,
  speaking in his voice, teaching draw, prep, attack, the Moon and the Ether one at a time.
- **U11. Tooltips that teach dice.** Hovering a die result shows what it was for and what would have changed it.
- **U12. A "focus mode".** Hide everything but the field, your hand and the phase button; for small screens and for
  calm.

## 10. Online (O)

- **O1. Play by link, at your own pace.** Asynchronous games: each player takes their turn when they can, and the game
  waits (hours or days). Perfect for friends in different time zones. Needs a small server or a shared store, which the
  site doesn't have yet; worth considering if online grows.
- **O2. Spectators.** Watch a friend's game from the room list.
- **O3. Rematch and swap seats.** After a game, one button to play again with the same settings, or with seats rotated.
- **O4. Emotes from the book.** A few short lines a player can send ("It hurts.", "Probably nothing.", "Sink!",
  "Please don't hurt me"), shown in a speech bubble over their Ace.
- **O5. Saved online games.** If the host's connection drops, the game is saved so everyone can rejoin.

## 11. Music and sound (N)

- **N1. Music that follows the game.** The music already ducks for events. Go further: a calm layer while the board is
  quiet, a darker layer when a Blood Moon rises or Demons come, and a tense layer when a base is under attack. The same
  song, with stems that fade in and out.
- **N2. A motif for each Substance.** A short phrase that plays when a Substance's signature card enters: Satan's low
  brass, the Leviathan's deep swell, the Jester's music box, Antony's choir, Yaldabaoth's grinding drone.
- **N3. The Moon has a sound.** A soft chime at each phase change, different for each phase; the Blood Moon with a low
  heartbeat; an eclipse with the music briefly pitched down.
- **N4. New songs for the new chapters.** "Art Talks Back; I Can't" (a broken brass piece, a horn that chokes: you have
  the image already in the chapter) and "Probably Nothing" (something that falls apart into syllables, like the
  chapter's language). You said new songs were next; these two chapters practically score themselves.
- **N5. Map ambience.** Each pixel map with its own quiet background: rain for the Antique Store, wind for the Desert of
  the Rite, water for the River, distant circus music under the Circus Tent.

## 12. The book and the game, woven together (B)

- **B1. Chapter cards.** Each chapter becomes a rare card: "Pills", "Chalice of Tears", "Seamed Libationary". Played as a
  one-time event spell with an effect drawn from its chapter. "Seamed Libationary": the writer is the reader; for one
  round, you play the turn of the enemy you choose (you make their moves, but they keep their cards). "Probably
  Nothing": a spell whose effect is random and is shown only after it is cast, and sometimes it does nothing at all.
- **B2. Quotes that react.** The book's passages already fold on the page. In the game, a short quote could appear at
  big moments: the right line from the right chapter when Satan falls, when the Leviathan rises, when the Second Sun
  judges.
- **B3. The Codex grows as you play.** The first time you meet a card or a place in the game, its Codex entry lights up
  as "seen". Over many games, the Codex fills.
- **B4. A reading companion.** On each chapter page, a small panel: "In the game" listing the cards, places and spells
  from that chapter, linking to the rules.
- **B5. Alice's gallery.** Alice is a painter. A page of "her paintings": the pixel art from the game, framed as her
  works, with captions in her voice. Some paintings could be the art you've already made for the plates.

## 13. Strange ones (X)

- **X1. The game reads you back.** "Seamed Libationary" says the pages contain everything happening, and that the reader
  is reading this very line. One rare event could put that into the game: the log suddenly writes what you are about to
  do ("You will move the Seven to D4") a moment before you do it, using the computer's prediction. If you do something
  else, it writes "You didn't." Unsettling and very on-theme.
- **X2. A card that is the player.** A Joker-like card named after you (your seat's name), which, if it dies, makes you
  lose... no, makes the screen crack for a round: your own view gets glitched, your cards shuffled on screen (the real
  game unchanged). A horror moment, rare.
- **X3. The Fourth Wall.** If you leave the tab and come back, the game notices: "You looked away." A tiny chance that,
  while you were away, a Doll moved one tile.
- **X4. Probably Nothing (the chapter, as a mechanic).** A button on the board labeled "Probably nothing." Pressing it
  usually does nothing. Once every few hundred presses, it does something.
- **X5. Night mode at night.** After midnight on the player's clock, the game is subtly different: the fog is a little
  thicker, a few lines in the log are stranger, the Moon looks closer.
- **X6. The painting that changes.** The background art of the setup screen changes very slightly each time you open
  the game, until one day a face is there that wasn't before.

## 14. Small things I noticed while working (Q)

Not ideas so much as notes, from fixing things over the past days:

- **Q1. Spawn lines on 6x6.** The open question: should 6x6 and bigger have one spawn line, like 5x5? With the default
  1v1 now 6x6, two lines means a lot of starting depth. One line would make 6x6 feel closer to the old 5x5.
- **Q2. Demon Aces and the computers.** Now that Demon Aces crumble when their band is gone, the computers could learn to
  kill the soldiers and ignore the Aces, which is the efficient play. They half do this already.
- **Q3. The Blood Moon spawn.** The Blood Moon can call Demons too; with the new caps it's safe, but a Blood Moon band
  could have its own look (darker, with red eyes) and its own popup line.
- **Q4. Hand size on a phone.** With seven or eight cards in hand on a phone, the hand wraps; a fan or a horizontal
  scroll would keep it to one row.
- **Q5. The log as a story.** The log is very complete. A "story view" that shows only the big events (deaths of
  wildcards, Moon changes, Oaths, Demons, the Bank's big moves) would make it pleasant to read back.
- **Q6. Cheat mode as a sandbox.** Cheat mode could grow into a full sandbox for testing: place any card anywhere, set
  any player's mana, set the Moon's phase. Useful for you when trying out new ideas from this list.

---

That's the list. If even three of these become part of the game, it was a good night's work. My own favorites, if I had
to choose: **G1** (story mode through the chapters), **S8** (Alice, the painter whose art talks back), **K3** (the
constellations), **Y1** (relics), **U4** (the turn summary) and **X1** (the game that reads you back).
