## Part B — digest

### Automation (the Configure Automation menu)

- A1 Ritual Dice automation, set from a configure button by the spell and from the Configure Automation drop-down: auto-buy on/off;
  how often (every turn, or every N turns); how many a turn; only when the grave holds chosen kinds (Special, Wildcard, Base,
  Support); only while mana is at least X (arrows); never when Grave Blessing can't be cast (moon events, a full overdraw);
  "buy to make up for overdraw" on/off; "don't buy while overdrawn" on/off; stop while owing X or more in debt or loans (0 = never
  stops); buy on credit on/off, stop on credit at X mana (arrows, down to −6); presets by playstyle with creative names. Turning
  auto Ritual Dice on turns auto Grave Blessing on.
- A2 Ritual Dice: each purchase (1 mana) adds 3d6 to this turn's Grave Blessing; it can be bought more than once a turn.
- A3 Lazy Hand automation: kinds never placed for you (Standard, Special, Support, Wildcard, Base) plus "Yes, and" single kinds;
  auto-sacrifice from the hand: kinds to sacrifice plus "Yes, and" single kinds (e.g. the 2 Grunt); "not when mana is full" (on by
  default); when off, arrows for how far into the surplus it may go, or "up to the most surplus" (fades the arrows); "only while my
  mana is below zero" (fades what no longer applies). A note advises against using it.
- A4 One "Configure Automation" drop-down (was "Field automation") holding Field, Ritual Dice and Lazy Hand, all in the same style;
  labels on the left, checkboxes and arrows on the right; more compact on a computer.

### Field Automation

- F1 "Don't attack" (off by default) at the top of the hierarchy unless moved.
- F2 Target priority (off by default; turn it on): a hierarchy of card kinds (suit ignored, strongest first by default), with
  equals; Grunt and Infantry their own kinds; "ignore how strong a Grunt or Infantry is"; "treat Grunts and Infantry alike".
- F3 "Flee when low" under Lives (stacks that lost cards pull back), shown only with the Tall formation ("Deep" renamed "Tall").
- F4 Small notes under Lives and Picking battles on how they differ.
- F5 A caution note on every hierarchy: a complex setting where mistakes can cost a lot.

  flags and the like fade more under it. Its next attack or defense adds a d12 roll: 1 +1, 2 +1d2, 3 +1d3, 4 +1d4, 5–6 +1d6, 7–9

- S4 Necromancy: Grave Blessing first, Ritual Dice second, both in a dark purple emphasis.
- S5 Spell names in blackletter by default; a Display switch turns it off.
- S6 The status tags ("Loan" and the like) show every status effect of a player, with details on hover or hold.

### The Bank

- B1 Debt Collectors come after 3 turns at −1 or lower (was −6). Loans: in debt for 3 + ⌊owed ÷ 3⌋ turns → Collectors until paid
  (or the Bank makes you pay). Biggest debtor first.
- B2 "Credit and Loan Report" tab: a paper at every Full Moon (every 8 rounds without the Moon): first and last turn; mana taken
  from loans, from credit, the two together; interest owed (credit, loans, total); standing (good, or bad: Debt Collectors
  inquire); mana earned in the period; taxes deducted. The Miner land tax uses the same period.
- B3 Buy land: 2 mana, a spawn point on a center tile, until the next report. A card spawned there without a base costs 1d2 − 1;
  no Walls. A $ in the player's color marks it (see-through under a card). A base on it stays a spawn point after the land expires,
  until the base is destroyed. A base in the center pays 1d2 tax each period. Every Ace or Tree: 1 in 3 chance of a 1d2 tax each
  period (1 in 2 in bad standing). Taxes are collected only at the report; with zero or less mana they go unpaid (no credit) and
  the player falls into bad standing.
- B4 Players who stay in default for six turns: 50% more Debt Collectors.
- B5 Debt Collectors can only be attacked by players who are themselves in debt (teammates can't help).
- B6 A lender can forgive a loan. The Bank lets players give mana or cards to each other for free. Card trades at the Auction for
  everyone.
