Game types and rule options

Local

Two to four players on one device; any seat can be a computer. Each picks a Substance (see Substances below), a color, and a platonic solid as a symbol. The symbol marks the player's cards everywhere in the player's color, and the player's deck bar is drawn in that color, one block for every card in the deck, with a brighter overlay at its left end when the deck grows past its starting size. The mana bar is a deep indigo, one block for each mana.

Versus Computer

One to three coded opponents, not a language model, at three difficulties each, 1v1, free-for-all, or two against two with a computer as your teammate. Each turn it chooses between offense and defense from the field and the decks: defense bunkers (Kings with Queens, Aces held back, Hypostasis over a threatened stack); offense surrounds enemy stacks and casts curses. Easy plays an easier version of this, Medium is balanced, and Hard goes all out on your field and your deck, plays the Joker for souls, stacks Aces for the Grave Blessing and keeps its own deck high.

Survival

A tower defense for one to four players, always played with the midline. Two survivors play on a 6 by 5 field, three on 9 by 6 and four on 8 by 6 by default (the width and height can be changed), splitting the bottom two lines between them; the Horde spawns once for each survivor still standing, and the survivors take their turns one after another on the device. You open with an Ace and its Jack; the Horde takes the first turn. Each round the Horde spawns a group from a fresh deck on its two lines, stacking where it can, then moves on you and attacks the stacks that threaten it most. It has no spells, hand, deck or grave: its dead vanish, unless your Joker captures them. Spells that only touch a hand or a deck (Forced Hand, Double Edged Sword, Three of Swords, Naked Hand) are off. The moon still turns and Blood Moons still rise. When a Horde Joker shoots itself, the cards it captured from you go to the Outer Darkness and you lose 1 mana. You lose when your last card on the field falls, and the round count, in Roman numerals, is your score.

Rounds Grunts Infantry Jacks, Queens, Kings (random) Most spawns
1–5 1d4 + round 1d2 − 2 + round none 4, growing as round ÷ 2 (rounded up)
6–10 1d4 1d4 + (round − 5) 1d2 − 1 + (round − 5) ÷ 2 3d2 + (round − 5) ÷ 2
11–15 1d4 1d4 + (round − 5) 1d2 − 1 + (round − 5) ÷ 2 3d2 + 2
16 on as 11–15 3d2 + 2, +1 every five rounds

Aces never spawn. The Horde ignores two limits: a King or a Queen standing alone can still attack. When the Horde's own two lines are full, a new card takes the place of the weakest Horde stack still standing there, which fades back into the Ether. Each spawn keeps its highest cards first (Jokers, then Kings, Jacks, Queens, then soldiers from 10 down) until the limit is reached. From round 11 the Joker spawns first: roll 1d4, and on a 4 a Joker spawns and you roll again, two Jokers a round at most.

Substances

A Substance is what you play as. Each one changes your deck, your spells or your limits. Random picks one for you when the game starts.

  • Beginner: eight starting cards (six drawn, plus a second Ace and a Joker), eight mana at most and an eight-card hand, and three Grunts already standing as one stack on the center of your frontline (or just left or right of it). Please Don't Hurt Me (Enchantment, free, once a game): none of your stacks can be attacked until your next turn begins; your cards turn pink and everyone is told. The Beginner's deck also holds four Scouts, an Assassin, two Archers, a Wall, two Repairmen and a Battering Ram, the first Ace comes with two Scouts and a Miner, Archers cost the Beginner 1 mana, and a free Scout comes every Full Moon.
  • Angel Machine: Antony's Substance, always Bone white with the cracked mask (no one else can pick Bone). Three of Swords costs 1 mana. You earn a bolt for every card you lose while defending against an enemy's attack, and for every card Antony kills. With three bolts, Lightning Strike (Sorcery, free) immobilizes an enemy stack for 1d2 + 1d2 − 1 of its turns: a blue bolt with the count sits on it. The deck holds Samson, the mech suit: two hearts, defense 12; one step straight or one or two diagonal; the Slugshot Cannon (3d20, at an enemy two tiles away in a straight line or one diagonal, and from two away a miss costs him nothing), the Arm Blade (12 + 3d8, up close) or the Mech Slam (16 + 1d4, up close). When Samson falls, everyone is told that the angel Antony was sharing his form, lodged in his heart, and has raised his corpse with himself: Antony takes his tile, with two hearts, 12 + 6d4 on attack, 12 on defense, and Samson's movement. Samson wanders through Limbo and Antony, when he falls, goes missing: both show up as tabs (Limbo, Unknown) inside the Outer Darkness, and neither returns. Neither stacks with anything but an Ace.
  • Satan: always his very dark crimson with the claw. The Satan card: three hearts, 6d12 on attack and on defense with no modifiers (no moon bonus, no odds, no Hate), never stacks, and falls into the Outer Darkness, where it statics red. Hate (Curse, 3 mana): an enemy stack's strongest card turns red; while it lives, its stack has disadvantage on every attack and defense, and everyone attacking it has advantage.
  • Binah: three Slavemasters in the deck (8 + 1d8, stacking like an Infantry). Enemy soldiers killed in a Slavemaster's fights become your thralls, ten at most. Slaves (Necromancy, free) puts a thrall down on an empty tile of your ground as a Slave: a rifle of 10 + 1d12 at an enemy two tiles away in a straight line or one diagonal (from two away a miss costs it nothing), one straight step a turn, no defense, and it stands alone. When a Slave dies it goes to the grave as the soldier it was. Binah's Jokers add 3 to their attacks.
  • Jester Fool: a seven-card hand and seven mana at most. Every soldier in the deck is a Puppet (a 6 Grunt). The Tree of Neither Life Or Death starts in your hand: put it on an empty tile of your ground. It has three hearts and cannot defend; it holds up to six cards like an Ace, but never Kings or tens; it gives 2 mana at the start of each of your turns and grows a free Puppet inside it every two rounds; it falls into the Outer Darkness with every card in it. BullGoat (Conjuring, 3 mana) brings a BullGoat into your hand: two hearts, defense 10, 10 + 1d20 up close, or a charge of 10 + 2d20 at an enemy two or three tiles away in a straight, empty line that carries it up to its target, even in the attack phase. When the BullGoat dies it goes back into your deck and comes out as the Goatbull: two hearts; Horns (10d2 + 1d10, up close); a Charge of 10 + 2d20 at exactly two tiles; and Stomp, an ability check of its 1d20 with advantage against the target's 1d20 with disadvantage (Samson rolls plain). If it wins the stack is Behooved, pinned for 2d2 of its turns (1d2 on a tie), unable to move or attack; a roll of 16 or more that the target doesn't match also deals 1 damage, and at the start of each later pinned turn it checks again needing 5 less (11, then 6…). A Goatbull attacked while it pins loses its advantage in later checks. The Goatbull falls into the Outer Darkness. The Tree takes no damage from ranged attacks. Deck of Cards (Conjuring, 6 mana, once a game, while the Tree stands) makes your deck full again as it was at the start; the cards left in it go to the grave and your hand stays.
  • Tiphareth of Kether: the Northern tribe of the Euclids, with a symbol of your choice. Three Traffickers in the deck (8 + 1d8, stacking like an Infantry) chain any enemy card that dies on the eight tiles around them, three prisoners each, wildcards included. Life for Sell (Sorcery, free) puts a prisoner up at the Auction, which opens beside the Outer Darkness: any player can offer mana or cards from the hand, and the sale goes through only if you approve it; the buyer takes the card into the hand. The Blindfold (Enchantment, 2 mana, while you are under a Delusion): when the Delusion ends you keep it three more turns, blind but with nine mana and the free dice, and without anything Lucifer paid to stifle it. Immune to the Leviathan's toll. Jokers add 1 to their attacks.
  • Lucifer & Ouroboros: always the ink and scroll; the color is yours. Delusion of Ouroboros (Sorcery, 1d2 mana, rolled each round): for 2d2 of your turns you and your teammates can't see enemy cards beyond your own cards' reach (the tiles around them, and two tiles away in a straight line), and meanwhile your mana can rise to nine and you get 3d2 free mana at the start of each turn. Delusion of Lucifer (Curse, 6 mana, free in a round you cast Ouroboros on yourself): the same Delusion, with the same blindness and the same gifts, on an enemy; then you can stifle it, paying 2 mana or 2 random cards from your deck for each maximum mana or free die you take from them while it lasts. Deconjured (Curse) has two forms: the full curse (2 + 1d2 mana) stops all conjuring at the next Full Moon and the grave gift at the next New Moon; the short curse (2 mana) stops everyone's conjuring, yours too, for the next round. Pleasure and Joy (Curse) has two forms, until your next turn: Pleasure and Joy (1 + 1d2 mana) makes your cards glow and sends those that are killed back into your deck instead of the grave; Joyful Theft (1d3 − 1 mana) takes 1 mana from an enemy for every damage it deals to your units, and gives it to you.
  • The Leviathan: color and symbol are yours. The Leviathan card: three hearts, 6d4 + 1d2 + 6 on attack and defense, no stacking and no Queen bonus; what it kills falls into the Outer Darkness, and so does it when it dies. Rise to the Moon (Sorcery, free, only at the New Moon while it is on the field) lifts it onto the moon, with the serpent's words in a box and then “Spit the empty full into”: from the next New Moon until it is dead, every enemy pays 2d4 random deck cards, two random hand cards and one mana into the Outer Darkness at each New Moon (Tiphareth of Kether is immune). On the moon your mana cap rises by one for every card in the Outer Darkness, up to twelve. Any enemy unit can attack it there, only at the New Moon (select the stack, then click the moon or the attack button), and it defends with advantage. Antony's lightning needs no roll against it and takes one heart; if lightning takes the last one, the box tells of the twelve strikes. Exile: the Outer Darkness (Necromancy, free): a Joker of yours sends the souls it captured into the Outer Darkness.
  • Yaldabaoth: always the symbol of Saturn; the color is yours. Night's Judgement (Sorcery, free, at the New Moon or the Full Moon): he eats the moon; it leaves the sky and its phases stop. A Leviathan on it goes back into its player's deck. Take 3 mana, or count the moon as three cards toward the hunger. God Hunger (Necromancy, 1d2 mana, twice a turn): eat 1d2 cards from the grave, your own deck, the selected stack on the field (not cards with hearts, Aces or the Tree), or an enemy's deck; for an enemy's deck they roll 1d4 against yours, and if you don't beat it you lose 2 mana to them. Each time, take the eaten as mana, or feed the hunger (the eaten fall into the Outer Darkness). At seven, Regurgitate Saturn (free): Saturn takes the moon's place, and every enemy, at the start of each turn, pays 1 (a random hand card, the top deck card or 1 mana) into your soul pile, or refuses and a Debt Collector rises on your ground. A Debt Collector copies the leading card of whatever it fights, without its advantage, and each one still standing at a new round raises the price by 1 for everyone. Binah never pays.
  • The Firmament: always the six-pointed star. Above the harms of the Moon and Saturn: no Full Moon toll, drawing and necromancy under the Full Moon, no New Moon surcharges, its dead never fall into the Outer Darkness under a Blood Moon, no Saturn tax, no Leviathan toll; meteors and its own Asteroid can't touch it. A Miner on the moon brings 1d2 − 1 mana every turn, until the moon leaves the sky or the Leviathan climbs onto it. Blood Moon (Sorcery, 2d2 + 2d3): the next Full Moon turns red. Rain (Sorcery, 1d2 + 1): until your next turn, every sacrifice gives one more mana, and every sacrifice by someone else gives you one. Asteroid (Sorcery, 3d2): on a selected stack, one damage there, one damage on a 4 of 1d4 around it (Walls 1d2 + 1), fire for two rounds. Fog (Illusion, 2d2, once every three turns, not under the Full Moon): for 1d2 of your turns enemies see only what is beside their cards and their Scouts one less; at the New Moon they are blind and your own cards see one tile. Mars (Astrology, 2d3): Mars, “Strife”, takes the moon's place: every turn, stacks of only Grunts and Infantry get advantage on their next attack on 1–5 of 1d8. Second Sun (Astrology, 2d3, and one of your Jokers shoots itself): an indigo sun, “King”, takes the moon's place; no fog of war while it burns; every 2d2 turns it judges every other player (1 mana to you, one damage to one of their stacks or two deck cards, or blindness for that turn). Second Sun's Fall (free): the Sun crashes on a selected stack: two damage, one on a 2 of 1d2 around it, one on a 10 of 1d10 on the ring beyond, your own troops too; a Black Sun destroys the tile for good (and on a 6, the tiles around it). Venus (Astrology, 2d3), “Harmony”: +2 mana a turn and +2 maximum for you or a teammate, or (not in Survival) +1 mana a turn while every enemy gets +2 maximum. Jupiter (Astrology, 2d3), “Fortune”: Ace miners bring 1d2, two conjurings on sale each round, trades at the Auction, more mana for a Jester's Sacrificial Suicide, and sometimes a mana for you when others buy on sale or trade.

Substance cards with hearts lose one heart each time they take damage and fall at none. They can't be sacrificed, and they never lie in the grave. One-time spells say so when they are cast. Big moments come up as a box; smaller ones as a short notice across the top of the screen in the color of the player they belong to.

More players

  • Free-for-all (3 or 4 players): 7 by 7 by default, or any square from 5 to 9, with the corners cut by default. Each player spawns and raises Aces only on their own edge. The last player standing wins.
  • Two against two (4 players): side by side (teammates on neighboring edges of a square field), across (teammates on opposite edges), or same side (6 by 6 by default, 6 to 8 wide and 4 to 6 tall; each team holds one end and each player half its columns, like 1v1). Each player chooses Side A or Side B in their setup; each side needs two players. Teammates can't attack each other. A team wins when both enemies have fallen.
  • Decks: free-for-all players take a whole deck each (default) or half a deck (one red and one black suit). A team splits one deck, two suits and one Joker to each teammate; with a shared team deck teammates draw from one deck and spend from one soul pile but keep separate hands.
  • When a player falls: their deck and hand go to the grave and the grave is shuffled (default), or they go to the Outer Darkness. A shared team deck stays with the teammate still standing.
  • Turn order: classic (default for Local), draw and prep together with attacks in turns (default against the computer and in single-player Survival), or every phase together. A phase played together ends when every player is Ready. More than one person on one device always plays classic.
  • Phase timer (off by default): 30, 60 or 90 seconds a phase; under 60 and 90 the draw phase gets 30. When it runs out the game moves to the next phase, and during setup it places your cards the way a Medium computer would.
  • Computer speed: slow, normal (default) or fast.

Rule options

  • Blood Moon mode: the game begins on a Blood Moon. Each player's deck is shuffled before two thirds of it go to the graveyard, and each player starts with one Joker in hand.
  • Battle map: no map (plain tiles) by default, Random (one of the four regular maps), or chosen: Keep (a stone keep on your backline, a graveyard on the midline, the enemy's barracks), Sahel (red laterite and dry grass of the far north at night, with mud-brick walls and thatched huts), Forest, Dungeon (no castle or barracks), or the special maps: River and no map. Every map is at night. The art is drawn under the tiles and shows through the gaps between them.
  • River: a wide brown river like the Ogun near Lagos runs along the midline. It can only be crossed on a bridge two tiles wide, on the left or the right. The River map is always 7 wide and 5 tall and can't cut its corners. It isn't offered in Survival.
  • Field size: 1v1 is 5 (default), 6 or 7 tiles wide, with the midline on (default) or off and the corners cut or kept. Free-for-all and the edge 2v2 layouts are a square from 5 to 9 (7 by default); 2v2 on the same side and co-op Survival pick a width and a height. On a phone, 5 wide is the largest size that stays easy to read.
  • Display: the hand can sit under your deck or beside the field; the selected-stack box above or below the spells; the turn buttons in a bar right under the field (default) or at the top. At the bottom of the screen you can change the card icon size, the interface size, the text size (words only), the hand position, the tile names (off by default; columns A, B, C from the left and rows 1, 2, 3 from the bottom, like a chessboard), the page width on a computer (standard or full width) and the theme.
  • Log: every action is listed with the time to the second on your computer, colored in the color of the player who took it, with the tiles it happened on.
  • Phase timer off: while a timer is running, the quote box has a button that switches it off for the rest of the game.
  • Card info: the small line in the top-right corner of every card and stack opens its stats: type (standard, special, wildcard or base), attack and defense averages with their min–max, hearts, soul worth, effects, the cards in it and anything it captured or imprisoned. Drag that corner from one stack on the field onto another to compare them side by side: higher in green, lower in red, equal in yellow. The selected-stack box shows the same stats, and can compare with another stack or with any kind of card.
  • Status: hover or click a player's symbol for their limits, effects and recent spells; hover or click the moon for what it means for you.
  • Menu: at the top of the page; it pauses the game and holds every display setting.
  • The Bank (the gold bar at the top of the spells): every mana spent on spells goes to the Bank, and it lends only what it holds. Loans: 20% interest every 1d2 turns, due in 4 turns; what isn't paid then becomes mana debt. Below zero it lends six at most; at minus your maximum, five at most and at 30%. Spend on credit: spells you can't afford go on your debt. Every soul you gain pays the debt first. Below −3 × your maximum (−18 at 6) you lose at once. You can sell cards from your hand or deck for their conjuring price (they go to the Ether; the deck is reshuffled), and Binah can sell thralls for 1 mana each. A spell bought more than twice in each of two rounds in a row is upsold by 1d2 − 1. A Bank in the red puts the Ether on sale (1 less) and asks the richest player for a loan, which it pays back at 20% every two rounds. Anyone more than their maximum below zero is hunted by the Bank's own Debt Collectors (1d3 every 1d3 rounds); what they kill goes to the Ether and its price to the Bank. If the Bank is 12 in the red and every player 6 below zero, it collapses: everyone back to zero, every maximum falls to three, five cards from each deck and one Ace from the field go to the Ether, and prices wobble up for 4d2 turns. The Bank can open with (2 × players)d2 mana (off by default). One in a thousand rounds, the Banker dies of a heart attack: the Bank and the Ether close for good.
  • Gambling (Sorcery, 1 mana): 1d10; a natural 10 wins 7d2 mana from the Bank; a 1 costs one maximum mana, 7d2 of mana debt and a card from your deck. Increase Max Mana (Conjuring, 6): +1 maximum mana, eighteen at most. Mystery Generation (in the Auction, 1d2 + 2d3): 1d3 brand-new cards of any rarity made from thin air into your deck.
  • Barbarians (on by default): every 4 + 1d12 turns, and with every Blood Moon, a crimson band takes the middle of the field: a stack of 1d3 + 1 Grunts, a second stack (more Grunts, or 1d3 Infantry or 1d2 Jacks), sometimes a third, and an Ace base on a 6 of 1d6; more with three or four players (at least two Aces with four). They don't move and strike at anything beside them. Whoever kills the most of a band gets an Ether sale for three turns (every conjuring 1 less); if the band had an Ace that died and two or more stacks, or three stacks and no Ace, the Banker also forgives that player's debts. If Barbarians hold the middle for 7d2 turns, the Banker gets angry and every price goes up by 1 until they're gone.
  • The meteor: one round in a hundred a meteor falls on a random tile: one damage there, one damage on a 4 of 1d4 on every tile around it (Walls take 1d2 + 1), and fire burns the area for two rounds (one damage on a natural 20 at the start of a stack's turn).
  • Hole in the middle (off by default, never on the River): an empty pit of one or two tiles each way in the center, doubled (a cross) or tripled (a square) where there's room outside everyone's lines.
  • Archers (standard, 1d2 mana, 1 for the Beginner): 1d20 + 1d10 at a stack exactly two tiles away, straight or diagonal; a miss costs nothing; no defense. Or Suppress: the target attacks with disadvantage next time (or waits 1d2 of its turns), and the Archer rests 1d2 turns. Alone, with a Queen, or inside an Ace, the Tree or a Wall. Eight per deck in the Ether.
  • Cannons (6 mana, one per deck): 2d20 at anything up to three tiles away, once every 1d2 + 2 turns; a miss costs nothing; two damage to a Wall; it can't take an Ace's last heart or harm the Tree. One step a turn, no defense; alone or inside a base.
  • Walls (base, 4 mana): five hearts, defense 10, never move; go up on any empty tile of your two lines; hold six cards, but only Archers and Cannons fire from inside (one shot a turn). Charges hit them twice as hard; Rams, Cannons and Lightning Strike do two. When a Wall falls, everything inside dies.
  • Support cards never fight and die last in their stack. Standing alone, an enemy beside them can Capture one into its stack's capture slot (one per stack), or Destroy it. Set a captive to work beside the stack in your prep phase and it is yours. Healer (2 + 1d3): a shield that turns one blow (back in 1d4 rounds), or one heart healed in or beside its stack (1d2 + 2 turns between heals; the shield is down 3 turns after); never with a Queen. Repairman (2): one heart back to a Wall, Ace or Tree in or beside his stack, then 1d3 + 1 turns of rest during which nothing fires from that base; he can strengthen the Tree up to five hearts, or upgrade an Ace once with his life. Battering Ram (3): worked by one or two Grunts, Infantry or Puppets, two damage to a Wall beside it; blows never kill it. Miner (2): in one of an Ace's two miner slots, 1d2 − 1 mana a turn from the next round; on a crystal it digs; elsewhere 1 mana on a 6; the first to fall when its stack is hit, and an attacker beside it may take it instead.
  • Crystals (on by default): a crystal cluster with 12 mana rises in the middle of the field every 1d6 + 4 turns (Headquarters opens with one); a Miner on it digs 1 + 1d3 mana whenever it rolls a 2 on 1d2.
  • Field Libation (Necromancy, free, not under the Full Moon): your stacks show an X; check cards and pour them out; each is worth its mana on a 2 of 1d2 and goes to the grave on a 1.
  • Fog of war (on by default): you see enemy cards only within two tiles of your own, or three of a Scout; foggy tiles are shaded. It can be switched off before a game.
  • Scouts (support): never attack or defend; see three tiles; uncover Assassins, ambushers and face-down cards within a round two tiles, and enemy Scouts within three. Enemies see a Scout only from right beside it or with their own Scout close by. Every deck holds two (the Beginner four and an Assassin); every Ace you put down brings one from the Ether (the Beginner's first brings two; Lucifer & Ouroboros get none); the Beginner gets a free one each Full Moon. Scout Conjure: 1 mana.
  • Assassins: two hearts (a pair stacked together has three), attack 10 + 1d12, defense 8; one straight, two diagonal, or two forward or back. Hidden like a Scout. Only another Assassin stacks with one. Assassin Conjure: 2d2 + 1 mana.
  • Face-down cards: tick "Place face down" before putting a card down. You see it with a faint stripe; enemies see only its back. It attacks with double disadvantage and defends with advantage, and flips face up once it attacks or is attacked. A Scout within two tiles sees what it is.
  • Ambush: an Assassin or a face-down stack can be set in ambush (the card turns sideways). It vanishes from enemy sight unless they stand right beside it, and it strikes with advantage, out of turn, at the first enemy that steps beside it. Moving it ends the ambush.
  • Quiet Eyes (Illusion, 2 mana now and 2 each round, 1d2 + 1 rounds): enemies without a Scout on the field see only what is right beside their own cards. Bird's Eye (Sorcery, 1d2 mana): the fog lifts for you and your team for the rest of the round; it doesn't help against a Delusion.
  • Surplus mana: you can hold up to three times your maximum for a round (6/6 can become 18/6); at the start of the next round anything over the maximum goes to the grave.
  • Stacked odds: advantage and disadvantage cancel one for one. Two of the same kind roll with that advantage (or disadvantage) and add +2 (or −2) on top; each more adds 1.
  • Oaths (Astrology, free, cast again to break): Oath of the Earth under the Second Sun: no necromancy, and every Sorcery dice price rolls twice for you and keeps the lower. Oath of Venus: +2 mana and +2 maximum each turn you start without having attacked a player; each attack on a player costs 1d2 mana and two maximum for 1d3 turns. Oath of Jupiter: every conjuring costs one less, except what already costs one. A popup tells everyone when a planet with an Oath rises.
  • Mercury (the Firmament, Astrology, 2d3), “Guile”. Under it anyone can call a vote: Peace Time (free, everyone must agree, 3 + 1d2 − 1 rounds: every stack goes home and what has no room goes back into the deck, weakest first; every card with hearts heals; curses and enchantments end; everyone gets three grave cards, one at a time; no player may attack another or step into another player's lines) or Total War (free, everyone must agree, 3 + 1d2 − 1 rounds: every card killed falls into the Outer Darkness and pays its killer a mana, curses cost 1d2 − 1 less, teammates may fight and every damage to a teammate pays its soul's worth + 1d2 − 1). Buy Peace (4d2): Peace for two rounds without a vote. The Oath of Mercury unlocks Betrayal (1d2 + 2d3, team games: you leave your team for good after a yes or no; for two rounds kills fall into the Outer Darkness and damage between you and your former teammates pays its dealer), Declare War (1d2 + 2d3: Total War for 1d2 rounds without a vote) and Bribe the Banker (1d2 + 2d3 now and every round: one player pays one more for every spell for 3d2 − 1 rounds).
  • Eclipses: each planet in the sky has a 5% chance of an eclipse at the start of each round (the moon's eclipse is the Blood Moon). The suns: every hand, Assassin and face-down card is shown for the round, everyone may take one of their own cards back from the Outer Darkness, the field darkens and the page shimmers; Yaldabaoth eating the Sun now is blind and loses control of his troops for this round and the next. Jupiter (two rounds): everyone gets +2d3 − 1 maximum and a soul pile filled to twice the maximum, the Bank forgives every debt and loan and doubles what it holds, the Ether is on sale, miners bring twice as much for 1d4 turns, and Yaldabaoth gets a mana for each soul of others in the Outer Darkness. Venus: its gifts double, and everyone may give five random deck cards to another player to take five from the grave. Mercury: everyone votes Peace (1d2 + 1 rounds) or Total War; a split vote means war. Mars: the strongest in the Outer Darkness rolls its way back into its owner's deck, everyone gets two free curses (never the same one twice), and Samson may walk out of Limbo on a 2 of 1d2. Eclipse mode (setup) opens the game under the eclipse of the chosen sky: the Blood Moon for the moon.
  • Yaldabaoth and the Sun: The Sun Cries (free, once every 1d3 turns): three times and it becomes the Black Sun, and the Firmament that raised it pays Yaldabaoth 1d2 mana or 1d2 deck cards every 1d2 of its turns. Night's Judgement can eat any planet at any time (Jupiter feeds 4 hunger, the rest 3); an eaten planet never comes back. A player left with no ground to place cards on is out.
  • The sky: the moon (“Fate”, default), no moon, or a planet from the start: Saturn (“No”: everyone but Binah and the Firmament pays it 1 a turn to the Bank, or the Bank sends a Debt Collector; Yaldabaoth can't be played) or Mars (“Strife”: stacks of only Grunts and Infantry get advantage on their next attack on 1–5 of 1d8 each turn), the Second Sun (“King”), Venus (“Harmony”) or Jupiter (“Fortune”); any of these four keeps the Firmament out, and their favors go to the Bank. Hover or click the sky for what it does to you. A Leviathan can coil on Mars (its toll comes every eighth round), and Yaldabaoth can eat Mars; once eaten it never comes back.
  • Substances: only the Beginner can be played by more than one player in a game.
  • Dice box history: each dice box notes the player's last spell, the tile it was cast on and the time. Click a dice box to open the history of every player's rolls and spells, with tiles and the time on your computer; click anywhere else or press Escape to close it.
  • Headquarters (Local and Versus Computer): each side opens with two Aces as bases, the first holding its Jack, and troops can only be spawned inside your own Aces. New Aces may still be raised on the backline and become bases too. The only ways to win are destroying every enemy base or an economic victory; losing every other card on the field doesn't end the game.
  • Midline: on by default; can be removed outside Survival.
  • Decks: one deck between the two players (each takes one red and one black suit, dealt at random or chosen), two decks (default; a full deck each), or three decks (a full deck each and a whole third deck waiting in the Ether).
  • Specials (Jacks, Queens, Kings, Aces and Jokers): none besides the opening Ace and its Jack (default), half (the specials of half your suits), or all. Every special a player does not start with waits in the Ether, ready to be conjured.
  • At least one Joker (on by default): every player's deck holds at least one Joker, taken from the Ether when the specials setting would leave none.
  • Starting mana: each player starts with souls in their soul pile, taken from the Ether as soldier souls (or newly made when the Ether has none). Default 3d2; it can be 1d6, a fixed number, or none. With one deck each player rolls their own by default; with two or three decks one roll sets the same starting mana for everyone by default. Either can be switched.
  • Moon combat bonus (on by default): at the Full Moon every attack adds 1d2 to its score; at the New Moon every defense adds 1d2.
  • Spells in the attack phase (off by default): enchantments, curses and sorcery can also be cast during the attack phase. Necromancy, conjuring and sacrifices stay in the prep phase.
  • Optional Ace rule: the cards inside an Ace must match its color.
