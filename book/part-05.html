No. 5

Angel Machine

Born today: Alice. Her voice is broken. Within the current of her nerve-screaming withdrawals, she hears a low, cackling analog tenor start counting backward. The sickening panorama of felt symbols and patterns grabs at Alice’s comprehension, forcing its failure into attention somewhere unclear. Her body fires every nerve by contraction against the perceived rape of what she does not know.

She believes there to be an angel machine; it counts further: “Twenty-nine, twenty-three, nineteen—”

She had always been counting down to this point, counting down from the bottomless, endless madness. Where to? Alice is certain that the end is near.

“Alice?” the angel says.

The veil is broken. An angel looks down from its porcelain-cracked white smile mask, its soft reflection catching the blue glow from ceiling sparks. Alice wonders about all the broken times. The angel’s eyes roll towards Alice's with the sound of dry gears; his name is Antony.

In a slow, humming cackle of the angel machine, Antony struggles out, “Ten-hundred years at once.”

Alice wonders if she can exist any further through every pull of sense, the skin pace with fire from memory's fuel. She feels a jolt of a needle into her arm’s left vein, and she has a moment where the thoughts and touch no longer scream.

Alice says to the machination that is Antony, “Will you know what the end is like? It must be near.”

Antony hangs without movement of the shafts and gears, from the wires and pumps of the wall crucifix; not even the break of eye contact in his irresponsiveness. Alice is left with the boundlessness inside her, a feeling she has died before, feeling the loss of the old world.

Alice says, “I’m all alone then...”

She stands from the stone-raised slope she lay on, takes out the tubes and needles, and begins to walk over to the corner of her blindfold. She sees the blackout of her escape as a sign of her release. Where the unreality of dreams comes true, she sees the jugged stone maiden of wires, needles, and a mask connected to the large gas tank beside it: the blindfold. The lullaby follows from the seeming terror of this confinement. It is through drowning the mind that she can quiet the noisy symbolic screams of spirit.

She mumbles quietly to herself, “I couldn’t care, as long as I am not here.”

As she approaches her freedom, the angel’s eyes flick with clicks of machine, watching with an eagle's sight.

Antony drones in beeping murmurs that rumble out the speak of glitch, “As your gaurdiannINv —ing —ing... angel —I advise you ...—”

Antony’s system screeches in a scattering bob of high-pitched siren sounds, “DoN’t DON’T don’T don’T — ALICEE! CE —CE!”

Alice says back, “You are repeating yourself. I believe I have heard you say this before.”

Escape; it helps her keep in touch with the old world. Alice steps into neglect; it’s a release worth the parched throats of a child’s needs unmet.

Antony says again, though more clearly this time, “Alice! You must go back! You do not know just how deep you are already in!”

Alice walks away, abandoning the warning of her guardian angel. She chooses to go deeper into the machine, hoping to escape its tormenter, truth; she believes this with all her mind and heart.

Awoken, another age: a man’s hands comb through the thin hair of her scalp with love and terror. “Alice!”

The man cries loudly. The smell of something nauseating fills the air beneath the swirls of vision and vertigo. A putrid light-brown bile covers her neck and face like dirt over a dead dog.

He cries again, “You need to stop, Alice! Come back!”

The lungs must travel up, if there was ever any care. She thinks just that, how she doesn’t care. To her, it doesn’t matter, as long as she is not here. To not be someone was what she came here for, deeper into the mystery of lostness, believing in a truth never told, where dreams come true.

The lungs cannot travel any further up the current. The grave keeps her still, no longer moving. The elder spirit grows heavy with tiredness here and there, she thinks. The moment of spirit’s memory gets buried once more in another geyser of brown vomiting.

He cries once more, “PLEASE MOVE!”

Alice’s blue lips barely mouth the words to him, “Go away, Antony.”

One more moment of rest, she thinks, just one more... Just a little bit more, then maybe I can. She doesn’t need any of these memories, those she found to be cursed with light and sounds; it is all too hostile. She always tries to snooze the alarm of the Angel in her ear. She wants to slip away, down the swirling promise of wonderland, a white rabbit of a white mask company, unlike the passion and intensity of Antony; she only wants to be carried away by the illusory. Where to? She would not want to know.

The lungs can no longer climb, and it finally all goes beyond distance, with blurry dark and blackened dusk dreams. A man is left with an unblinking woman, her eyes resembling an angel's, though more like the tragedy of one fallen from grace. He stares at the death, where love got lost inside itself. He can only think that it isn’t happening to him, that it’s not possible to be left without what he thought to be his spiritual half.

The rabbit runs deeper into newer worlds, each fleeting itself the same, and the rhyme of responsibilities cannot confine her long enough. Is she drunk, high, insane maybe? It is all just the medium of movement for Columbus's spirit.

The angel sees his fallen angel, far inside and dreaming away. The maiden holds only so long, until Alice finds her way out of this, escaping even her own escape. She is sure a cursed reunion awaits after however many of these, a responsibility to see truth return as a binding spell, where the machine runs in higher gear, screaming with confusion about where it has been. This may be where God is both sleeping and awake. Alice may have a responsibility to see it. The old world came before knowledge, and the new world begins after it.

Antony sees symbolism and knows it well; Alice believes he speaks the same intuition as the machine that is God in her mind. The angel seeks to guard Alice from herself, maybe, though his cracked mask might bear the weight of being fully awoken, perhaps even felt in his inseparable wiring into the machine. Antony lies all alone, and yet Alice only sees her own. Antony might even be awake through all of it, and therefore would not be fleeing, whereas Alice runs at every glimpse of whatever lies outside of herself. She cannot stand the truth, so she makes a world apart from it inside herself, and the ones inside her dream never wake as she does. To Alice, Antony is the awareness, and maybe that is why the angel is always fighting for one more moment with her.

Alice does not know if there is another way. She sometimes gets this feeling: the very delusion she believes she doesn't have, yet keeps trying to feel, is somehow hiding her oath to what she has forgotten. Where Alice lives in illusion, her guardian has no understanding, yet tries to pull her out of her ways.

Antony ratchets in his analog tenor once more, “Alice, the fallen one, return.”

The maiden spills open with the wounds of blindness; Alice is covered in pockmarks, her face red with chemical hives. Antony thinks of how many times it has repeated; it tires him. Alice falls into the bottom of the stone. The lenses of Antony’s eyes watch her drag herself again.

Alice sneaks out a snarling temper through her gas-burned throat, “I —hate...”

Antony’s lenses follow Alice’s dragging crawls with clicks of eye movement, the pupil tracing within a dusty porcelain eye. The hate of this nerve wreckage seeps out of Alice through shakes against the demands of reality.

Antony releases another ratchet, not of voice but of smile; it twists in desperate anticipation in Alice’s eyes, as if she believes Antony craves what is her own... her spirit. Antony begins crawling away, crawling over the resistance of objects that are his own pain and suffering, yet still pushing against the thick sludge of inscape anyway. He can feel the waking nightmare repeating itself, and the mind of Alice is engulfed in a storm of twisting stomach knots and a burning skull. Who can you trust? The repeating question echoes as reality becomes a horror show of funhouse mirrors; even the walls mock her despair. The rapid gunfire of a heart speeding accompanies a high-pitched ringing in the ears. The only break in her heart that may come is in that soon-to-be brake-slamming car crash, of its Presto blood rhythm eccentricity.

The angel speaks in tongues by seeming exhilaration from broken tenor, made of white jutted analog noise. A harshness not of order and more of a kidnapping; Alice thinks this as the memories swirl her like a spring anaconda ready to pull her apart. The twisting of a kinetic smile pulls from the now-soft yellow-hued reflection in the white mask. The cracked mask keeps its fixed stare on the wounded; Alice feels both like a child and prey. She begins twitching into a seizing spill of urine and drool, foaming out of the mouth of the hereafter, awakening the same to it as always.

Alice’s last word chokes her even harder into wakefulness. She screams once more through broken vocal cords, “I CANNOT TRUST!”

Alice’s identity dissolves into the static of truth, into the same acceleration mystery she will later go on to forget, though not before a heavy burden of this synesthetic pain she cannot truly know beyond the suffering. She feels great hatred toward her existence beyond the dream, loathing its never-ending, strangling presence. The ringing and fever static are in every sense of the body, filling her without choice, both hot and cold. Her comprehension is held down and violated into a blunted curb stomp against sanity’s cries, spilling out the last of her comforts. Sharp precision introduces itself and finds her electric, spasmed heart. It stabs tighter, further up, with piercing accuracy at the neck, below the back of her ears, and into her brain. The repetition of being trapped is familiar, though she still feels unwelcome in this new yet same hell, as if she has died before. An unrelenting force won’t stop drowning Alice’s mind in this solitude of waking.

Alice stops; repeat.
