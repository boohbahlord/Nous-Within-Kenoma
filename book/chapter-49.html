Chapter 49

You Aren't There

The hard concrete pools into the ears and sucks him like sludge. No muffle or sound, just sinking. He sees the body slump over without himself, as if a human as machine without being. Skull smacks concrete. Skin wrapping hides the vacuum. Exhaustion’s noose dangles the void heft. He watches his empty body.

Christopher thinks these things, watching the prison of eternities that hides his existence slip away. First he thought he was falling, but he begins considering that the room is rising up without him. Maybe? Alice comes into view, and it breaks something in him to see her become further again. He doesn’t know what cracks inside him.

Alice has her food forcefully slurp itself. The last tail wiggles air—bout until the fast snake finds the crevice under the rock, making her stomach weighted with stone rope sensation. Alice falls with the stretched stomach, almost pregnant-looking. Where she lands, the hands catch and the hair hangs like dangled curtains. Weeping…

Christopher is sucked dead, stomped and smothered with the bittersweet indetermination of a source that flickers once before leaving, of light that introduced itself by negation. The light goes out as fast as it entered: the feeling of supreme gift being taken. Loss walks into the home of his heart and asks for a place to stay, clinging to its pulse like vampire. He realizes how much he wants and needs her.

She weeps into devastation of desolation, the tears collecting in the ground Christopher can’t be. Never has he needed more to breathe, to have a pulse, or to live, just to comfort her. Alice yelps softly in asphyxiated chokes that rise and fall: “St… Stay… Stay here. Stay for a while…” She stumbles on her words before catching her breath in a hurried inhalation, saying quietly from her storming expression, “A little longer… Please…” Alice can’t stop the flooding vacancy that penetrates her, vacant as his chasmed husk in view.

Christopher cannot say a word to her, despite the manic desperation to reach her. The box of her and Antony’s machine either floats or shrinks far or close, and it doesn’t sit; it’s restless in his soul.
