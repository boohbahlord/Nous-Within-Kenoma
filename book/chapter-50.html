VHS Tape

The rainfall is bad. Sarah can barely see past the windshield to find the white or yellow lines of the road back home. It is a lonely turn around the shaved hill. Trickling raindrops become bullets, and there was even a time she lost the bite of her tread on a curve during the storm. Nevertheless, she knows which turns to take. Past the hill, it is only a little longer.

Sarah, short of her home, gets the sinking feeling: “What if I hit someone? What about an animal?” She tries to see past the storm’s atmosphere, flooding the depth of sight and its diligence due. She can’t seem to know if she will hit anything, so she leans a little forward, hoping to catch what comes in the road, searching, but nothing. She has already slowed down, but she speeds up without thinking anyway, wanting to get home.

She parks the car and thinks with care towards what she purchased from the shop: the VHS tape and the painting of the jester. Sarah tries to hold both items under her coat and rushes out from the car with her valuables, her feet and hair quickly getting soaked before she arrives under the porch. She goes inside, finding the warm lights of her home still left on, in contrast with the dark blue outside from the storm, where the sunlight is drowned in. She places the items of hers on the kitchen table, next to the half-full bottle of wine and the empty glass.

Hours pass with her going about chores: laundry, cleaning, cooking, dishes, and folding. By then, it is already dusk, and the storm is lost somewhere else. The ground remains soaked, as can be smelt through the window she opens in the kitchen. She looks over at the table, remembering what she bought: “Oh…” Sarah thinks a bit before taking the VHS to the video player. She pushes the back, one of its long squared sides, down and into the small hole below the flap of the VCR. The tape vanishes inside the slot of the equal shape, where the skirt hang falls to cover it. The grey and white noise jumbles with the hiss from the play. Sarah mutters out loud, “Yeah, that needs rewinded, doesn’t it?” She goes back to the kitchen after pressing the back arrows for that.

The tape rewinds before it plays, so Sarah does a rare guilty pleasure: she turns on the coffee as sunset almost finishes. The brew machine is rhythm; it can be done in her sleep. You know, the cheap coffee grounds opened with a whiff of Pavlovian beans, the filter filled by scoops of which, the place in the machine it slides over, and the pot beneath it all.

Sarah reminds herself of the one thing she always forgets: “Don’t forget the water.” She has the coffee pot of the machine filled at the tap. She wonders how full it ought to be, since she won’t have much coffee, but forgetting to add water later is only a problem if the reservoir is dry. Sarah drowns the well in lukewarm of the sink’s faucet. It isn’t always she thinks about this, so it must be a different kind of night for her to have thought of which at this time.

The coffee pot is placed on the plate. The red translucent switch is flipped, and the light of the same color glows behind it. Then, the borble and churgle, with little unsure gurgle stacks run through like fingers over a wet, loud flip book, of hot blurby blur liquids. Small steam eventually finds its way out the eyelid cracks of the lid, rising up as dancing induced with snake charm. The sounds blob away as she wonders about the tape she bought earlier in the city from Louis’ shop collection.

The sun goes down, like that. The clouds go colorless. The room is cast dark, but a lamp was left on at the end table next to the couch. She turns the kitchen light on to better see. The sound of tape rewinding still noises like a ribbon of scotch cracked like belt.

Minute warm finds her, and the chills of something follow after the heat. Snuggled in the knit of sweater, Sarah is pouring the coffee into a used wine glass. She thinks it to be a unique idea, where the slight hint of sweet velvet hue cherry drink could spike the black a little alive. A crow watches her in the glee, from afar, not able to say anything to her. She looks out and finds the bird staring with its black eyes… They don’t break eye contact.

Sarah walks up to her glass pane from inside, its warmth of kitchen light contrasting with the cold dark outside, where the watching avian on the other side of the window holds the look. The eyes look wide shut in their reflection… She has a while, so she contemplates the watcher, looking back into them. The view is a while short.

The winding of the VHS tape backwards slows down before it finishes; the whirl thunks and clicks as it’s pushed out the mouth of the VCR. The television throws out a dead machination signal of grey and white against the sunny furnace of light from the modest living room lamp. The bird speaks for once, cawing like an abracadabra before throwing the stretch of black wingspan down, sending it up, as it flutters the wings into a carry away. Sarah ponders the event with a taste of the drink of minuscule mix, knowing the video is ready, without any sound from her except for the smallest quiet sip and a heater humming its old overplayed song.

The short while ends with the question she speaks: “Where did you go?” Sarah thinks of the vanished vigilance.

Walking in a slow and steady way, she finds the living room dullishly cozy in hue like heat. She kneels down with a jean stretch, reaching with an elbow bend at the rectangled black box, pushing it in by the soft nudge of knuckles, before tucking the strands of hair hanging in her right eye behind her ear. Sarah has a moment of self-awareness, speaking to herself again: “I am too curious about this. It can’t be me who found it.” The tape is pulled into the larger grey box’s gulp, digesting with a reel and a grind before it is seated inside with a clunk. The sound of it rolling up to a play makes a huzzing acceleration, until the static blurs its snow over the video and its scanlines, projecting the color in smears over her appearance.

The vision of the box tells an image of a woman, her form flickering in between rolling lines as she clicks on the camera. She speaks from a bed she sits on, surrounded by paintings on the wall: “My name is Alice, and—” More lines fall now, as the reel looks flipped over like a pilot losing control mid-flight. The picture starts falling again and again, rolling from the top down through the bottom, before a new sight takes its place, plummeting the same. Sarah watches closely like a curious monkey girl, hunchbacked and forward-leaning on the carpet, near the screen.

The film begins to stabilize now. The feeling of a double speak, becoming a cipher; they are connected at the port of neck like a sliding click beneath skin. Sarah can only think to call it paranoia coming on, with hardly enough time to describe the emotions of this or what’s happening. The flickers rev higher before a stable capture of the flick.

A woman is staring down the barrel of lens after clicking on the camera, as she pulls back and sits on her bed. She says her name, “My name is Alice,” facing the camera with a rattled expression hidden in her eyes. Sarah leans a little closer into the screen. On the video, Alice looks at the painting and says, “I am not running away—” then she turns back to the camera, “—from my art career.” Sarah feels a little confused already, squinting her eyes while listening. Alice gulps while glancing down, her focal point swinging with the force of a deadly hammer, before saying, “I always see it, the feeling of what I must draw. I have seen it before it happens.” She nervously wipes her brow in the low-quality footage and chuckles a little.

Sarah considers a detail out loud with thin speak: “She talked to my painting I bought earlier…” The idea she has is that of a puzzle. “That is it. That’s what clicked,” Sarah says again in the blending of the colors from the display and herself. She rolls her thumb around the lips while in a hunch position like her reasoning. She draws in to focus more.

Alice continues speaking after the pause, covering her mouth now with her hand as she says with a nodding yes, talking a little muffled, “That is how I like to describe my art.” Her hand rolls to the left of her face in a swathing hug of self-soothe in abandonment, before it glides with a revolution falling over her face, pulling the flesh down with it. Alice leaves out more words to the viewer: “You can do what you want to someone’s life, but you cannot take away their will to themselves. I won’t let what happened to Christopher or my past stop me from being who I am. That is what it means to be yourself, even if what I do, painting, were to kill me in the process.” Alice gazes off in a catatonia, swinging back and forth on her bed alone, trying to calm like a baby in the arms of a parent. She just glares and gawks with the disturbed look of some kind of syndrome.

Sarah notices, and she says something again: “Secretly. She talks like there is a gun to her head.” The feeling of sympathy, of wanting to hold her.

While frozen up inside on a stare to nowhere, the words are said: “I am no longer willing to give it control.” Alice looks up at the camera: “I will not stop my work. Nothing will bury my light; I will let it shine.”

The light cuts off in a flash, and the whole house is cast in dark. Sarah sinks at the gut. The memory finds her, something about a man she cannot capture with any normal name she can imagine. The voice opens up at the ear to her right: “What if I was the secret, would you not want to know?” The sound is low, the tone of a counseling man. Sarah feels locked like a portrait, and the sweat is colder now. The voice speaks again, and the dark room becomes blacker in the direction of it as it utters, “You won’t understand, but you will lock the window, now.”

Sarah, at first, is drained at the lungs with a shank of fear, and the air doesn’t find her. Something does find her, though: the feeling of Alice, and it is lived out inside her, like vengeance. She responds with a commanding prejudice, “No!” The space becomes frozen, and the words trip out after leaving, poured into the vast. She wonders how she is to hold her breath for much longer. Almost unable to move in a flinching at the drying throat, she forces out the trifled mouse sound, saying it small: “Help me.”

The word lands somewhere, but it is an idea in the mind rather than a sentiment. The window slides up and open, and its noise touches the conception of her with itself, a sensation known in a first time that echoes through the house and into the future; it is known here first. The shadow dawns over the other, being in a different origin, but carrying its same nothingness. The suspicion recognizes how it stands like winter and makes the shadow at the right seen through a flash of thought, where the long chin recoils away in ruin. Sarah gasps for oxygen, able to breathe once again, like she doesn’t have to obey the gun.

The thud of weight beneath a single foot: the opening to the night has let it in. The multitudes of hairs stand, and something is unlocked from the solitude inside. The fascination forces her body into praise of him, knowing it is him… It is him.

The weight of body in her own palms, known, as she looks at his revelation into being. The thirst is quenched, and the call she made from another life is heard. The wilt of him scares away the plot with cunning and wisdom. The man… His long skirt hangs like heavy theatre curtains. The dreamy eyes felt but unseen behind stitched eyelids, and the supple delicate of mouth sewn shut with it. There are feathered wings where pulled butchering yanked them like abuse of a swan’s dignity. The wrists and ankles look forever scarred from an impossibly tight bondage. In all this age of his young corpse moving, his blue and grey hangs short but long with a curly hair that lulls and curls, his swirl of personality like the insanity of his past which trickles like drips in the air.

Sarah is obliviated by the reunion, where the presence of his soul makes her womb leap in a stirring infatuation she cannot hide in her tremble and warm breath. She cannot move from the weight, where she is captured into a still by the mere observance of him in her cuticle lens. She waits for him, unable to stop what is about to happen, and a stuttering flinch in her throat stands by like a loyal dog.

In the blinking eye of hers, he is gone. The cognizance talks like a godhead of his, where the never-said saying that borrows the poison heat of the dark throws its hand sigils, their scribbling like minced hand talks. The string at her heart to his knows they are tied no matter where, and he pulls her leash towards the window. He wants her to follow him tonight, and she does. The being that is him wears Sarah like a glove, making her draw without write onto the between he into. The trickle again… The dripping of them is spiritual as much as it is physical, where the condensation of the open glass rolls with a dribble of water like both of their restless feelings below.

Where the moon shines full of itself, Sarah looks out for any sign of him. Sarah is shown the green saturate of leaf outside the home, which laps up a drop of dew once more. It is so heavy and full in cusp from the fluid that it can only gape at the sides, opening wide from a convex that cannot take much more drink of the clouds’ leftovers. The libation of her and its cup spill together. She overflows herself over and from him, gripping the window he came through, hoping his insight pierces more again rather than teasing.

Flashing imagery rises up in her like a new death, and its convulsing vista is in the red of him that drinks her mess, between her legs from a chalice. Sarah wants nothing more than to nourish him, to please him.

Before Sarah can give any more, the guilt of something from behind wants to teach about a time she was murdered by him in this same house, and it is not long ago that her soul became snared for what felt like forever. The mind is split almost, and the divide takes place, yet the itch is to run into his arms still yet. The need of him and his of her rise into a scarecrow in her shadow, standing longer towards the black of the demon from long ago, throughout every wall of the home blanketed by the evil of bleak. The both of them scape him into a fall of unrealizing, scourging the form of him into the sin he was and always will be, where he is birthed into a false sense of past over their former lives, the depth of which now repeats in him alone, no longer playing within their history.

Where the sanctuary is cleansed and purified, Sarah jumps out the window frame from his yank and is caught by the mind of the two of them. They crawl up and into each other like senses further than blood-pumped machinery, pulled together by threads which join them as much as they undo his stitches at the eyelids and mouth. Where he can see and talk, she can now realize a light of herself which speaks creation and sees a glimpse of truth beyond. In this one moment, the man speaks: “I was named Curse by God, but I am many names in other lives and yours. I have become three of four corners, and you my cornerstone, who holds me as much as I surround you. You are neither two; I am both, and am also one of which opposite of yourself. You are the woman I adore in every life, no matter if I see it or not. I will follow you to every corner of the world, because I am built on you and from you.”

The soul screams a rupturing slide of its new self, out from itself, but of the two, and this being is nameless until its true home, where the power of which is known not by the whore named dark.
