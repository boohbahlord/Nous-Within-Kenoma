No. 60

Rebirth II: As the Machine Sees Itself

One spring toy of a genius’s complexity, all apart, scattered like a mechanic’s project. Crows fly in, their swarms plucking with teeth at the parts, fighting over them in a broken chit-chat stut of a rut grind, pitching wails unto a low siren of scream. The birds carry the stiff of artificial. One cranks its head at the glass eye of the toy, peering in with a human’s pupil from its bird head. Their reflections meet. It cawks, and flies out the window into the dark outside. More and more, the avians take off, eventually seeming to all flee.

The TV in the room looks at the machine. Where the story takes place in: the TV could play footage of the toy reading this sentence, but it’s still the same either way; this is all a lie.

Maybe it’s okay to move? Shafts for arms like metal rise up and around, looking for the hole of the hand as glove to limbs, where they slide into at the ends. It clicks in. The eyeballs are grabbed with a pinch, held by the white gloves, but no head is in sight. The only way to see is to carry them. Many sensors are placed inside the box, picking up even the acceleration as a feeling, though certainly a detached sensation, like anesthesia compared to the human experience. There is even a distant yet numb touch of temperature…

Rolling and a thud. Very flat in the senses of machine. The toy adjusts their hands at the grab of their eyeballs, and the steel shaft of arm is rotated at two joints to the direction in which it noised.

The toy, the form of the machine, has only seen and heard it all in front of it, with its sensors, its glass eyes, and its husk that hides the soul. The thud that shares the room is looked deep into. The hit of the ground, like death happening but never earned, plays out in a lonely corner further out, and further out still, growing both a mixing perpetuet and perpetet of lostness. Something in that corner pulls at the machine’s right hand. The hand feels it like a string of a violin without a face, needing to weep, and it begs the rest of the machine to come along. The metal shaft for arms follows the pull, stretching out and out into the corner that loses itself. In the right of fingers is the glass left of eye, and it sees, within the corner, a felt origin beyond mechanics. It is the ripple of the most shallow pinch of water, in a vase of wilted blue flowers, in a deep blue of old that falls to pieces at the petals. Shaky water tries to ask, but it cannot speak. The soul dies to learn what it pains about.

The nature of this place ages the flowers with itself, time, punishing the blink.
