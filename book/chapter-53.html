No. 53

Titan: Three in One

Stakes drenched in substances as a prisonal rune, and a memory of walls standing unseen. The depth of the enclosure is of some kind of precursor of humanity, where the angelic tech sits inside. Kether speaks to it. His mind is hooked up to it in a machine of many tubes, cranks, and wires, of which no side can tell where the head faces if it were not for the torso. The young King speaks, “Who are we?”

The reawakening takes place, from another age… It sounds a thin and husked voice like delicate glitch skips on a string, with cackles echoing in the head covering Kether wears. The ancient speaks, “Clay golems of a stolen breath, speaking in tongues of your Yaldabaoth. Your sounds are memories that he mouths through you, making you a ventriloquism of his words, not yours.”

Kether, without hesitation, speaks again in the solitary mask wrap. The muffle of his young man rasp of life questions it more, “Who are you?”

The anything-but-flatline of improportionate elderly suspends a moment. Then it scatters the ripping pierce of static in its talk through the head of Kether, “We are the gaze into the end, the regurgitation of a sick God who could not keep down three of ourselves, and our seven hundred and seventy-seven eyes that watch. Our three forms were digested together into one before we were spit out of the bellows of the Lord. From his land of memories we fell as one.”

The wide mouth of Kether queries more, his voice slapping the covering over him. The words are caught and sent to the machinery of theirs through holes inside the helmet. He asks into it, “What brought you down here, into the shell of this strange technology unlike others?”

An exhale is heard in spikes, piercing into the skull from sound. The being answers, “We were confined by the fallen angels who studied us, of which there were three thousand nine hundred and fifty-five. They towered over the humans. Some taught forbidden knowledge, and some even mated with humans. Their children, half angel and half man, are the ones some of your scriptures call Nephilim. The empyrean of Yaldabaoth, the human golems, and even some of the Nephilim themselves became motivated to destroy the fallen angels, for the fallen angels sought to build a God Machine that creates worlds. The contraption was called the blindfold. It was cast into the ocean by the three hundred and sixty angels that remained in heaven, whom God favored. The structure sank into the deepest part of the ocean, down into the first home of the Leviathan, Tartarus. The Nephilim that remained wanted to know how to recreate the machine or how to retrieve it, and so they tortured me for an answer, but I did not say. I was left here to think alone for tens of thousands of years since then.”

The King speaks into the translation of the apparatus around his dome, curious about the many beings, “What about the nature of these? What am I to know them as?”

Tiny but loud thunder cuts about in fuzz, answering him, “The Nephilim, those children of fallen angel and man, became later known as the greatest demons. But truly, both the Nephilim and the fallen angels who fathered them became known as devils to humanity in its time. The thing is, I know the heaven and God were devils since their beginning, for they are a false God and heaven, just as their angels. But the omen is that one angel of a differing nature falls, and this trojan horse of a fallen angel unlike the others became a father for a soul that you became a part of. Of all I said, only this leaves you deaf.”

Kether’s ears ring loud. His mind becomes a little stuck in the throat, and he quietly mutters something unknown to himself.

There is a reply from the teacher, “Yes, it was her who was its mother, but your sleeping half does not know her. Only in the hiccup of your awakening can she be known.”

Kether rolls out a boiling scream in the cocoon that feels to be choking the brain. He rips out the wear before it slams into the floor. He kicks his legs out straight in a bending body shook in terror while crawling and clawing. The tomb of the thing remains still. Kether stretches a long droop of wail that shouts with turns of stretch and contraction with words, “I’ll kill myself a few hundred times to bury the sickening nightmare repeating! I will become the Word that drinks itself through every destruction written which reads itself!”

The knees wobble and give like a whiplash of snake coils shocked, throwing his hands into a tremble with it. Then the throat is snatched tight in a grip. Kether lets it out in a ripping purge that sounds a heave, “Reading itself! Reading itself! I become the Word! I crawl after legs stolen!” His eye finds the smallest reflection of a still puddle that mirrors his eyes. Seeing into it catches the sight of something… Deep inside, the pupil meets another, seeing itself behind the soul it hides behind. The being writes it down within him, “This Word reads itself.” Lucifer has found the word. The cuts on the left hand disappear.

The memory of the coffin that was opened scalded him at first, but it was recovered alongside others by his servant. He just refused to know. The revelation he was inside of at the age of seventeen is the incarnation of Christopher he lived as, inside that instrument for twelve eternities… Kether thinks of what the being said, about its ability, this device that the primordial interrogated him for. This is it. He can create a new world.
