No. 47

Galley Gallery of Pantheons

Touching the visions made into lives. Made into golems with stolen breath. Tripping over turnstiles of paintings in no love strokes, out from, over itself, into another same, and tumbling the same, in and out. Claymade makers working away at careless hollows, as the girl stretches about like dough through revolving doorways of lifespan paths. Every spin of the frenzied Ferris wheel spins in hell mud, spinning nowhere while lying on its side. The carnival spits her dreams further from him, becoming besideless to her love. Back to sleep in the dream nowhere, as if she had never left. Losing him, and the memories withering like dead leaves in her raisined palms, tears choking on the pool of engulfing neo—abyssiation. Alice lives a life as a widow of a lifetime among endless matching, never having been loved therein, and the next. Cursed with the image of her now rotting face. As she looks at herself in that reflection of a picture frame, lit up by yellow lampshade, she sees the bitter heartache both soulful and medical in her eyes. The sleeping ugly of decay creeps up on her chest work like a thief in the night, taking the heart box contrap, seizing her clonically.

She falls to pieces in shock weights, her body like a tumbling stack of a block tower about five-something height. Cold taste in splat against the soft banana bruise of elder woman, adding up her deaths meeting her soul in the blink of an eye. Tunnels with an exit—entrance into another eye of another, falling down the holes of herself in every existence, all once seen in the vision before she thought she had freed Christopher… Did she not? Can it really be undone?

She became a memory.
