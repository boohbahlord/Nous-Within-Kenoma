No. 23

Chalice of Tears

The dark crescent shadows over the terrain; the cast is the source of tall rolling mountains. The two suns rise, one Red and the other Blue. The sky is mixing into a purple cast with frays of velvet, indigo, scarlet, and sapphiric neptune haze. At the other pole, an ocean shore crystallizes with shimmers of refraction, sparkling. The tides foam white, up and onto the sand they run through.

Between the poles, there is a girl and a man. Both stand in the dark of dusk, where the light bleeds over the skies. The girl is a crimson rose of dark and bloody red. The man is a trumpet gentian flower of deep and rich blue.

Where the suns set together, they walk towards one another. The man named Blue stretches his arms up in a bend forward while moving, and a mirage of rain follows with him, the arcane of his hue hazed into it with a heel-raised glide. He blurs like dissolve amid the sight, but the fog and light carry his essence just as well.

The aspect of the air levitates with a drifting impression of form, implying itself in any part through streaks of glow that reveal his outline within.

The girl is named Red, and she follows after the whirling dewdrops spewing, chasing with a golden chalice, trying to catch his water. She drinks up as often as she can, so as to make more room in the cusp before the brim overflows. She cries his Blue back out and catches them with the cup, finding herself pursuing every last drop of him. Suddenly, during the enchanting occasion, the air feels sucked away, as if it was pulled out…

Red is taken aback for one moment, still in the warmth of night falling over the firmament, and then the wind fills itself into every in-between. The girl blinks for one instant, and then the man is carried away, pulled off into the distance from a puff of breeze.

She is left alone again. She savors every sip that is left of him. The suns fall down, and she falls the same, crying in the dirt. She catches the tears of hers that remind her of him, cherishing them as well. The clouds are nowhere to be seen above, every star unhindered, the absence of which reminds Red of Blue.

The lushful breeze of the warm starry night is now empty. After drinking from all her tears and chalice, all that is left of him is the smell of his rain that he spilled and mixed into the soil. She is jealous of the ground she lies on.

Red asks of everything, “Why?”
