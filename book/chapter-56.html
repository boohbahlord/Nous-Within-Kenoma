No. 56

The Night Never Ends

Some call upon angels, sharing words with them in the angelic language, and some manipulate the angels. Some call upon what humans name devils, sharing words with them in mockery of God and creation, and some manipulate the demonic.

Kether’s left hand began the path to oblivion, which is the many faces of the raging head. He takes on the task of creating something from nothing.

It is written with blood in the lone dummy of his choked heart:

Ab innominatis, ex capite furenti, et deorum damnatio.

Meum numen in absentia, hoc verbum divinum.

The threads and dice only watch it unfold itself like a wilting bloom of petal highs that contact the something which waits in a lonely “Don’t.”

There is a bitter alongside the restless heart syndrome that wakes up like thirteen eternities on top of the chest. The Kingdom, at the pain of the weight, tries to dig down deep into the dirt, hoping to find its crown and sleep, but only finds its jolt of grave. Kether would rather pull soil over himself to become buried with pride than admit he is dead wrong. The hate lays him down as cost for his blindness. The death that never began rises up like a depth to nowhere inside of him. Kether reverses time like regurgitate, and the four corners play out empty inside his placeless material from the terminal disease of not.

They are in a home without rest. The chemicals are the black nous that hung their souls down deeper to nowhere, a penetrating insanity that talks to itself forever, the bitter walk irritating a mind with nowhere to sit. They made the dirt into a passage towards the lowest emanation that becomes you, where the in becomes an out of itself. The placeless forgives nothing, not even itself, and it even breaks mirrors at the sight of them. The reflection burns the mind of soul within the timeless head, caught in itself, screaming to be let go towards a God made of itself.

Their smothering walls of order within God’s mind itself are the curtain, which hides the synchronicity machinery from the little sanity left. Wanting to leave, they return to climbing high on a ladder whose structure is small as thread and large as everything, both containing one another in a sick joke that climbs down in either direction.

Kether is warned of the fake mercy of a rest in the prison, and so he wanders up and down the rot of home with paranoia, looking through the blinds with pinprick pupil swipes, out into the abyss of a night never ending. With fear, he waits for a never-coming doom, which has revealed its presence through the irony of the restlessness: it arrives through his fear of a rest in the land of delusion, never seen or found. The punchline lands, and it is the nothing awaited, which lives in the never-ending linger of anticipation.

Weeping and gnashing of teeth is the certainty of a self-made prisonscape setting in, which warns of a nothing that cannot be: “It’s not safe here.” Orange street lights in the dark stare back into the eyes of a disturbed Cotard syndrome who became the end.

You cannot hold onto nothing, nor can you mend the space between it and something. There is nothing heard, seen, nor felt, only the empty who floats like a world whose substance of hearing, seeing, and feeling is that which it’s not. Have you ever listened to the ring of it? It’s just a phone at the end table of a brown couch away. Agitation, sharp below the ear, stabs with pressure like a machine at the phone’s yelling.

Tired of it, he swims upstream through a belt looped at the neck that spins around as the mind’s swirling lostness, calling out through a crushed hourglass throat that bursts with laughter out from rupturing veins. He is caught in the bend of catch that holds onto the pretend of release, taking itself nowhere.

The clocks would reverse just to trap him further, for the woven time will abort any chance of peace. There are silver-like letters that split the bones that resist the blunt, like a cut down the run of grain in a log, where the nerves are peeled like a fruit in the system. He never found the present or the future in the walk. Kether is thrown hard at the wall of the never-born, reversing deeper into a past never beginning. There are only blinking memories of a never-known in the hereless, and all the while, screams in between laughter that hurts. He says alone, “It can’t be.”

He is the everlasting end, strung out as a prayer to himself never heard. These words are testament to the cycle. In this end, there is no light, and the play of nobody plays itself, like a dummy without a ventriloquist. The lonely nobody, their fake lacquer face coming down with a dull frown in their abandoned house, everything turned into an imitation, only has to say, “Come back…”
