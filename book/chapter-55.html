No. 55

Blue to Gray

The replay of it, a memory, and it’s nestled in the paint of the same blue before the weather changed; the love hurts her. It stares back from the strokes of the canvas Alice makes in her lonely.

The humid air clings as I do to him. We watch the blue skies with bushes of fluffy white clouds in between its lawn of heaven. In the stride we share, I can’t help but feel like myself, so I tighten and wrap further around him, before tripping at the toe of my rain boots on the plank of wood. We both tumble down over the train tracks, lying on them with one another now. He smiles at me with pinched eyebrows that question me with a lighthearted look. I can’t help but throw my arms around his big neck. He says a word in play to me, giving me a nickname with a smile, “Clumsy.”

I roll the gap between both my legs around his thigh close to me, and I laugh at his attempt to amuse me, finding it adorable from such a structured and beautiful man. Our noses touch in a moment, before I find his light in his pupils that reflect myself. I become distant, swept up from a warmth and into a space cerebraled rather than purely feeling, and the sharpness of him sparks through like mine. There is something greater than just the surface of him, and it shadows his appearance, wit, and touch, all at once. It’s kinda like me… That true presence completed inside me for a moment. Losing the world in there, and not the appearance of their eyes, no.

It starts to rain again within that hot humid spring, and the arms give up. I fall in, and not as a human, but as a lost soul remembering a feeling alone. I cry into his collarbone and shoulders. The world almost reacts, not by way of the God, but rather from our own something? Whatever. I am not able to say what it is, and knowing it is kinda unsettling, like roots being pulled up from something inside me to understand. The showers from above pick up a little more, as warm as him.

He inhales before saying with a power like time, “Strange, isn’t it, how we can feel that something?”

Something cracks and splits in me, throwing my head into a break before I tell him in a dead voice, “I don’t want this feeling. Something is wrong. I don’t know if I am ruining anything.”

The downpour keeps picking up, its million trickles into the ground that fill the air with its sound, and there is just too much space in the silence of it. Am I tripping over myself? Is this all in my head? It’s a lot for me, whatever it is. The rain makes the clothes heavy with water.

He kinda gravels before clearing his voice with a simple talk, “I think the rain is pooling under my ass a little now.” I can’t help but laugh a little, almost forgetting the thoughts. I almost wanna wave him away from wanting him so badly.

I tell him, “No, I actually have been thinking about something, but I don’t know what.”

He snaps back calmly, “Well, what do you mean, Alice?”

Maybe he doesn’t understand. Fine, so I say more, “It is a thought I cannot describe. I don’t even know what it is.”

Then he comes back again with two other questions, “Did you forget? What does it make you feel?”

I don’t know if I would describe it as anything like that, nor are the feelings simple enough to name easily. Maybe I have gone a little crazy. I don’t really think I am ready for this conversation either. I decide to change the subject, and I communicate that, “Yeah, I don’t really know what, nor do I know how to say it. I wish we could go further out, more than just a stroll down the railroad track. I wanna get away from everything.”

That is when he talks with a smooth temptation that is so like him to do, “I got more. I was waiting to do it later with you, but the rain might ruin it if I open the bag.”

So now I am obviously pulled into him with intrigue, so I half joke in what I ask, “What did you get your hands on this time, you devil?”

The sky keeps going, just creating mud with its tears all over, and not leaving. It doesn’t really care if it drowns us by this point. I get a little caught in my head about the weather, and he is already answering, “More than one, actually three of them. PCP, more painkillers, whatever, and your favorite, Ketamine. You like that, Alice?”

I am a little overjoyed at the treasure trove, and it sends lights through my head like a festival. I only laugh a little guiltily at the excess before one more shameless and grinning question, “Okay… but how much?”

I start to cry alone over the painting when I remember, and he isn’t here to comfort me this time. The fucking sky turned grey and ran something in us away, into those horrible things we smiled about. Was it us?

I wipe my face with the back of my hand and smear the acrylic a little. I don’t want to forget him. I look at the strokes again, the blue heaven, and somewhere in it I can still see that flicker in our stare, before the storm came over.
