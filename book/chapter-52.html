No. 52

Sepulcher

Sobbing at every crow, getting sick at every sewing needle, hurting with every branch tap on the window, and feeling lost at every full moon illumination. The VHS tape never rewound, just left inside, making noises. The broken coffee table, split in half, is covered in scrambles of paper piles: letters towards him, to cope. The wine bottles clutter the kitchen table. The strange but magnetizing man, the fallen angel, dead. Buried under the tree.

This autumn, she comes back to the tree for the first time since the death of Curse; she does not know why it happens the way it does.

The breeze blows an allergen and memories, like the whistle through the teeth of skulls. Sweet voice… His sound low but delicate with firm, as if it held her by the string of heart. The silence wails high, up and up, like a rollercoaster. The calm has to give… There is a slight hatred that weighs her down everywhere, at the world with no pardon to its injustice, but it’s real.

Sarah goes past a gulp, before a light cold that dries her cracked lips busts her mouth into bleeding, and she says in solemn devotion to somewhere, anywhere: “Don’t die, or at least come back…” The words fall heavy onto the ground, cracking like a vase, spilling with tears over the burial of his body. The vase of spoken lies shattered, like the dry leaves of the fall season, colored by their state as corpses, like him. The tears flow with a tight huddle against the heat, trying to hold onto the mountain of face they slide down from, before dripping into the dirt. Sarah says one more thing in an empty kind of paint over the sepulcher that is the tree: “Don’t leave me behind.” She cracks with the vase of words, her temperament losing its moisture, leaving her life distilled of itself, though with no room of death to take her away.
