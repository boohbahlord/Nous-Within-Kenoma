Chapter 52

Word

The crow flutters back onto the tree, slowing its descendence with grace. Its sight is down at the snake, cawing at it, words that thunder a lightning strike again, ripping the paper of sky with their color. The snake hisses back from the muddy face of Sarah, before shooting head first down her throat with a pushing force that stretches her neck wide into a pregnancy. Curse now comes out from the form of the crow he flew down to her as, where he loses its wings with it. Landed next to her, he picks her up with one arm, owed to his size and strength, before carrying her back in through the open window.

The electric is back on, and she is laid on the couch next to the yellow-lit lamp, where the TV still speaks from its image of static, fuzzing about wildly in predictability. The couch is a muted dull of grape that sinks a little from her weight. Curse looks down at her, and thinks to himself inside his mind through the voice of a soul, “Sleeping beauty… One day you will hear my voice again, maybe.” He holds her light hands that hang dead heavy in cold. The heart of Curse is steady yet sharp-feeling in precise worry, and so he puts his large index and middle finger at the artery of neck, but nothing… Pulseless as she is, he believes her to be in there.

Curse, now startled and afraid, does something bizarre: he takes out a dagger from the false heaven and shoots it into his torso, dragging the blade with force. The opening of layers slides as an improv of surgery, pulverizing and rupturing in a blunt cut across, coughing up blood at the body. The blood tries to run from the slicing and streams. Curse kneels down, yet his head is still far up from a stature unhuman, so he leans in closer to a bend of neck and face long down, over her mouth. The angel takes his stitched lips over hers, where his pale corpse in a blue asphyxiation and cemetery grey touches her flushing life. The touch is enough to undo the stitches a second time on contact, where her pulse returns on him there, both now breathing into each other. His curly ends of hair cover her face, and she leans up and into him, before a kicking form legless springs with the force of a closed spring jumping, and it sends her into a scream. Curse sucks at her mouth hard, before Ouroboros is swallowed by him. As the snake crawls up her throat in a launch, the gullet stretches wide again, swollen with squirms under skin.

When the power of slither is usurped out of her, Curse stands up with a gulp, as Ouroboros crawls out the open wound he created. Sarah rises up, her eyes wide in both terror and trust in Curse’s power. The serpent looks up from the belly of the beast, and is clasped with the angel grip that almost crushes the eyes out of the head, shooting the pest into a rambunctious throw around. Curse yanks the cord out of himself, like an umbilical of parasitic hate which births the deformity of a truer curse. Sarah jumps back on the couch, watching his might in terror, worried at its effect on herself. The fallen angel barks now with his mouth open at the thing, “Serpent!”

Ouroboros hisses through the clench of knuckles, wiggling about without escape. Curse says more, though now in a deep tone of judgement, “I was and still am the Antony, the angel from the fullness, and many other forms, and I know your essence to be in more than just flesh, for you fleet yourself even. One thing is true, though: your soul knows as I do, your time is fleeting with yourself. Seventeen! Thirteen!—” The Ouroboros wrangles with its on-and-off pulse again, feeling the song play again. Sarah shows a confused look. Curse continues counting back in determinating grit, “Eleven!—”

The devil pest knows nowhere to go but in, and so, true to its nature, the head opens backwards, like a flower blooming, before the petals meet the stipe of the snake that turns inside out. The inside of the snake is revealed as parchment that reads, “Reading itself.”

Curse continues counting backwards even louder, “Nine! Seven!—” The demon wraps its outside above the knuckle with its insides, but it cannot get lower than the clasp of pipe-lodged fingers. The recoiling into is stopped with force. “Five! Three!—” The manuscript becomes bouqueted, where a geyser of blood shoots out, painting everything around it with the spelling, “Word.” Curse says the first and last number firmly, “One!” The cuticles wilt. Sarah is at the far end of the kitchen, watching from afar in an expression gone frozen and worried.

Curse says out loud, “I am the bark that held your lifelessness; death.”

Sarah calls to him in a piercing high-pitch screech, “Curse, your stomach!”

Curse looks up with his sewn eyes, his mouth being the only thing alive, before saying, “I love you, Alice.” He collapses, with all his might spent with him. His body breaks the coffee table. Sarah yelps into a bawl before screaming frantically at the loss, and she says in a dying wail, loudly, “You will leave me behind!”

The box television still illuminates a grey from the static, where the sound of fuzz rips a little now at the death, almost sobbing with her. The tree returned to death.
